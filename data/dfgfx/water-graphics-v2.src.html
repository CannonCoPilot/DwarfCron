Open problems and loose ends

Known issues outside the item you are looking at, so none of them gets lost.

Lake classed as river blocks W3

In Shortsling the plugin files most of the lake as river (663 cells with a current near 213, 143), so river streaks run across the lake. The water-body classing has to be fixed before the lake surface.

DF's shallow-water sprite part of W5

DF draws depths 1-3 as bright aqua at 70/80/90% opacity and 4-7 opaque, so drying puddles read as solid squares. Swapping the tile's water value does not change the sprite DF picks; whether it can be suppressed per tile is still to test.

Depth tint on bed art your call

Driftwood, boulders and items fade a lot through one or two levels of water. Say whether bed art should take a lighter tint than the bed itself (B8).

Slow warm-up on the big sea map DF's own

Learnedchanneled (768 × 768) runs at about 2 FPS for its first minute after unpausing with the plugin and tool both off. Not the plugin's cost; you closed B2.

Release package after the look pass

The plugin's dist/ package predates the depth, falls and wet-sprite work. It gets rebuilt once the checklist settles. The dll is tied to DFHack 53.16-r2.

Wildlife tool rc not installed C1-C3

seasonal-wildlife v7.1-rc carries the sponge work and the cave-entry fix. It is not in your game and not pushed; it goes in when you say.

How the plugin draws a water cell

DF paints only the top level and one level of water below it, and nothing at all below water at the view level. The plugin plans each water column on the core thread and repaints it bottom-up on the render thread, inside DF's own map pass and its lone tile redraws.

  1. Bed. Floor or ramp in the column's bed family, with driftwood and terrain boulders in the bed's second layer.
  2. Things on the bed and in the water. Corpses, items and body parts with their own sprites.
  3. Water levels. One tint per level above the bed; opacity after n levels is 1 − (1 − a)n, charted on the right. Then the depth darkening for each level the bed lies below the view.
  4. Swimmers. Units and vermin on the level they swim on, multiplied toward the water's colour so only their own pixels darken.
  5. DF's top level. DF's own pass, with z-fog off over planned cells. At the view level, sprites DF paints over its water sheet are swapped for wet copies.
  6. Surface and falls. Shimmer on standing water, current streaks on rivers, lip streaks and froth at the falls, only where the camera looks down onto water.
  7. Waves. Crests on DF's wave fronts, then the landing ~ on shore tiles. DF draws its spray and mist itself.

Measured 3 Oct at a 120×68 view: 47.8 frames a second with the plugin against 42 for vanilla, since the plugin replaces DF's per-cell water redraw. 5 Oct, with wet sprites at the view level: about 44 at your zoom.

DF's ocean wave, end to end

The game simulates waves in both modes but only ASCII draws them. Matching ASCII glyphs to DF's data, then marking each kind with a magenta or yellow test tile in graphics mode, settled what each stage is.

Stage 1 · open sea ~ over ≈

A wave front leaves one of the fort's wave makers and heads for a point on the coast. Its visibility counter starts at 120 and runs down at random. ASCII draws it as a purple ~.

Plugin: faint crest, rising slightly as the counter falls.

Stage 2 · the run in ~ in the shallows

The counter reaches 0 about 10 tiles out, but the front keeps moving at 0 over water 2 and then 1 deep. ASCII draws it white. Earlier builds skipped every front at 0, which is why this stage was missing.

Plugin: the crest at full strength.

Stage 3 · the shore ░▒ on land

The front stops a tile or two short of the coast and is removed. DF puts out spray (Steam flows) on the beach, which ASCII shades ░▒. For 236 of 239 fronts sampled, the destination was a shore tile.

Plugin: ~ on the shore tiles ahead, then DF's mist.

Crops of the user's screenshots from the live fort, 5 Oct. One correction from the record: on 5 Oct the white stage was first tied to DF's OceanWave flows. Those turned out to be a standing population of 250–360 beach tiles that never changes when a wave lands (log E32).

What changed since

  • The 1 Oct page ranked a C++ plugin last. A raws mod could not show anything below DF's first water level, and a Lua overlay cost 16–24 FPS. The plugin became the chosen path on 3 Oct.
  • "Waves actually spawn in graphics mode" was open then. They do: about 200 fronts at a time from 3 wave makers in the user's fort.
  • The translucent-water raws mod and the Lua wave overlay are retired. The Lua overlay stands down whenever the plugin is enabled.
