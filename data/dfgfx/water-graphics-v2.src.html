See-through water, live waves and sea life in graphics mode

A C++ DFHack plugin now draws what graphics mode leaves out: the sea bed and the levels of water above it, fish swimming below the surface, river currents, and the whole of DF's ocean wave from open sea to shore. Every phase is in place; what remains is tuning how it looks.

Updated 5 Oct 2026. Plugin sw-wt/aquarium-plugin at f051398, running in the user's fort (Kideturdim). Experiment log: DwarfCron/data/aquarium-run/FINDINGS/2026-10-03-plugin-log.md, entries E1–E33. The 1 Oct research this page started from is kept, condensed, near the end.

DF's ocean wave, end to end

The game simulates waves in both modes but only ASCII draws them. Matching ASCII glyphs to DF's data, then marking each kind with a magenta or yellow test tile in graphics mode, settled what each stage is.

Stage 1 · open sea ~ over ≈

A wave front leaves one of the fort's wave makers and heads for a point on the coast. Its visibility counter starts at 120 and runs down at random. ASCII draws it as a purple ~.

Plugin: faint crest, rising slightly as the counter falls.

Stage 2 · the run in ~ in the shallows

The counter reaches 0 about 10 tiles out, but the front keeps moving at 0 over water 2 and then 1 deep. ASCII draws it white. Earlier builds skipped every front at 0, which is why this stage was missing.

Plugin: the crest at full strength.

Stage 3 · the shore ░▒ on land

The front stops a tile or two short of the coast and is removed. DF puts out spray (Steam flows) on the beach, which ASCII shades ░▒. For 236 of 239 fronts sampled, the destination was a shore tile.

Plugin: ~ on the shore tiles ahead, then DF's mist.

Crops of the user's screenshots from the live fort, 5 Oct. One correction from the record: on 5 Oct the white stage was first tied to DF's OceanWave flows. Those turned out to be a standing population of 250–360 beach tiles that never changes when a wave lands (log E32).

How the plugin draws a water cell

DF paints only the top level and one level of water below it. The plugin repaints each water cell bottom-up, in this order, before and after DF's own map pass.

  1. Bed. The floor or ramp sprite for the column's bed family: sand for the sea and lakes, mud for rivers, true stone or soil in caverns.
  2. Water levels. One tint per level of water above the bed, in the family's colour. The opacity after n levels is 1 − (1 − a)n, charted on the right.
  3. Creatures. Drawn on their own layer at the level they swim on, then multiplied toward the water's colour, so only the creature's own pixels darken.
  4. DF's top level. DF's own map pass, with its z-fog off over plugin cells, so the water does not turn milky.
  5. Surface. Shimmer on standing water and current streaks on rivers, only where the camera looks down onto water.
  6. Waves. Crests on wave fronts, then the landing ~ on shore tiles. DF draws its spray itself.

Measured 3 Oct at a 120×68 view: 47.8 frames a second with the plugin against 42 for vanilla, since the plugin replaces DF's per-cell water redraw.

Open for tuning

The next round is about looks. The sampler at the end of this page holds candidate art for the first three items.

Wave, foam and mist tiles art

The crest, the landing ~ and DF's mist sprite are all first drafts. Pick a direction from the sampler: soft, whitecap, swell, hard pixel or ASCII for crests; a swash, bubbles, lace or a foam line for the landing; puffs, dither or wisps for mist.

Transparency per depth numbers

One alpha per family sets the whole depth curve. Options: change the alpha, or give each depth its own value, for example clear shallows and a faster fade below level 3.

Surface shimmer skin art + numbers

Shimmer alpha 45 and streak alpha 70 today (shimmer ALPHA, streaks ALPHA). The sampler shows three other skins: caustic web, sun glints and wind ripples.

Blue square behind moving creatures bug

When a fish changes tiles, its tile can show a solid water-blue backing for a moment, hiding the bed. Not yet diagnosed. The likeliest source is the creature layer or DF's own unit tile being drawn opaque on the frame the plan updates.

Smaller items later

  • Fronts step a whole tile at a time. DF's move timer could slide the crest between tiles, once its count is measured on the rig.
  • Diagonal coasts get short slanted strokes rather than one joined line.
  • The release zip (5fe7208c) predates the wave work and needs rebuilding. The dll is tied to DFHack 53.16-r2.

How we got here

The plugin's experiment log, in four stretches.

3 Oct · E1–E10

Depth compositing. Fixed the sand flash on scrolling and the pop on z-change, stuck depth digits, creatures skipping levels, missing slopes and milky water. Fish sprites added to the mod.

3–4 Oct · E11–E20

River, lake and cavern forts. Shore glow, surf, lake shimmer and river streaks. A crash on map load fixed; the first release package built.

4–5 Oct · E21–E28

Play tests. Crest strokes and edge foam dropped as fake, a crash in the user's game fixed, the skin moved up a level, creature tint squares removed, the water scan made native.

5 Oct · E29–E33

DF's real wave process found and proven with test tiles. Crest and landing art drawn, the missing white stage traced to fronts at counter 0, the ~ limited to landings.

What changed since

  • The 1 Oct page ranked a C++ plugin last. A raws mod could not show anything below DF's first water level, and a Lua overlay cost 16–24 FPS. The plugin became the chosen path on 3 Oct.
  • "Waves actually spawn in graphics mode" was open then. They do: about 200 fronts at a time from 3 wave makers in the user's fort.
  • The translucent-water raws mod and the Lua wave overlay are retired. The Lua overlay stands down whenever the plugin is enabled.
