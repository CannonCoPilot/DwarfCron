Land natives, water natives: what happened to the river fish

Correction. My first answer (river fish file as land natives because rivers are not water features) was wrong. A probe of every wild animal in the water on three end saves (waterfall, lake + river, ocean + river) shows what really happens.

  • Where a wild animal comes from. Every wild animal DF puts on the map belongs to a population: a region's population (land animals), a feature's population (water animals: on all three forts every water animal, river or lake, points at feature 0 of type outdoor_river), or a cave's population (cavern animals). The tool reads a unit's layer from that source: a feature means water, a cave means cavern, anything else land. So the river fish are water animals to the tool, correctly.
  • Natives and waves in DF. DF stocks the map once at embark (the natives), then sends more from the map edge over time (the waves, each unit flagged roaming_wilderness_population_source, which is how DF knows to send it away again). The probe found that DF's embark fish carry that wave flag too: the waterfall's carp, milkfish, tigerfish and stingrays, the lake fort's sturgeon and alligator. Its embark otters and turtles do not.
  • Natives and waves in the tool. The tool takes a wild animal into its groups in one of two ways. Discovery takes every flagged DF wave, but skips any whose source is a water feature, because DF sends no water waves in play (finding E20). Adoption takes unflagged natives: in the caverns always, in the water always (seeded_water), on land only with natives_land on (off by default). A flagged fish from a water feature is neither: discovery skips it for its source, adoption skips it for its flag. That is the whole gap: 37 on the waterfall, 64 on the river drop, 2 on the lake fort.
  • Why the lake fort had so few. Not because lakes differ: its water animals at embark were few (8). Everything else in its water was the tool's own draws, which join a group the moment they are placed.
  • Ocean + river. Two sources, two gaps. River (and lake) animals come from the river feature, as above: an embark fish carries the wave flag and falls between discovery and adoption. Sea animals are different: an earlier probe (F14, the OCEAN test fort) found that ocean life has no feature index; it comes from the ocean tile's own surface entries, so the tool reads a sea fish's source as land, and an unflagged one is a land native, which is not adopted while natives_land is off. On the ocean + river fort no native fish were left at the end to show either case (its water held the tool's draws, all grouped, and 50 sponges); its embark otters were unflagged river natives and were adopted.
  • Land natives are the land animals DF placed at embark (unflagged). DF leaves them be: they wander and stay. The tool leaves them as DF's (no group, no leader, never sent off) unless natives_land is on.

The fix (in the v7.3 build). Under season casts only cast species may be on the map, so natives can no longer be left as DF's. Every native, on every managed layer and with or without the flag, is adopted into a group at load; a native of a species not in the season's cast is sent off with the season's other off-cast groups.
