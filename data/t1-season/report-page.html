v7.3 season casts: first runs

v7.3 makes the cast the season's roster: per layer and season a small set grown from a high-value seed (your ruling of 7 October: the pool is not the roster), every other species resting. These runs check that arrivals follow the cast. Groups come from the tool's ledger: a tool draw (water) or a DF wave the tool found (land, caverns). Units come from the recorder and include births. A stand-in is a species the exhaustion check handed a cast member's niche.

Result. Arrivals follow the cast. On the final engine the ocean fort (V73_OCEAN3) and the lake fort (V73_LAKE3) ran through two season changes each: every arrival on land, in the water and in the caverns belonged to its season's cast, each season applied at its own tick, and each change sent off the groups whose species left the cast, ocean groups included. The runs found and fixed five things in the tool, and settled one question:

  • The exhaustion check ran before the season's apply (6e1ffb2): at autumn 1 it handed 17 of the 22 cast species' niches to stand-ins. The same bug made test item 1's summer-1 hand-overs (corrected under Findings).
  • A season change waited for the daily job (34a3a4b): up to a day at the old season's odds; a spring troll came in 291 ticks into summer. The change now applies at its own tick.
  • Aquatic vermin were cast on land, and one species could fill two vermin slots (29a8a91).
  • Ocean and river groups escaped the turnover (8817eb4): their species are filed under a land key; 3 orcas, cast spring and winter, stayed into summer.
  • The casts were saved but never loaded (b0895b9): after any save and load a fort ran with no casts. This would have hit every fort you save.
  • The giant wrens of the first run were not reproduced with the wren held; the hold was measured to work.

Two rig lessons, not tool faults: a small fort on a lake or ocean seam can miss the water (the water tile's edge rows take their neighbours' biomes; forts are now placed on the water tile's own cells), and the rig's embarks carry no drink, so a salt-water fort died of thirst (the runner now waters the dwarves).

Land natives, water natives: what happened to the river fish

Correction. My first answer (river fish file as land natives because rivers are not water features) was wrong. A probe of every wild animal in the water on three end saves (waterfall, lake + river, ocean + river) shows what really happens.

  • Where a wild animal comes from. Every wild animal DF puts on the map belongs to a population: a region's population (land animals), a feature's population (water animals: on all three forts every water animal, river or lake, points at feature 0 of type outdoor_river), or a cave's population (cavern animals). The tool reads a unit's layer from that source: a feature means water, a cave means cavern, anything else land. So the river fish are water animals to the tool, correctly.
  • Natives and waves in DF. DF stocks the map once at embark (the natives), then sends more from the map edge over time (the waves, each unit flagged roaming_wilderness_population_source, which is how DF knows to send it away again). The probe found that DF's embark fish carry that wave flag too: the waterfall's carp, milkfish, tigerfish and stingrays, the lake fort's sturgeon and alligator. Its embark otters and turtles do not.
  • Natives and waves in the tool. The tool takes a wild animal into its groups in one of two ways. Discovery takes every flagged DF wave, but skips any whose source is a water feature, because DF sends no water waves in play (finding E20). Adoption takes unflagged natives: in the caverns always, in the water always (seeded_water), on land only with natives_land on (off by default). A flagged fish from a water feature is neither: discovery skips it for its source, adoption skips it for its flag. That is the whole gap: 37 on the waterfall, 64 on the river drop, 2 on the lake fort.
  • Why the lake fort had so few. Not because lakes differ: its water animals at embark were few (8). Everything else in its water was the tool's own draws, which join a group the moment they are placed.
  • Ocean + river. Two sources, two gaps. River (and lake) animals come from the river feature, as above: an embark fish carries the wave flag and falls between discovery and adoption. Sea animals are different: an earlier probe (F14, the OCEAN test fort) found that ocean life has no feature index; it comes from the ocean tile's own surface entries, so the tool reads a sea fish's source as land, and an unflagged one is a land native, which is not adopted while natives_land is off. On the ocean + river fort no native fish were left at the end to show either case (its water held the tool's draws, all grouped, and 50 sponges); its embark otters were unflagged river natives and were adopted.
  • Land natives are the land animals DF placed at embark (unflagged). DF leaves them be: they wander and stay. The tool leaves them as DF's (no group, no leader, never sent off) unless natives_land is on.

The fix (in the v7.3 build). Under season casts only cast species may be on the map, so natives can no longer be left as DF's. Every native, on every managed layer and with or without the flag, is adopted into a group at load; a native of a species not in the season's cast is sent off with the season's other off-cast groups.

The count comes straight from how the roster is dealt, and nothing in the code bounds it:

  • The candidate pool is large. It is every population entry in the embark's region tiles plus every neighbour a map block draws from (DF draws from all nine). A 16x16 embark spans several biomes: the river + ocean fort's land pool was 168 species.
  • The preset activates every candidate. Every in-embark species that is not mega, night or locked is turned on. The guild-first builder (roster build), whose slot maxima would cap land at about 8 species, only runs when called by hand.
  • Every active species gets a season. Each role group (realm, habitat, role) is sorted by climate and dealt one season per species in quarters, so each season gets about a quarter of the active roster. The rules forbid a species with no season.
  • Additions on top. The pair guarantee (up to 4 a group), vermin defaults (3-4 seasons each), exhaustion hand-overs (14-28 a fort at summer, all of them the timing bug in the findings) and water season spill each add seasons.
  • Arrivals follow the roster: in spring 7-13 cavern species arrived on each fort. Every cavern arrival was an in-season roster species, or one the roster leaves unassigned (blood men, mud men: 17-26% of cavern arrivals), which the tool never holds back. No off-season cavern species arrived.

What would bring it to 5-10 per layer per season (deep: 1-3):

  1. A cast per season. Pick up to N species per layer per season (default 8, deep 2) by guild slot: on land 1 apex, 2 mesopredators, 3 grazers, 2 others, along the trophic pyramid, with your uniform selection and the x3 pack bonus. Every other species stays active but seasonless (resting) this year. That needs one rule change: “at least one season” becomes “at least one season or resting”. Rotate the cast each year so variety comes across years, not within a season.
  2. Enforce it at one gate. ROSTER.wants is the single check behind the live stock writes, the out-of-season hold, the cavern odds, the water draw candidates, the apex scheduler and the exhaustion check. A cap there holds everywhere.
  3. Make the additions swap, not add. An exhaustion hand-over or a spill borrow replaces a cast member one for one, and the pair guarantee works inside the cast.
  4. Leave vermin out of the count (they never come as waves), and run the builder automatically at embark instead of the plain preset.

Stopping DF's own waves, so only the tool's waves and placements arrive

Where arrivals come from now (the tool's ledger, last stretch of each run): every land and cavern arrival is a DF wave the tool opened the gate for or failed to hold; every water arrival is the tool's own draw. DF's water features never send waves (E20). So water is already tool-only, apart from what DF stocked at embark.

  • Land: doable with what exists. The tool already zeroes the stock (and sets extinct) of out-of-season and inactive land entries every pass; either one alone stops DF drawing that species (E8, N1). Zero every managed land entry in every season instead, keep the stock in the tool's reserve, and have the tool place its land waves itself at the map edge (dfhack.units.create plus the six writes that work headless). The irruption code already places from the reserve this way. The cost: a placed wave has to walk in and later leave like a DF wave, and send-off must not refund the stock to DF.
