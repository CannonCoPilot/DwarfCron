How Dwarf Fortress wildlife really behaves, and what it means for seasonal-wildlife

Wild animals in DF 53.16 hunt only when a predator relation exists, and only a non-BENIGN animal acts on one; LARGE_PREDATOR starts nothing we could measure. DF writes those relations from the fort's side toward new arrivals, almost never between two surface wild animals, so hunting between wild animals there needs the tool. Pack size predicts kills; prey mass doesn't lower them, and it harms hunters only at elephant scale or when the prey fights back. Leaders hold groups together. The threshold sweep could not judge most of the tool's dials, because three of its manipulations never reached their subject. Your rulings on all of it are in, and seasonal-wildlife v7.1 builds them; none of v7.1 has run on the rig yet.

1Key findings

Twenty results the tool's design rests on, as corrected by your review. Each names the block that measured it; the sections below hold the evidence.

No relation, no hunting.

0 attacks in 48 placed predator pairs over 3,000 ticks without a PREDATOR_OR_PREY relation. Written, every non-BENIGN predator attacked. One run per pair; the pattern holds across the 40-cell matrix.

P1 · 1 run per cell
BENIGN decides who acts; LARGE_PREDATOR starts nothing.

A BENIGN wolf never attacked; a coyote, not LARGE_PREDATOR, made 343 attacks on deer. LARGE_PREDATOR's only measured effect: a BENIGN badger given it was drawn into fights with the fort's dogs. Its documented roles (one group per map, a worldgen list, maybe its own wave pool) are untested here.

P1 · T1 · HO · E32b
DF aims the fort's side at arrivals, not wild at wild.

Citizens, livestock, pets and anything a script placed or released marked 92 of 103 surface arrivals with PREDATOR_OR_PREY; surface wild animals marked each other 9 times. In the caverns, natives DF drew mark each other. No attack followed any of it.

RELS · RELS2b · 2 reps
Pack size predicts kills; prey mass doesn't lower them.

Within wolves, kills rose with pack size (×3.8 odds per doubling) and not with the pack-to-prey mass ratio (×0.96). Prey mass did not raise wolf deaths from deer to buffalo; elephants killed 28% of wolves, and wolf-sized capybaras, which aren't BENIGN, killed 5 of 5.

PK · CAL · SW · R3 re-analysis
Savage giant packs take megafauna.

10 giant hyenas killed both elephants in both runs; 4 giant saltwater crocodiles killed both hippos in all 4 runs. Elephants killed none of 20 giant hyenas.

GPK · GPKW · GPKR · 2 reps
Lone hunters need dense prey.

Spread out, they rarely meet any. Among 48 placed prey, the skill package doubled a cougar's kills of them, 14 to 29 over 10 seasons per arm (one-sided p = 0.07); its cells always ran in the same order, so the gain is not clean. Your ruling keeps the package (R8).

STL · STL2 · LONE10 · 10 runs per arm
Amphibious predators cross the shore; aquatic ones never leave the water.

Alligators killed 5 of 8 deer from the water and went 15 tiles inland. Sharks never attacked deer on land and took shore seals only when they entered the water. Wolves never caught fish, even breathing water.

W1L · W1O · REACH · FISH
Leaders hold every kind of group.

Flocks and schools held to 5–30 tiles with a leader against 97–149 without (2 reps); orca pods half as wide. Without predators a led deer herd stayed within 2–4 tiles against 52 (1 run).

L1 · COH
FREQUENCY is a relative weight.

Wave shares 61 / 24 / 13% at FREQUENCY 100 / 50 / 25 (1 run), per layer in the caverns too. Halving the predator ladder cut predators to about 2% of land animals in both cell orders.

F1 · ECO2-FC · SW4
The apex step did what it was set to; the target was too low.

The ladder gave apexes 6–8% of land weight, which predicts about 4.8 apex waves over the six season runs; 6 came. Your rulings take the step off the ladder and steer apexes by placement and stock toward a pyramid target (R34, R9).

S8 · SW4 · D §1.3
Raw NO_<season> flags win unless cleared.

A NO_WINTER groundhog took 0 of 86 winter waves over 2 runs; with the flag cleared, 40 of 57. The tool's own season deal governs (R29).

T2 · ECO2-W
DF didn't draw a SAVAGE species onto a calm map.

The one species tested, the smilodon, never came to calm CTRL in a season (2 runs), though its population entry was added. Add invasive now places such species itself (R36).

INV2 · 1 species, 1 fort
An emptied entry stops a species at once.

DF never sets the extinct flag; watch the entry's quantity. In one run a replacement's first wave came 2,090 ticks after the hook fired.

N1 · 1 run
Nothing in DF eats remains; the tool's pass does, too fast.

No animal moved or ate a corpse on any layer. v7.0's pass cleared land and water carcasses within about 2,400 ticks, far faster than nature; v7.1 slows it to a natural pace (R18).

S · SCV · SCV2b
Gobbling is class-specific, and a written token works.

Badgers given GOBBLE_VERMIN_CLASS:EDIBLE_GROUND_BUG took 7 and 10 roaches and no grasshoppers (2 of 2 reps); DF honours a class the tool writes onto vermin at run time (2 of 2). Two vermin species only.

VRM2 · VRM4
Curious beasts leave because they stole.

18 of 18 placed bears and raccoons left within 3,000 ticks. Their leave countdown is reachable, but DF zeroes it at the theft and zeroed it again within 300 ticks of a reset. Clearing the CURIOUS_BEAST flags kept them (0 of 10 left).

CB · B
The sweep could not judge most of the tool's dials.

The placed wolf pack was never a tracked group, so the pack floor and sneak bonus weighed one wolf and no arm wrote SNEAK; the nudge fired 0 times. Cells shared a load in a fixed order, and two reps see only effects of about ×5.

SW1–SW7 · B §6.2
Cavern invasions are disabled in DF's code.

No flag or setting can raise one: two seasons with caverns open and irritation pinned raised none. Irruption is the tool's replacement, an ecological surge of cavern dwellers, rebuilt in v7.1 (R4, R5).

E23e · T9c · R4 · R23
Fortress oceans are 1–2 levels deep.

OCEAN2 has no column 3 levels deep (column depth, stacked water tiles). Its pelagic slot came out empty for a code reason: ocean life rides land-layer entries, which v7.0's water roster never read. The stranded orcas were the harness's unled control.

DEPTH · S8O · COHO
Wild-only combat alerts can be dropped safely.

In a prototype test (one fight per case), the filter dropped wild-only alerts, kept fights with dwarves, wolf men and goblins, and survived a save and reload.

2How the study ran, and the words it uses

Small, fast cells on saved test forts that are restored before every block and never saved. From 30 Sep most blocks ran 2 replicates (LONE 5, LONE10 10, VRM3b 6); the first-day matrices and several 30 Sep probes ran once, and the text says "1 run" where one result stands alone. Your review found that most arena blocks ran their cells in one load, in a fixed order, so their effects are not clean. Every test from here on runs on world region8 at 5 replicates per arm, a fresh load per arm, every animal wiped first.

A cell

A cell places two groups of animals side by side, steps the game, reads who attacked and killed whom, and clears its animals. The usual cell was 6 predators and 10 prey placed 10 tiles apart, at least 30 tiles from any dwarf, for 3,000 ticks (2.5 in-game days). Longer cells ran 30,000 ticks (CAL, STL) or a full season of 100,800 ticks (LONE, S8).

  • An attack is one DFHack UNIT_ATTACK event, which fires only when a strike leaves a fresh wound or kills; blocked, dodged and parried blows are not counted. A kill is a death incident whose killer and victim were both placed animals. Natives and cavern fights are counted apart.
  • Arms. Most pairs ran with DF alone and with the predator relation written the way the tool's ecology job writes it.
  • Spawn questions (F1, T2, N1, INV2, S8) watched DF's own arrivals with the surface gate released every 1,500 ticks.
  • Desk work covered the wiki definition of every token asked about, a census of all 967 creatures in the two creature folders (767 vanilla plus 200 extinct), an audit of the tool's code, and about 68,000 simulated rosters. The token census counted 363 wild species; the population figures in section 18 use a wider filter, 736 wildlife species including 185 animal people and 178 giants.

Where an animal came from (R1)

Four origins, each a real code path. They differ mainly in one flag, flags2.roaming_wilderness_population_source (DFHack calls it ROMAING_WILDERPOP), which this report calls the roaming flag.

  • DF brought it (an arrival): DF drew a group from a regional, cavern or water population entry and walked it in at a map, wet or cavern edge. The roaming flag is set, the entry is debited, and DF gives it a leave countdown. While two or more flagged units of a layer remain, DF sends that layer no new wave.
  • It was released (a resident): the tool's gate took a group DF brought and cleared its roaming flag so DF could send the next one. Nothing else changes; DF still refunds the entry when it leaves.
  • The tool placed it: seasonal-wildlife made the unit with dfhack.units.create on a free tile of its layer, copied a managed entry's population reference, debited the entry, set a 25,000-tick countdown, cleared the roaming flag and reset its relation row to NONE (−1).
  • The rig placed it: the test harness's cx-eco spawn, used only in experiments. The same creation, but it borrowed the first matching entry in the world (or another species' entry), debited nothing, set a 200,000-tick countdown and reset the relation row to STRANGER (0). Every CAL, STL, LONE, REACH and GPK hunter was rig-placed. The v7.1 harness now places like the tool.

Native in the tallies means "not placed in this cell", whoever brought it. Wild has two meanings that disagree exactly on placed and released units: DF-wild (the roaming flag is set; DF's own aiming follows it) and DFHack-wild (isWildlife: the unit has a population reference). All four origins are DFHack-wild; only an arrival DF brought and nobody released is DF-wild. Citizens, pets, livestock, caravan animals, invaders and visitors are neither; megabeasts, forgotten beasts, night creatures and vermin sit outside both.

"The fort's side" (R2)

Every unit with an enemy-status slot whose roaming flag is clear: citizens, livestock and pets, caravan and visitor animals, and any placed or released animal. DF writes PREDATOR_OR_PREY from these units toward each new arrival, and the arrival writes back BENIGN_ANIMAL (if BENIGN) or DANGEROUS_ANIMAL. It is a disposition in DF's relation table, not an allegiance and not an attack. That the roaming flag is DF's test is a measured correlation over four blocks; WILD1 will test it directly.

Depth words (R22)

Water level is a tile's own fill, 1–7 (DF's flow_size); below 4/7 aquatic creatures suffocate. Column depth is how many water tiles stack in a column at 4/7 or more; DF stores no such field, so our code counts it. "Fortress oceans are 1–2 levels deep" is column depth. Underground, cavern layer 1–3 and the magma/underworld layer are used instead of "depth"; the raw UNDERGROUND_DEPTH runs one higher than DF's layer_depth (raw 1 = cavern 1).

Four traps the review exposed, and how they are handled

Placed animals aren't wild to DF. A placed or released animal has its roaming flag cleared, so DF aims it at every later arrival (RELS). That is why placed hunters killed the fort's native wildlife in CAL, STL and LONE. Kills here count placed pairs only; the v7.1 harness can keep the flag on placed units.
Cells that share a load run in order. CAL, PK, STL, STL2, LONE, LONE10 and SW1–SW7 ran their cells in one load per replicate, in the same order every replicate. Natives and the tool's own group records carried from cell to cell, and SW1R showed that can fake an effect. None of those blocks' effects is clean until rerun with a fresh load per arm and the order reversed, which the v7.1 harness now does by default.
A manipulation can fail silently. The sweep scored outcomes without first proving its dial changed anything: the placed pack was never a tracked group, SNEAK was never written and the nudge never fired. The v7.1 harness prints a manipulation check first and aborts the block when it fails.
A verb can say "on" while doing nothing. The first scavenging and season runs (SCV2, S8C) tested nothing: the tool was off, or a loop never ran. Each was rerun with a receipt printed every pass.

3Predation: what DF carries out

DF hunts only on a PREDATOR_OR_PREY relation, and any attacker that isn't BENIGN carries it out. LARGE_PREDATOR neither starts hunting nor is needed for it. Most fights end in flight rather than a kill, and the hunter is hurt mainly when the prey fights back.

  • DF alone never hunted. 0 attacks in the 40 predator × prey pairs and 0 in 8 predator × predator pairs, wolf packs side by side included (P1, 3,000 ticks, 1 replicate per cell).
  • BENIGN blocks it. The fox made 0 attacks in every cell; a wolf made BENIGN made 0 against 64 for the control. The BENIGN orca ignored written seals until the flag was cleared, then killed 4 of 8 (HO). A giant fox attacked deer only with BENIGN cleared.
  • Prey that isn't BENIGN fights back (1 run each). Water buffalo killed a lion; deer made non-BENIGN killed 2 of 6 wolves; capybaras killed all 5 wolves on the lake shore.
  • AT_PEACE_WITH_WILDLIFE doesn't stop a written relation: 328 attacks, 2 kills (1 run).
  • CRAZED and OPPOSED_TO_LIFE aim wildlife at the fort. CRAZED deer ignored the rabbits beside them, crossed 30 tiles and killed 3 dwarves.
  • Kills are few for the attacks made: 0–3 of 10 prey per cell, even at 300 attacks.

What LARGE_PREDATOR does (review item 1)

Documented (wiki): a drive to "attack other creatures that are smaller than it"; at most one large-predator group on a map (two on savage maps); a worldgen list of 7 large predators per biome; its own wave pool, so "a lion does not compete for selection with a gazelle". DFHack never reads the token.

Measured: it does not start hunting (flipping it on a coyote or off a wolf changed nothing without a relation, T1), and a relation does not need it (the coyote's 343 attacks; wolves hit buffalo 25 times their mass, so the "smaller than it" rule does not bind once a relation exists). Its one measured effect: a BENIGN badger given LARGE_PREDATOR fought the fort's dogs, 11 attacks, and the dogs attacked it 40 times, against 0 for plain badgers (E32b, 2 reps). Whether it has its own wave pool is open: the one clean datum (F1, 1 run) had LARGE_PREDATORs at FREQUENCY 1 crowded out by kangaroos like prey, against the wiki. LPP1 settles it at the testing stage.

In the tool, since v6.9 BENIGN is the arming switch: every non-BENIGN predator is armed. LARGE_PREDATOR only names the apex and raptor guilds, labels a pack for cohesion and multiplies pack "take" by 1.5, a factor with no measured basis.

Who gets hurt (R3)

Prey mass alone does not harm the hunter. Over deer to water buffalo (3.5 to 25 times a wolf's mass), wolf deaths stayed flat: 11 of 831 wolves, odds ×1.07 per doubling of prey mass (CI 0.68–1.68). Harm jumps in two cases: prey vastly larger than the hunter (elephants killed 28% of wolves at 125 times their mass, but none of 20 giant hyenas at 8 times), and prey that is not BENIGN, whatever its size (capybaras 5 of 5 wolves; raging or non-BENIGN deer 19 of 72). "Fights get longer with prey mass" is not supported once all the wolf data are pooled (×1.00). Injury severity was never logged; PMH1 (a mass ladder at fixed prey speed with a wound logger) is designed for the testing stage.

Over longer runs DF also writes relations itself, but on the surface only from the fort's side toward arrivals (section 4). So "no relation, no hunting" holds for DF's own arrivals, and every surface hunt between two wild animals needs the tool's write.

Between two surface wild animals it writes almost nothing; between cavern natives it writes freely. The roaming flag, not the species, decides which side a unit is on. 5Reach: across the shoreline, in the air and underground

Amphibious predators hunt across the shoreline both ways. Aquatic predators take prey only in the water, and land predators never fished on their own. Fliers take fliers, and cavern fliers take cavern prey. The same rules hold in cavern pools, rivers, lakes and the sea. v7.1 adds two levers to change the last two: bears that fish, and raptors that stoop on land prey.

  • Crocodilians leave the water to hunt. Alligators killed 5 of 8 deer and 5 of 8 capybaras from the water; saltwater crocodiles killed 4 of 8 water buffalo. In REACH, 1–3 of 3 crocodiles stood on dry ground in every sample, 15 tiles inland.
  • Sharks hunt only in the water. Blue sharks killed 4 of 8 seals placed in the water. Seals placed on the shore drew 53 attacks and lost 1, a seal that entered the water; seals fled onto land. Tiger sharks took 5 of 6 swimming ducks in both replicates (WB). Sharks and lampreys never attacked deer on land.
  • Land predators don't fish on their own. Wolves already swim; with water-breathing added they touched pike 1–2 times, never killing one. Grizzlies and tigers given swimming made 0 attacks on pike in 4 runs, and the wolf control 0 in 2 (FSH2). Your ruling (R33, R43) is to make it work for bears rather than switch it off: v7.1 pairs a bear with a shore, walks it to the bank and lures the school to the edge in its run season, and polar bears swim to the fish. Built, untested.
  • Fliers take fliers. A kea flock killed 5 of 12 parrots; a BENIGN-cleared eagle killed 2 of 12 ravens but never engaged a rabbit. Your ruling (R47) wants raptor-on-land edges real and frequent: v7.1 arms raptors, caps their prey at twice their mass and brings a raptor down beside related small land prey (a stoop).
  • Cavern fliers take cavern prey: giant bats killed 5 of 12 crundles.
  • Lake species are surface units and take a written relation: 21 of 21 alligator–carp pairs held to 3,000 ticks (LAKEP).
  • Cavern natives fight mostly by species. 5 of 8 pairs behaved the same at a second spot (HC4): trolls × gorlaks and troglodytes × elk birds fought at both; jabberers, blind cave ogres and giant olms at neither. Cave toads, cave crawlers and cave crocodiles moved with the spot. The tool keeps writing cavern relations.

What the tool got wrong before v6.9. Water-layer units were left out of the ecology, ocean species drawn from surface entries counted as land, and only 42% of the 2,599 pairs it would write passed its own eats() rules. v6.9 filters the write by reach and size.

6Group hunting: pack size, prey mass and prey speed

Kills follow how many hunters there are and whether the prey can outrun them, not the pack's mass against the prey's. Prey mass doesn't lower kills; it hurts the hunters only at elephant scale. Savage giant packs took elephants, rhinoceros and hippos. Predation thins herds; it never wiped one out.

  • A lone wolf barely engages even a deer (0–1 attacks). Kills start at 5–7 wolves, up to water buffalo (PK, 3,000 ticks, 2 replicates).
  • Is it pack mass over prey mass? No (review item 3). Within wolves, kills rose with pack size, odds ×3.8 per doubling (CI 1.0–13.8), and not with the ratio of pack mass to prey mass (×0.96); a model forcing the ratio is rejected (p = 0.019). The best single predictor was whether the prey could outrun the hunter: in STL the odds of a kill were ×0.20 when the prey was the faster (CI 0.06–0.71), and in LONE kills rose ×1.85 for every 100 units slower the prey's sprint, with prey mass flat.
  • Prey mass shapes pairing only through the tool. DF pairs and attacks on the relation alone; the tool's 5% pack-mass floor and its eats() size rule are the only places prey mass decides anything.
  • Deer escape by running. Across CAL's wolf cells the kills were deer 1, moose 4, water buffalo 7, elephants 3, and ten hyenas killed 8 elephants over 2 replicates. Speed in ticks per 100 tiles (lower is faster): elk 122, deer 137, wolf 149, moose 157, buffalo 183, elephant 488.
  • Predation thins, never wipes out: at most 5 of 6 prey in 25 days. The best rate was 1.3 kills per 10,000 ticks (10 hyenas on elephants).
  • Savage giant packs take megafauna (GPK). 10 giant hyenas killed both elephants in both runs; 10 giant dingoes 1 of 2 rhinoceros per run; 4 giant saltwater crocodiles both hippos in all 4 runs. No hunter was lost.
  • The pack-mass floor. In CAL, hunts below 5% of the prey's mass made 0 kills and lost 4 hunters; 5–20% made 15 kills. v7.0 writes an edge only at 5% or more, using the group actually present. The sweep never tested it: the placed pack was never a tracked group, so the floor weighed one wolf (section 12).

CAL and PK ran their prey in mass order inside one load, so wolf deaths against cell position (ρ 0.29) cannot be separated from mass there; the SW arenas, with all three prey in every cell and both orders, carry the clean contrast (elephant 56 of 200 wolves, buffalo 2, deer 0).

7Solitary hunters and SNEAK

A lone hunter's problem is meeting prey. Among 48 placed prey, the skill package doubled a cougar's kills of them over 10 seasons per arm (29 against 14, one-sided p = 0.07), but the package always ran in the same cell position. SNEAK on its own was never shown to help, and only one level of it was ever tried. Your ruling: keep the solitary package, SNEAK included, and research it after the alpha (R8, R58).

  • Spread out, lone hunters rarely kill. In CAL's 12 solitary cells there was 1 kill. Pooling STL and STL2 (64 runs of 30,000 ticks), skilled or AMBUSHPREDATOR hunters killed in 10 of 48 runs and unchanged ones in 2 of 16; adding CAL's lone cells, 3 of 28 (Fisher's exact, p = 0.35). STL could only have detected effects of about ×5 or more.
  • SNEAK alone (review item 12). Kill ratio against the control 1.75 (CI 0.44–8.2), all of it lion kills, and the lion's own control killed as often. That does not show SNEAK alone was best; it doesn't rule it out either. The one gain whose interval clears 1 is the full package in LONE/LONE10, ×2.1 (CI 1.2–3.9), confounded with cell order.
  • Do wild animals sneak? Not shown either way (item 13). 0 of 426 snapshots, one tick each, 5,000 ticks apart, caught a hunter hidden. That caps hidden time near 0.7%, which still allows one short hidden approach per engagement, and half of that test read a different flag. By the wiki and DFHack's speed code, SNEAK acts only while a unit is hidden. SNK1–SNK3 sample the hidden flag every 10 ticks around attacks and time when prey start to flee.
  • Combat skills: more strikes, not more engaged runs (item 14). Wounding strikes rose about ×8.7 (CI 2–37), but 276 of the 315 came from two cougar × deer runs, and the share of runs with any attack didn't change (5 of 8 against 11 of 16). 9Groups, leaders and curious beasts

A leader holds herds, packs, flocks, schools and pods together on every layer. A groups-at-once limit of √(embark tiles) + 1 added about one group on 5×5 and 6×6 maps; below that, the number of groups was held near 2–3 by how often groups are released and how long they stay (Little's law), not by the limit. Curious beasts visit to steal, then leave. Your rulings rebuild all three in v7.1.

  • Leaders on land (1 run each). Without predators (L1), a led deer herd stayed within 2–4 tiles against 52 unled, and a led wolf pack within 3–10 tiles against 46. Under wolf attack (L2), herds led by the lowest id and by the largest male spread 12 and 42 tiles against 69 unled, and took 1 attack each against 88. Which member leads made no consistent difference.
  • Every layer. Led gorlaks, troglodytes, pike, milkfish and carp spread 2–9 tiles against 33–47 (HC, HR, HO, HL). Flocks and schools held to 5–30 tiles against 97–149, in both replicates; a led orca pod was about half as wide (COH). Protection in water is unresolved: in one run tiger sharks killed 5, 10 and 1 milkfish from unled, lowest-id and largest-male schools.
  • Your leader rule (R32): the largest adult male leads; with no male there is no leader; if the leader dies the group stays leaderless and herds, packs, schools and pods panic. Schools and pods also need a leader in the water. Built in v7.1.
  • Groups at once (ECO2-G). At 5×5 and 6×6 every √ + 1 replicate (3.12–3.81 groups) held more than every default one (1.77–2.85); up to 4×4 the two were within noise. On CTRL, a 4×4, auto held 3.6–4.7 land groups and a limit of 1 held 1.4–2.4 (SW3B).
  • What holds groups below the limit (item 16). The tool released at most one gated group per 5–20-day gap (mean about 15,000 ticks), and a group stays 22,500–59,000 ticks (its leave countdown plus the walk off). By Little's law the number present is the release rate times the stay: about 2–3 whatever the limit. T8g's measured stay, about 34,500 ticks, matches. The limit binds only where it sits below that, which on bigger maps (longer walks off) is 5×5 and up.
  • What was tested, per layer. Land on CTRL only, caverns on BOATS only, water on BOATS's ocean and river only; lakes, pools and the per-layer switch were never run. The three tallies defined a "group" three different ways, none checked against DF's population entries. G2 recounts every layer three ways at the testing stage.
  • Your rulings (R7, R31, R44, R45). Every layer has its own limit, by map size or one fixed value for slower computers (no per-layer on/off). An adaptive release clock per layer aims at the limit with a stochastic feel. Caverns are gated at a fixed 5 groups per layer, natives counted. Water bodies are managed like land, without the old "2 per body". Built in v7.1.
  • Curious beasts (item 5). A placed raccoon walked about 35 tiles to the wagon pile, took a rope, and its leave countdown dropped to 0. The countdown is reachable, and raising it lengthens an ordinary animal's stay, but DF zeroes a thief's at the theft and zeroed it again within 300 ticks of the CB reset (4 of 4 left). Clearing the CURIOUS_BEAST flags kept them (0 of 10 left), species-wide. Your ruling (R30): let them steal first, then clear that one individual's curious flags and reset its countdown, so thieves keep meddling and the thief stays. Built in v7.1 (it also drops the loot where the thief stands).

10Arrivals: FREQUENCY, the trophic pyramid, seasons, exhaustion, invasives

FREQUENCY is a relative weight: each species gets about its FREQUENCY over the total of the pool's picks, per layer in the caverns. Raw NO_<season> flags win unless cleared. An emptied population entry stops a species, and DF never drew a SAVAGE species onto a calm map. Your rulings set a trophic pyramid as the ladder's target, counted in units.

  • FREQUENCY is proportional (1 run each). Four species at 100, 50, 25 and 12 took 61, 24, 13 and 0% of land waves against 53, 27, 13 and 6% predicted (F1, 38 waves). In cavern layer 3, FREQUENCY 50, 25 and 12 gave 55, 26 and 18% against 57, 29 and 14% (ECO2-FC). Arrival shares aren't presence: in SW4 predators made 11–14% of the animals present against 6% of FREQUENCY, because they stay while prey move on.
  • The predator ladder is the one lever that moved the map. Halving the predator FREQUENCY cut predators to 1.8% and 1.9% of land animals in both cell orders; doubling wasn't reliably above ×1 (SW4).
  • Why "the apex FREQUENCY doesn't bring apexes" (item 4). The ladder gave apexes 6–8% of land weight, which predicts about 4.8 apex waves across the six S8 runs; 6 came (0, 0, 0, 3, 2, 1). The step did what it was set to; the target was too low, and set in waves rather than animals. FQ1 tests FREQUENCY 1, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50, 75 and 100 one by one at the testing stage.
  • Target units, not waves (R59). A pack of 7 and a lone bear are each one wave. v7.1 divides each species' weight by its mean group size, which matches the group sizes seen on the rig (troglodyte 7.51 against 7.5, wolf 4.79 against 5).
  • NO_<season> wins unless cleared. With NO_SPRING set at run time, a FREQUENCY-100 kangaroo took 0 of 19 spring waves (T2). A winter groundhog with NO_WINTER took 0 of 42 and 0 of 44 waves; cleared, 21 of 32 and 19 of 25 (ECO2-W). The tool's own season deal governs (R29).
  • Exhaustion. A kangaroo entry of 3 went to 0 with its one wave and no kangaroo came after, though DF left the extinct flag false. A replacement's first wave came 2,090 ticks later, and it took 25 of the next 26 non-bird surface waves (N1, 1 run).
  • SAVAGE invasives on calm maps. The smilodon's entry was added in both runs of INV2, yet DF drew none in a season (one species on one fort). Your ruling (R36): Add invasive places such species itself.

13Scavenging

No creature in DF eats or moves remains, on any layer. v7.0's scavenging pass cleared carcasses on land and in water within about 2,400 ticks, which zips scavengers about like a clean-up sweep. Your rulings slow it to a natural cadence and add sharks and other carnivorous swimmers (R16–R18, R42).

  • Nothing scavenges on its own. Six fresh corpses beside vultures, hyenas, black bears, raccoons and wolves for 4,000 ticks: none moved or held one. The same held in caverns, pools and the sea, and in 4,000 ticks DF removed none of 7 cavern corpses (SCVC).
  • A full path works where a destination alone doesn't. Given a tile-by-tile path, 3 of 6 hyenas reached the corpses within 300 ticks, and deleting the corpses survived a save and reload (S2).
  • Why the rig's walk-and-eat fed wolves but not jackals, vultures or anything at the water (R16). The rig's eat checked once, at 400 ticks, so it caught only the wolves still beside the pile. Jackals and vultures carried a borrowed population entry (CTRL has none of their own); vultures fly and don't walk a ground path; water corpses lay a level below the scavengers, and the rig paths and eats only on the same level; alligators weren't scavengers to the tool at all.
  • The tool's pass with the tool on left 0–1 of 6 carcasses in every land cell and 0 in every water cell (SCV2b, SCV2Wb). The pass also counted CTRL's native ravens, and its fallbacks could not fire, because their state lived in one script copy.
  • Who counts as a scavenger was the tool's rule: BONECARN, CURIOUS_BEAST_EATER or a short named list, which among vanilla swimmers left only pond grabbers, sea serpents and sea monsters.

v7.1, built and untested

A carcass is found after hours, sooner when large (median 6 hours for 1,000 kg and up, 18 hours under 150 kg), and only by scavengers within perception range (land 20 tiles, fliers 40, swimmers 16). Scavengers walk; a stuck one gets one short hop as a last resort, under a daily budget. A feeder eats 5% of its own mass an hour, up to a quarter of it per visit, then is sated for 12 hours, so a big carcass takes days of visits. Carnivorous swimmers, sharks included, count as scavengers. Who ate what is recorded per species.

15Oceans and water

Fortress-map water columns are 1–4 levels deep, and deep columns exist on some maps and not others. The raws carry no water-depth token, so depth can't sort species, but it can sort maps. Your rulings keep column depth and a tile's water level apart (R22), and want prey on the map to pull pelagic predators into any ocean fort's shallows (review item 8, R14).

  • Column depth by fort. LAKE is 91% one level; OCEAN2 43% one and 57% two levels; BOATS has 23% at three and 9% at four.
  • Big fish don't suffer in 1–2-level columns over a few days: two whale sharks and three blue sharks in OCEAN2 were alive and moving 3,000 ticks later.
  • The raws hold no water-depth token (the cavern token UNDERGROUND_DEPTH is a different thing). UNDERSWIM is on 79 of 83 aquatic creatures. The usable signals are AMPHIBIOUS in an ocean biome (shore dwellers), body size, and BEACH_FREQUENCY.
  • Unled orcas strand (item 9). Orcas are AQUATIC with BEACH_FREQUENCY:10; 3 of 12 in unled pods drowned within 15,000 ticks, none of 12 in led pods. Those unled pods were the harness's control, not the tool's doing; the tool's own leader lever has never been measured in water.
  • Why pelagic predators stayed away (item 8). The one water-apex slot went to the saltwater crocodile, the pelagic slot was for prey and optional, large ocean species were drawn at a tenth on shallow maps, and the survey missed every level below the top.

v7.1, built and untested

One tick after every water placement, a stranding guard moves a unit that can't breathe. Every school and pod gets a leader that is in the water, and the draw is weighted by what is swimming in that body now (R62).

16Caverns, alignment, invasions and irruption

On good and evil forts the map's own alignment counts as natural and is managed, while the opposite alignment and night creatures stay locked. DF's cavern invasions are disabled in the game's code, not by any setting, until a future major version (R4, R23). Irruption is the tool's replacement: it livens cavern ecology, gives players something in place of the lost invasion, and must later live alongside a returned one.

  • GOODF and EVILF (region6). GOODF manages fluffy wamblers, pixies and fairies; EVILF manages harpies, ogres, blood gnats, knuckle worms and demon rats. Each fort locks the other alignment as mythic, and all 10 night creatures stay locked. Cavern evil species (blind cave ogre, gorlak) class as natural once the cavern layer is on.
  • Cavern invasions (E23e). BOATS's caverns hold 334 animal-person and civ-race populations. Two seasons with the caverns marked discovered, irritation pinned at 300,000 and three dwarves on the cavern floor raised 0 invaders in all four runs. No route can: the mechanic is off in DF's code.
  • Invasion and irruption (item 7). An invasion is DF's own army raid. An irruption, in ecology, is a sudden surge of a population beyond its normal range, driven by crowding, scarcity or disturbance. It is not a raid and uses none of DF's invasion machinery.
  • v7.0's irruption (R6). Pressure per cavern rose with citizens in that cavern's levels (0.02 per citizen per 300 ticks, decaying 1%); at 1.0 the next group DF drew there was flagged agitated for 10 days, then a 30-day cooldown. The player got no DF announcement, only ledger lines, and the early gap-halving took the highest pressure of any cavern for all of them. In T9b, 3 citizens crossed the threshold on day 19.
  • The halved cavern gate (T9c). Under pressure, the scheduled gap shortened from 8–20 days to 3–9, but waves then sat overdue for up to 42 days, and cavern animals were no more numerous (18–20 against 21–25).
  • Cavern limits (SW5). A cap of 1 or 2 per cavern held 14–16 groups against 24–27 at auto, with DF's natives outside the cap. Your ruling (R44) gates every cavern layer at a fixed 5, natives counted.

Irruption v2 in v7.1 (R4, R5, R6, R10, R35), built and untested

  • What drives it. Each cavern layer keeps its own pressure: citizens in and just above its levels, dig jobs there, a one-off pulse when the fort first breaks in, the cavern's scarcity, the season and a little chance. An untouched cavern never fires.
  • What the player is told. DF announcements at every step: "Something stirs in the first cavern...", the start (which pauses the game by default), each wave, the end ("The fortress has driven the troglodytes back...") and the cooldown's end. A warning lapses if pressure falls.
  • The event. Three waves, a day to three apart, of the cavern's own civilisation races (troglodytes, animal people, the civ set of R61), placed from that cavern's own population entries at twice their largest group size (4–20 each), entering at the edge farthest from the fort's access point. Animal-person group caps lift there while it runs.
  • Behaviour tokens on civ units (R10). Each enabled token goes to about 10% of a wave (at least one): CRAZED, rage, thief, sneak, wander, meander, linger, mischief, no-fear and ambush. Exactly one unit per wave carries them all. Every token is a toggle or slider in the Panel and the web companion (R5).
  • The fury tier goes to the animals. While a cavern irrupts, its non-civ animals are agitated (at most 30); the tribal dwellers never are.
  • The end. After 10 days, when the fort repels it (half the wave dead), when every wave is spent and gone, or 3 days after the last wave; then a 30-day cooldown for that cavern. Every write is undone.

Testing comes last: probes M1–M9 check that each write sticks and acts, then normal rotation against irruption without and with tokens, then a rebalance of wave size, spacing and token shares.

18What the vanilla raws hold

The population the tool works with: every vanilla wildlife species by guild, size, frequency, group size, seasons, biomes, locomotion and behaviour tokens. These distributions explain most of what the rig found: why surface predators are rare, why so few swimmers scavenge, and why apexes barely arrive.

Cavern depth (R28)

Layer 1 is not "only RAT_LARGE and MOLE_DOG_NAKED": those are the only two species confined to it. 37 species can live in cavern 1, 51 in cavern 2 and 39 in cavern 3 (figures 15a and 15a2, now sorted by depth and then by name). A species can be drawn for every layer its range covers if its biome matches that layer: chasm (land), water (pools) or lava (magma). MAGMA_CRAB is 3:5 with a lava-only biome, so it may live in cavern 3, the magma sea or the underworld, and was seen in CTRL's magma sea. The v7.0 tool never read the raw: it layers DF's waves by the cavern they come from, so DF's own waves are never misplaced, but its place verb would have put a magma crab on the surface and a placed fire imp on dry cave floor. v7.1 seats a roster species only inside its raw depth, puts lava-only species beside magma or refuses them, and refuses the magma/underworld layer in place. Raising cavern 1's diversity beyond the species DF populated there would need a borrowed population entry, as the rig does on the surface; that is untested underground.

Extinct creatures (R24)

The two workshop mods (3753912171, 3755752944) only restore natural attacks to the 100 extinct animal people; in the 53.16 raws 96 of those already re-apply every weapon of their root animal. The gaps that matter to the tool are in the 100 plain extinct animals: all 16 extinct land large predators sit at FREQUENCY 50 where vanilla apexes carry 2–5 (so on a savage map a T. rex is drawn 10–25 times as often as a lion), 17 large herbivorous dinosaurs and the glyptodon lack STANDARD_GRAZER, and four are misfiled (Titanoboa as a mesocarnivore, Dimetrodon as a water apex, the 47 kg Tiktaalik as an apex, Eoraptor as prey). v7.1 applies 83 corrections on load and restores them on unload: apex FREQUENCY 2–5, grazers, roles, clusters and realms, standing down where an installed mod covers the same thing. 19The roster builder and food-web rules

Built guild-first, every roster closes: over 54,560 offline builds, no isolated or split animal, no mutual predation, no humanoid-rule break. Guilds decide which species may meet; size and group bonuses only weight what the guilds allow. v7.0 carried a partial port; v7.1 carries the v2.2 builder with your rulings on picks, apexes, the flying layer and water apexes.

How a roster is built (v2.2; v7.1)

  1. Pool per layer: the region's population entries, by guild. Caverns by the cavern an entry belongs to, within the species' raw depth; GOOD/EVIL species on matching regions, cavern ones always. Ocean life on land-layer entries counts for the water roster.
  2. Slots per layer and guild (land: 1 apex, 1–2 mesocarnivores, grazers, other prey, shore; ocean, lake, river, flying and cavern tables of their own; the flying layer has one raptor slot). Picks are uniform within the biome filters, ×3 for pack hunters, with animal people at a tenth and giants at a fifth of the usual odds (R13, R26).
  3. Edges follow guild diet rules within reach, with BENIGN cleared on every armed predator and a 5% pack-mass floor. Animal people and cavern civ races hunt and are prey; ANT_MAN and the cavern vermin-root animal people are civ races (R61); forgotten beasts and megabeasts are never written.
  4. Guards. A species no predator can take drops out unless the map's vegetation supports it. Every unfilled slot is reported by cause; when a pass adds nothing, a new predator–prey pair is seeded.
  5. FREQUENCY ladder last, in units (each weight divided by mean group size), with the pyramid targets as the level totals and the v2.2 guild weights inside each level. Apexes are off the ladder and placed by the apex scheduler (R34). Within a guild, body mass−0.75, clamped 0.25–4×. Animal people and giants get lower FREQUENCY once seated; none falls to 0.

What the rules got wrong as first written

  • Fixed size bands made wolf, dingo, hyena, cougar and cheetah "small", barring them from deer.
  • Every giant has PETVALUE 500, so "high value" meant giants, which seeded 75% of land webs.
  • Animal people were 37–44% of surface pools; only 43% of surface predator links were ones DF can act on.
  • The cap table spanned classes some layers can't hold, so no web ever finished on its own.

Nine fixes, then the guild-first rebuild, closed those. The temperate lake had no apex in about half its rosters once fishing was switched off; your ruling (R43, R49) fixes bears' fishing instead and fills water apex slots from a curated aquatic-apex list, on which the sea lamprey is only a mesopredator. Animal people now carry their own body mass, and those of vermin roots become small predators (R25). The full history of each round, with your rulings, is in the appendix.

What v7.0's port left out, and v7.1 adds

  • The v2.2 ladder (v7.0 had v2.1's, about 31% land predators predicted against v2.2's 16%): ported, in units, with the pyramid targets.
  • Separate ocean, lake and river tables, a flying layer: added.
  • The SNP slot for prey-guild animal people and the giant boost by flag: added.
  • The builder's GOBBLE_VERMIN edge table: ported (R51).
  • Still absent: a ladder per water body or per cavern layer, since FREQUENCY is one value per creature.

The ×3 pack bonus never reached the apex in v7.0: it read a floored cluster midpoint (so a 1:2 hunter counted as solitary), always seeded with the alphabetically first species, and let animal people carry their root's cluster. v7.1 fixes all three (R41). Separately, v7.0's civ_hunt and civ_prey never fired, because pool entries lacked the civ flag; in v7.1 they act, which is new behaviour on the rig.
