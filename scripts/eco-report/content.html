ECO study · Dwarf Fortress 53.16 · DFHack 53.16-r1.1 · seasonal-wildlife v6.9–v7.0

How Dwarf Fortress wildlife really behaves, and what it means for seasonal-wildlife

Wild animals in DF 53.16 hunt only when a predator relation exists, and only a non-BENIGN animal acts on one. On the surface DF almost never writes such a relation between two wild animals, so hunting between wild animals there needs the tool. Group size, not prey size, decides kills; leaders hold groups together. Of the tool's fixed values, only the predator FREQUENCY ladder changed how many predators the map holds, and the group limits how many groups it holds. None changed kills measurably.

1Key findings

Twenty results the tool's design rests on. Each names the block that measured it; the sections below hold the evidence.

No relation, no hunting.

0 attacks in 48 placed predator pairs over 3,000 ticks without a PREDATOR_OR_PREY relation. Written, every non-BENIGN predator attacked. One run per pair; the pattern holds across the 40-cell matrix.

P1 · 1 run per cell
BENIGN is the switch, not LARGE_PREDATOR.

A BENIGN wolf never attacked; a coyote (not LARGE_PREDATOR) made 343 attacks on deer. Non-BENIGN prey fights back: capybaras killed 5 of 5 wolves (1 run).

P1 · T1 · HO · 1 run per cell
On the surface, DF almost never pairs wild with wild.

DF aims citizens, livestock and placed animals at new arrivals. Between surface wild animals it wrote 5 of 370 entries in RELS. In the caverns it pairs natives freely.

RELS · RELS2b · RELS3 · 2 reps
Pack size decides kills, not prey size.

A lone wolf barely engages a deer; 7 wolves and 10 hyenas killed elephants. Deer escaped by running; slower prey died more, though size and speed are confounded across the four prey.

PK · CAL · 2 reps
Savage giant packs take megafauna.

10 giant hyenas killed both elephants in both runs; 4 giant saltwater crocodiles killed both hippos in all 4 runs.

GPK · GPKW · GPKR · 2 reps
Lone hunters need dense prey.

Spread out, they rarely meet any. Among 48 placed prey, the skill package doubled a cougar's kills of them, 14 to 29 over 10 seasons per arm (one-sided p = 0.07); counting natives, 21 to 49 (p = 0.024).

STL · STL2 · LONE10 · 10 runs per arm
Amphibious predators cross the shore; aquatic ones never leave the water.

Alligators killed 5 of 8 deer from the water and went 15 tiles inland. Sharks never attacked deer on land and took shore seals only when they entered the water. Wolves never caught fish, even breathing water.

W1L · W1O · REACH · FISH
Leaders hold every kind of group.

Flocks and schools held to 5–30 tiles with a leader against 97–149 without (2 reps); orca pods half as wide. Without predators a led deer herd stayed within 2–4 tiles against 52 (1 run).

L1 · COH
FREQUENCY is proportional.

Wave shares 61 / 24 / 13% at FREQUENCY 100 / 50 / 25 (1 run), per layer in the caverns too. Halving the predator ladder cut predators to about 2% of land animals in both cell orders.

F1 · ECO2-FC · SW4
The apex FREQUENCY doesn't bring apexes.

At FREQUENCY 4–27, apexes arrived 0–3 times a season, and 0 times in all 4 CTRL runs (2 reps per fort). Placing them is the dependable route.

S8 · SW4
Raw NO_<season> flags win unless cleared.

A NO_WINTER groundhog took 0 of 86 winter waves over 2 runs; with the flag cleared, 40 of 57. v7.0 clears the flag on managed species.

T2 · ECO2-W
DF didn't draw a SAVAGE species onto a calm map.

The one species tested, the smilodon, never came to calm CTRL in a season (2 runs), though its population entry was added. Add invasive has to place such species itself.

INV2 · 1 species, 1 fort
An emptied entry stops a species at once.

DF never sets the extinct flag; watch the entry's quantity. In one run a replacement's first wave came 2,090 ticks after the hook fired.

N1 · 1 run
Nothing in DF eats remains; the tool's pass does.

No animal moved or ate a corpse on any layer. With the tool on, v7.0's scavenging pass cleared land and water carcasses within about 2,400 ticks.

S · SCV · SCV2b
Gobbling is class-specific, and a written token works.

Badgers given GOBBLE_VERMIN_CLASS:EDIBLE_GROUND_BUG took 7 and 10 roaches and no grasshoppers (2 of 2 reps); DF honours a class the tool writes onto vermin at run time (2 of 2).

VRM2 · VRM4
Curious beasts come to steal, then leave.

18 of 18 placed bears and raccoons left within 3,000 ticks; with the CURIOUS_BEAST flags cleared, 0 of 10.

CB
Most of the tool's dials don't move the map.

Ecology cadence, nudge, pack-mass floor, sneak bonus and the ×3 pack bonus showed no effect on kills once cell order was controlled. Kills were 0–4 per arm, so only a large effect could have shown.

SW1–SW7 · reruns
Flags don't start cavern invasions.

Two seasons with caverns discovered and irritation pinned raised none, even with 334 animal-person populations below; what does trigger one is untested. A halved cavern gate brought no extra traffic.

E23e · T9c · 2 reps
Fortress water is shallow.

OCEAN2 has no column 3 levels deep, so v7.0 gives it no pelagic boost, and its optional pelagic slot came out empty. Placed orcas strand when unled: 3 of 12 drowned in 15,000 ticks.

DEPTH · S8O · COHO
Wild-only combat alerts can be dropped safely.

In a prototype test (one fight per case), the filter dropped wild-only alerts, kept fights with dwarves, wolf men and goblins, and survived a save and reload.

2How the study ran

Small, fast cells on saved test forts that are restored before every block and never saved. From 30 Sep most blocks ran 2 replicates (LONE 5, LONE10 10, VRM3b 6). The first-day matrices and several 30 Sep probes ran once (P1, P2, T1, W1L, W1O, HC1–HC3, HCP, HR, HO, HL, F1, ECO2-FC, RELP, SLOTV, LAKEP); their conclusions rest on the pattern across cells, and the text says "1 run" where one result stands alone.

A cell

A cell places two groups of animals side by side, steps the game, reads who attacked and killed whom, and clears its animals. The usual cell was 6 predators and 10 prey placed 10 tiles apart, at least 30 tiles from any dwarf, for 3,000 ticks (2.5 in-game days). Longer cells ran 30,000 ticks (CAL, STL) or a full season of 100,800 ticks (LONE, S8).

  • An attack is one DFHack UNIT_ATTACK event. A kill is a death incident whose killer and victim were both placed animals. Natives and cavern fights are counted apart.
  • Arms. Most pairs ran with DF alone and with the predator relation written the way the tool's ecology job writes it.
  • Spawn questions (F1, T2, N1, INV2, S8) watched DF's own arrivals with the surface gate released every 1,500 ticks.
  • Desk work covered the wiki definition of every token asked about, a census of all 967 creatures in the two creature folders (767 vanilla plus 200 modded extinct), an audit of the tool's code, and about 68,000 simulated rosters. The token census counted 363 wild species; the population figures in section 18 use a wider filter, 736 wildlife species including 185 animal people and 178 giants.

Three traps the night exposed, and how they are handled

Placed animals aren't wild to DF. A script-placed animal has DF's wild flag cleared, so DF aims it at every later arrival (RELS). That is why placed hunters killed the fort's native wildlife in CAL, STL and LONE. Kills here count placed pairs only.
Cells that share a load run in order. Natives arrive during a replicate and pile into the later cells. SW1 and SW2 first read as lever effects; reversed-order reruns showed cell position. The runner now alternates the order between replicates.
A verb can say "on" while doing nothing. The first scavenging and season runs (SCV2, S8C) tested nothing: the tool was off, or a loop never ran. Each was rerun with a receipt printed every pass.

3Predation: what DF carries out

DF hunts only on a PREDATOR_OR_PREY relation, and any attacker that isn't BENIGN carries it out. LARGE_PREDATOR doesn't decide it, and most fights end in flight rather than a kill.

  • DF alone never hunted. 0 attacks in the 40 predator × prey pairs and 0 in 8 predator × predator pairs, wolf packs side by side included (P1, 3,000 ticks, 1 replicate per cell).
  • LARGE_PREDATOR isn't needed once the relation is written. The coyote made 343 attacks on deer. Flipping LARGE_PREDATOR on a coyote or off a wolf changed nothing without the relation.
  • BENIGN blocks it. The fox made 0 attacks in every cell; a wolf made BENIGN made 0 against 64 for the control. The BENIGN orca ignored written seals until the flag was cleared, then killed 4 of 8 (HO). A giant fox attacked deer only with BENIGN cleared.
  • Prey that isn't BENIGN fights back (1 run each). Water buffalo killed a lion; deer made non-BENIGN killed 2 of 6 wolves; capybaras killed all 5 wolves on the lake shore.
  • AT_PEACE_WITH_WILDLIFE doesn't stop a written relation: 328 attacks, 2 kills (1 run).
  • CRAZED and OPPOSED_TO_LIFE aim wildlife at the fort. CRAZED deer ignored the rabbits beside them, crossed 30 tiles and killed 3 dwarves.
  • Kills are few for the attacks made: 0–3 of 10 prey per cell, even at 300 attacks.

Over longer runs DF also writes relations itself, but on the surface only from units that aren't wild toward wild arrivals (section 4). So "no relation, no hunting" holds for DF's own arrivals, and every surface hunt between two wild animals needs the tool's write.

4Who DF aims at whom

DF writes PREDATOR_OR_PREY from every unit that isn't wild (citizens, livestock, placed animals, groups the tool releases) toward each new wild arrival. Between surface wild animals it writes almost nothing; between cavern natives it writes freely.

5Reach: across the shoreline, in the air and underground

Amphibious predators hunt across the shoreline both ways. Aquatic predators take prey only in the water, and land predators never fish. Fliers take fliers, and cavern fliers take cavern prey. The same rules hold in cavern pools, rivers, lakes and the sea.

  • Crocodilians leave the water to hunt. Alligators killed 5 of 8 deer and 5 of 8 capybaras from the water; saltwater crocodiles killed 4 of 8 water buffalo. In REACH, 1–3 of 3 crocodiles stood on dry ground in every sample, 15 tiles inland.
  • Sharks hunt only in the water. Blue sharks killed 4 of 8 seals placed in the water. Seals placed on the shore drew 53 attacks and lost 1, a seal that entered the water; seals fled onto land. Tiger sharks took 5 of 6 swimming ducks in both replicates (WB). Sharks and lampreys never attacked deer on land.
  • Land predators don't fish. Wolves already swim; with water-breathing added they touched pike 1–2 times, never killing one. Bears and tigers given swimming made 0 attacks on pike in 6 runs (FSH2), so v7.0's fishing switch now defaults off.
  • Fliers take fliers. A kea flock killed 5 of 12 parrots; a BENIGN-cleared eagle killed 2 of 12 ravens but never engaged a rabbit, so raptor-on-land-prey edges stay rare.
  • Cavern fliers take cavern prey: giant bats killed 5 of 12 crundles.
  • Lake species are surface units and take a written relation: 21 of 21 alligator–carp pairs held to 3,000 ticks (LAKEP).
  • Cavern natives fight mostly by species. 5 of 8 pairs behaved the same at a second spot (HC4): trolls × gorlaks and troglodytes × elk birds fought at both; jabberers, blind cave ogres and giant olms at neither. Cave toads, cave crawlers and cave crocodiles moved with the spot. The tool keeps writing cavern relations.

What the tool got wrong before v6.9. Water-layer units were left out of the ecology, ocean species drawn from surface entries counted as land, and only 42% of the 2,599 pairs it would write passed its own eats() rules. v6.9 filters the write by reach and size.

6Group hunting: pack size decides

DF's combat turns on how many hunters there are, not how big the prey is. Savage giant packs took elephants, rhinoceros and hippos. Predation thins herds; it never wiped one out.

  • A lone wolf barely engages even a deer (0–1 attacks). Kills start at 5–7 wolves, up to water buffalo (PK, 3,000 ticks, 2 replicates).
  • Deer escape by running. Across CAL's wolf cells the kills were deer 1, moose 4, water buffalo 7, elephants 3, and ten hyenas killed 8 elephants over 2 replicates. Speed in ticks per 100 tiles (lower is faster): elk 122, deer 137, wolf 149, moose 157, buffalo 183, elephant 488. Size, speed and fighting back are confounded across these four prey.
  • Predation thins, never wipes out: at most 5 of 6 prey in 25 days. The best rate was 1.3 kills per 10,000 ticks (10 hyenas on elephants).
  • Savage giant packs take megafauna (GPK). 10 giant hyenas killed both elephants in both runs; 10 giant dingoes 1 of 2 rhinoceros per run; 4 giant saltwater crocodiles both hippos in all 4 runs. No hunter was lost.
  • The pack-mass floor. In CAL, hunts below 5% of the prey's mass made 0 kills and lost 4 hunters; 5–20% made 15 kills. At your ruling v7.0 writes an edge only at 5% or more, using the group actually present, with a sneak bonus at 25%. The sweep couldn't separate either from noise (SW2, SW2R).

7Solitary hunters

A lone hunter's problem is meeting prey. Among 48 placed prey, the v7.0 skill package doubled a cougar's kills of them over 10 seasons per arm (29 against 14, one-sided p = 0.07). Spread out, skills made no measurable difference.

  • Spread out, lone hunters rarely kill. In CAL's 12 solitary cells there was 1 kill. Pooling STL and STL2 (64 runs of 30,000 ticks), skilled or AMBUSHPREDATOR hunters killed in 10 of 48 runs and unchanged ones in 2 of 16; adding CAL's lone cells, 3 of 28 (Fisher's exact, p = 0.35).
  • Wild animals never sneak. 0 of 426 samples showed a hidden hunter, with or without Sneak 10 or AMBUSHPREDATOR.
  • Combat skills raise engagement, not kills: the fight arm made the most attacks (315) at the others' kill rate.
  • Without a relation, a hunter ignores its placed prey (0 attacks in 8 runs).
  • Dense prey changes it (LONE, LONE10). One cougar among 6 each of 8 species, one season per run. Kills of the placed prey: 14 unchanged against 29 with the package (one-sided permutation p = 0.07). Native kills: 7 against 20. All kills: 21 against 49 (p = 0.024). Attacks 664 against 1,393. The natives were hunted only because a placed cougar is non-wild to DF, so the placed-prey figure is the clean one. The package cougar never died; the control died once.
  • FLEEQUICK and VISION_ARC showed no reliable effect over 4 replicates (FVA); the tool doesn't use them.

8Creature tokens: what the raws hold and what they do

Of the tokens tested, four flags and one value changed wild behaviour within 3,000 ticks: BENIGN, CRAZED, OPPOSED_TO_LIFE, MEANDERER (movement only), and PRONE_TO_RAGE at 25 or more. The CURIOUS_BEAST flags decide whether an animal stays, and NO_<season> changes what DF draws. Sixteen tokens asked about appear in no creature definition.

  • PRONE_TO_RAGE works by dose. Deer at 25, 50 and 100 made 17, 81 and 98 attacks on written wolves; at 100 they killed 4 of 6 wolves in both replicates (TV2). At 1, the badger's raw value, nothing happened.
  • VIEWRANGE leaned the expected way but isn't separable from noise. Deer that see 40 tiles drew 3 and 0 attacks, those that see 5 drew 168 and 7, and the control 7 and 3. VIEWRANGE is never set in the raws, so every creature sees 20 tiles.
  • MEANDERER off scattered deer over 72 and 65 tiles against 39 and 42, repeated in TV2. LOOSE_CLUSTERS made no difference.
  • UBIQUITOUS set at run time is ignored at the pick (0 of 13 and 0 of 23 waves); its role is worldgen.
  • The activity tokens (DIURNAL, NOCTURNAL…) are documented for Adventurer mode only.
  • Sixteen tokens appear in no creature definition. Six still exist in play: CRAZED (werebeasts), SENSE_CREATURE_CLASS (night trolls), BLOODSUCKER (vampire curse), OPPOSED_TO_LIFE (undead syndromes), NATURAL_ANIMAL (DF's name for [NATURAL]) and CANNOT_CLIMB (an announcement). The other ten exist nowhere, including GOBBLE_VERMIN_CREATURE and NO_SUMMER.
  • Token blocks. AQUATIC, IMMOBILE_LAND, NO_DRINK and UNDERSWIM travel together on 77 species. 25 of 32 grazers carry VISION_ARC 50:310, the only value in the raws. 11 of 12 LOOSE_CLUSTERS species are thieves. LARGE_PREDATOR belongs to no block and never co-occurs with BENIGN; 26 of the 60 wild predators carry neither CARNIVORE nor BONECARN.

9Groups, leaders and curious beasts

A leader holds herds, packs, flocks, schools and pods together on every layer. Whether it protects prey is open: one land run says yes, one water run says no. A groups-at-once limit of √(embark tiles) + 1 adds about one group on 5×5 and 6×6 maps. Curious beasts visit to steal, then leave, unless their flags are cleared.

  • Leaders on land (1 run each). Without predators (L1), a led deer herd stayed within 2–4 tiles against 52 unled, and a led wolf pack within 3–10 tiles against 46. Under wolf attack (L2), herds led by the lowest id and by the largest male spread 12 and 42 tiles against 69 unled, and took 1 attack each against 88. Which member leads made no consistent difference; v7.0 makes the largest adult male the leader at your ruling.
  • Every layer. Led gorlaks, troglodytes, pike, milkfish and carp spread 2–9 tiles against 33–47 (HC, HR, HO, HL). Flocks and schools held to 5–30 tiles against 97–149, in both replicates; a led orca pod was about half as wide (COH). Protection in water is unresolved: in one run tiger sharks killed 5, 10 and 1 milkfish from unled, lowest-id and largest-male schools.
  • Groups at once (ECO2-G). At 5×5 and 6×6 every √ + 1 replicate (3.16–3.81 groups) held more than every default one (1.77–2.85); up to 4×4 the two were within noise, with no speed cost anywhere. On CTRL, a 4×4, auto held 3.6–4.7 land groups and a limit of 1 held 1.4–2.4, in both cell orders (SW3B), so the limit binds there too.
  • Who brings the groups. On land and in the caverns DF draws every group; the tool only opens or closes the gate. It draws water groups itself and places animals only on command. So a higher limit fills only when DF has groups to send.
  • Curious beasts. A placed raccoon walked about 35 tiles to the wagon pile, took a rope, and its leave countdown dropped to 0. 18 of 18 placed bears and raccoons left within 3,000 ticks; resetting the countdown didn't hold them, clearing the CURIOUS_BEAST flags did (0 of 10 left). v6.9's curious TOKEN resident|thief picks per species.

10Arrivals: FREQUENCY, seasons, exhaustion, invasives

FREQUENCY sets a species' share of DF's waves in proportion, per layer in the caverns. Raw NO_<season> flags win unless cleared. An emptied population entry stops a species, and DF never draws a SAVAGE species onto a calm map.

  • FREQUENCY is proportional (1 run each). Four species at 100, 50, 25 and 12 took 61, 24, 13 and 0% of land waves against 53, 27, 13 and 6% predicted (F1, 38 waves). In cavern 3, FREQUENCY 50, 25 and 12 gave 55, 26 and 18% against 57, 29 and 14% (ECO2-FC). Each cavern layer draws from its own pool. Arrival shares aren't presence: in SW4 predators made 11–14% of the animals present against 6% of FREQUENCY, because they stay while prey move on.
  • The predator ladder is the one lever that moves the map. Halving the predator FREQUENCY cut predators to 1.8% and 1.9% of land animals in both cell orders; doubling wasn't reliably above ×1 (SW4).
  • NO_<season> wins unless cleared. With NO_SPRING set at run time, a FREQUENCY-100 kangaroo took 0 of 19 spring waves, though with the other species at FREQUENCY 1 it would otherwise have taken most (T2). A winter groundhog with NO_WINTER took 0 of 42 and 0 of 44 waves; cleared, 21 of 32 and 19 of 25 (ECO2-W). v7.0 clears the flag on managed species so the tool's own season deal wins, and restores it when switched off.
  • Exhaustion. A kangaroo entry of 3 went to 0 with its one wave and no kangaroo came after, though DF left the extinct flag false. A replacement's first wave came 2,090 ticks later, and it took 25 of the next 26 non-bird surface waves (N1, 1 run).
  • SAVAGE invasives on calm maps. The yeti was refused on biome (INV). The smilodon's entry was added in both runs of INV2, yet DF drew none in a season. That is one species on one fort, and the block didn't log the gate's other releases; still, Add invasive should place such species itself.
  • Apex FREQUENCY. Apex arrivals were 0 in all 4 CTRL season runs and 0–3 on BOATS and OCEAN2 at FREQUENCY 13–27, and an apex was present in 1 run of 6 in SW4 at any multiplier. DF may draw large predators from their own pool. Placing them is the dependable route; stocking them was not tested.

11A season on v7.0 with the roster builder

The builder fills every slot it has candidates for and names the gaps. Surface predators made 5–42% of arriving units. v7.0 carries the v2.1 FREQUENCY ladder, which the offline builder predicts gives about 31% predators on land; the 14–18% target belongs to the v2.2 ladder, which isn't ported yet. Giant jackal packs pushed BOATS up; CTRL's raven-heavy supply held it down whatever the roster said.

  • On CTRL the builder barely moves the surface. Builder off: 45 and 34 surface units, predators 4% and 0%. Builder on: 37 and 37, predators 5% and 5%. Ravens were most of both.
  • On BOATS the roster differs per run. Picks are uniform, and replicate 2 seated giraffe men and elephant men as grazers (72 giraffe men arrived). Water slots stayed empty: no water apex or freshwater fish exists in BOATS's pool, and the build output says so.
  • Plump helmet men swamp BOATS's caverns: 54 and 180 arrived, about 15 per wave. That slot needs a group-size cap.
  • No pelagics on OCEAN2. The v7.0 survey found 0 of 5,374 OCEAN2 columns 3 or more levels deep. The pelagic slot is optional (0–1) on a shallow ocean and rises to 0–2 with triple weight where deep columns exist; on OCEAN2 it came out empty, so nothing could strand. Placed orcas do strand: 3 of 12 drowned in 15,000 ticks when unled, none with a leader (COHO).
  • A deep layer delivered demons on CTRL: 15 and 16 units in 4–5 groups, which the tool leaves unmanaged by design.

The first S8C run built no roster: its setup looped over a string instead of a list. It stands as the builder-off control; S8Cb is the builder-on run.

12Threshold sweep: which fixed values matter

Only the predator ladder changed how many predators the map holds, and the group limits how many groups. No value changed kills measurably once cell order was controlled, but kills were so few (0–4 per arm over 4 replicates) that only a large effect could have shown. The cavern cap trims only the tool's own groups; the water cap rarely binds.

13Scavenging

No creature in DF eats or moves remains, on any layer. The tool can do it: v7.0's scavenging pass cleared carcasses on land and in water within about 2,400 ticks. Which species did the eating wasn't recorded.

  • Nothing scavenges on its own. Six fresh corpses beside vultures, hyenas, black bears, raccoons and wolves for 4,000 ticks: none moved or held one. The same held in caverns, pools and the sea, and in 3,000 ticks DF removed none of 7 cavern corpses (SCVC).
  • A full path works where a destination alone doesn't. Given a tile-by-tile path, 3 of 6 hyenas reached the corpses within 300 ticks, and deleting the corpses survived a save and reload (S2).
  • The rig's own walk-and-eat verbs fed wolves on land (6 and 2 carcasses) but not jackals, vultures or anything at the water (SCV, SCVW).
  • The tool's pass with the tool on left 0–1 of 6 carcasses in every land cell and 0 in every water cell (SCV2b, SCV2Wb). The pass saw 6–21 scavengers, including CTRL's native ravens, and its teleport fallbacks never fired.
  • Who counts as a scavenger is the tool's rule: BONECARN, CURIOUS_BEAST_EATER or a short named list. In vanilla that leaves only pond grabbers, sea serpents and sea monsters among swimmers.

14Vermin predation

DF's gobbling is real and class-specific, and a token the tool writes carries it: onto a predator that lacks it, and onto vermin as a runtime class. With no consumer and no pet cats near, placed vermin stayed put.

  • Native gobblers took roaches only. Ducks and hedgehogs carry GOBBLE_VERMIN_CLASS:EDIBLE_GROUND_BUG: they removed 3–8 roaches per replicate and no grasshoppers, which aren't in that class.
  • A written token works (VRM2). Badgers given the class took 7 and 10 roaches and no grasshoppers (2 of 2 replicates); naming the roach creature did the same in the one replicate the cats left alone. Badgers with no token took nothing selectively.
  • Cats take everything: 38 and 39 roaches, 20 and 19 grasshoppers. CTRL's own two cats were the background eater that spoiled 3 of 14 replicates, caught by VRM3b's 250-tick unit log.
  • A runtime class on the vermin works (VRM4). Badgers with a matching gobble class removed 4 and 6 grasshoppers carrying the written class SWV_TEST (40 on the map), all within the first 250 ticks; the control removed 0 and 0. So v7.0's own vermin classes can drive gobbling.
  • Where vermin live. The densest surface spots are bumblebee and termite colonies (14,000–19,000 each) that nothing hunts; caverns hold at most 2 vermin near any spot.

15Oceans and water depth

Fortress-map water is 1–4 levels deep, and deep columns exist on some maps and not others. Depth can't sort species, since the raws carry no depth token, but it can sort maps: v7.0 widens the pelagic slot and triples its weight only where 3+-level columns exist.

  • Depth by fort. LAKE is 91% one level; OCEAN2 43% one and 57% two levels; BOATS has 23% at three and 9% at four.
  • Big fish don't suffer in shallow water over a few days: two whale sharks and three blue sharks in OCEAN2's 1–2-level water were alive and moving 3,000 ticks later. Over 15,000 ticks unled orcas did strand (below).
  • The raws hold no depth token. UNDERSWIM is on 79 of 83 aquatic creatures. The usable signals are AMPHIBIOUS in an ocean biome (shore dwellers), body size, and BEACH_FREQUENCY.
  • Placed orcas strand when they roam. Orcas are AQUATIC with BEACH_FREQUENCY:10; 3 of 12 in unled pods drowned within 15,000 ticks, none of 12 in led pods.

16Caverns, alignment and invasions

On good and evil forts the map's own alignment counts as natural and is managed, while the opposite alignment and night creatures stay locked. Flags alone don't start a cavern invasion, and a halved cavern gate brings no extra traffic.

  • GOODF and EVILF (region6). GOODF manages fluffy wamblers, pixies and fairies; EVILF manages harpies, ogres, blood gnats, knuckle worms and demon rats. Each fort locks the other alignment as mythic, and all 10 night creatures stay locked. Cavern evil species (blind cave ogre, gorlak) class as natural once the cavern layer is on; it's off by default on a fresh embark.
  • Cavern invasions (E23e). BOATS's caverns hold 334 animal-person and civ-race populations. Two seasons with the caverns marked discovered, irritation pinned at 300,000 and three dwarves on the cavern floor raised 0 invaders in all four runs. Flags alone don't do it; what does is untested (excavation into a cavern, wealth and population are the candidates).
  • The halved cavern gate (T9c). Under pressure, the scheduled gap shortened from 8–20 days to 3–9, but waves then sat overdue for up to 42 days, and cavern animals were no more numerous (18–20 against 21–25).
  • Cavern limits (SW5). A cap of 1 or 2 per cavern held 14–16 groups against 24–27 at auto. DF's natives sit outside the tool's limit.

17Combat alerts and the fight log

Animal-vs-animal combat alerts can be dropped safely; DF's own announcement settings can't do it, because they switch per report type (43 COMBAT_* types), not per who is fighting.

  • The filter drops each COMBAT alert in which no unit is a citizen, of the fort's civilisation, or fort-controlled. In a prototype test, one fight per case: a wild fight gave 1 alert with the filter off and 0 with it on (3 dropped); wolves set on dwarves kept their alert; fights with wolf men and goblins kept theirs (A2); a save and reload with the filter running was fine.
  • The fight log takes attacks from DFHack's UNIT_ATTACK event and deaths from the incident list. It gave every count in this report. It's blind to fights in caverns nobody has opened, where DF writes no reports; deaths still appear.
  • v6.9 ships the filter on by default, with a Panel switch and alerts on|off; humanoid fights always alert.

19The roster builder and food-web rules

Built guild-first, every roster closes: over 54,560 offline builds, no isolated or split animal, no mutual predation, no humanoid-rule break. Guilds decide which species may meet; size and group bonuses only weight what the guilds allow. v7.0 carries the builder as ROSTER.build.

How a roster is built (v2.2, ROSTER.build in v7.0)

  1. Pool per layer: the region's population entries, by guild. Caverns by UNDERGROUND_DEPTH; GOOD/EVIL species on matching regions, cavern ones always.
  2. Slots per layer and guild (land: 1 apex, 1–2 mesocarnivores, grazers, other prey, shore; water, flying and cavern tables of their own). Picks are uniform, ×3 for pack hunters.
  3. Edges follow guild diet rules within reach, with BENIGN cleared on every armed predator and a 5% pack-mass floor. Animal people and cavern civ races hunt and are prey; forgotten beasts and megabeasts are never written.
  4. Guards. A species no predator can take drops out unless the map's vegetation supports it. Every unfilled slot is reported by cause; when a pass adds nothing, a new predator–prey pair is seeded.
  5. FREQUENCY ladder last. v2.2 scales each roster so its commonest member sits at DF's cap of 100, giving predators 16% of land arrivals, 10% ocean and lake, 12% river, 19% flying and 15–16% in the caverns (offline). v7.0 still carries the v2.1 ladder (about 31% on land); porting v2.2 is an open item. Within a guild, body mass−0.75, clamped 0.25–4×.

What the rules got wrong as first written

  • Fixed size bands made wolf, dingo, hyena, cougar and cheetah "small", barring them from deer.
  • Every giant has PETVALUE 500, so "high value" meant giants, which seeded 75% of land webs.
  • Animal people were 37–44% of surface pools; only 43% of surface predator links were ones DF can act on.
  • The cap table spanned classes some layers can't hold, so no web ever finished on its own.

Nine fixes, then the guild-first rebuild, closed those. The temperate lake has no apex in about half its rosters; v2.2 filled it with fishing land predators, which are off by default since FSH2, so that gap is open again. The full history of each round, with your rulings, is in the appendix.
