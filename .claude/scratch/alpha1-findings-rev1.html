Seasonal Wildlife · alpha trial one · digest and repair plan

Alpha One Findings

The first alpha trial of seasonal-wildlife v6.2.1 marked 22 of 46 steps as anomalies. Traced to their causes, they come down to five root problems and ten workstreams. Three of the root problems are in the tool and two are in our own validation harness, which passed 148 claims while missing most of what a player hits in the first hour. Every finding below comes from the trial fort itself: its ledger and saved configuration, DFHack's error log, live timings on the running game, and the vanilla creature raws.

Fort Boatsbowed (tropical shrubland · tropical ocean · saltwater river · 30 dwarves) Build DF 53.16 · DFHack 53.16-r1.1 · plugin v6.2.1 (deployed files byte-identical to the repository) Tester tally 21 pass · 22 anomaly · 1 skipped · 2 unmarked Fort ledger 219 lines: 146 edits, 36 arrivals, 7 placements, 1 fuse trip

Five root causes

Most anomalies are symptoms. These five causes explain all but a handful of the 22 on their own; the workstreams further down fix them one at a time.

R1 · critical

Blocking a species does not block it unless it has seasons

The roster only manages species with at least one season assigned. Matrix assign clears seasons on everything it does not allow, so a blocked species is usually unseasoned and left entirely to DF. On this fort 63 blocked species still hold their full worldgen stock: cavies 5,191, giant rhinoceros 5,203, giant jackal 2,204, dingo 3,412, lion 1,602, every shark. Every water and cavern species also has no seasons, so those two layers are not managed at all.

Explains 4.2 (blocked rhinos and jackals arrived), 11.3 (arrivals the tool did not send), 7.2 (the pile-up), 11.1 (the "—" season question), 1.4 (no cavern species in the roster).

R2 · high

One flag decides a species' whole identity

Category is a single choice made from one raw flag at a time: a CARNIVORE flag makes a predator, a BENIGN flag makes prey, and nothing else is looked at. So a Giant Orca, BENIGN in the raws, is prey; a bird cannot also be a predator; a dingo carrying DF's LARGE_PREDATOR behaviour flag is filed as "large". "Aquatic" is set by any water-breathing flag or by a biome list that is all water, which puts otters, hippos, albatrosses and eleven night creatures under aquatic, and crabs and squid under "other". The diet model then has no habitat check, so lions eat sharks.

Explains 2.1, 5.2 (land and water eating each other), 1.4 (Roster's water view), 11.2 (the otter in the ocean).

R3 · high

The roster tools cascade through the whole web

Co-align and the Y/D fills update partners inside the same loop they read from, so each creature sees the sets the ones before it just widened. In one pass the change spreads across the entire food web: every species ends up in every season (co-align) or allowed (fills). With R2's unchecked diet, the web is dense enough that "partner" means nearly everyone.

Explains 3.2, 3.4, and most of why 3.1's matrix seasons do not survive.

R4 · high

Hot loops allocate on a 54 MB Lua heap

Six loops over the fort's 7,606 population entries build a throwaway one-key table per entry. On DFHack's 54 MB Lua heap each allocation triggers garbage-collection work: the same loop costs 1.5 s with the per-entry table and 0–33 ms without. That is the Live tab's 3.4–4.3 s refresh and much of the groups job's 0.3–1.5 s stalls. Reading the configuration costs 65 ms a call and every job reads it. The ecology job overran by 2.8 s on day 79 and the fuse cancelled it, silently: ecology has been off since and nothing in the window says so.

Explains 1.1 (hangs, CPU doubling), 5.2 and 6.1 (slow tabs), part of 12.2.

R5 · ours

The validator could not see what the player sees

It injects keys straight into the window, bypassing DF's own global bindings, so Ctrl+R and Ctrl+L, DF's record- and load-macro keys, passed. It enabled the map overlay to test it and never turned it off, so the tester's fort had "graphical bugs" from day one. It recorded "a species with no seasons is not managed at all" as a PASS, enshrining R1. It checks text by bytes, so seventeen em dashes that DF draws as ΓÇö, the tester's "Cyrillic RCo", passed as well.

Explains 3.5, 4.4, 3.6, 6.2/6.3/10.1, and why none of R1–R4 surfaced in 176 claims.

Ten workstreams

Each workstream groups the anomalies that share a cause, with the evidence, the fix, and the test that proves it. W0 is the harness; it goes first, because every later fix needs a validator that can see it.

W0

Make the validator see what a player sees

first

Steps 3.5, 3.6, 4.4, 6.2, 6.3, 10.1 · root R5

Evidence

  • DF's interface.txt binds Ctrl+R RECORD_MACRO, Ctrl+L LOAD_MACRO, Ctrl+P PLAY_MACRO and Ctrl+S SAVE_MACRO; DF acts on these before any DFHack screen.
  • overlay.json kept seasonal-wildlife.groups enabled from validation until the tester switched it off; the widget's own default is off.
  • The claim mech.unassigned ("not managed at all") is recorded PASS.
  • Seventeen UTF-8 em dashes in displayed strings: the Dry-run title, the Food web headings, the water and cavern status lines.

Fix

  • A static check of every window key against DF's interface.txt and DFHack's global keybindings; any collision fails the run.
  • Snapshot and restore every persisted DFHack setting the run touches (overlays first).
  • Replace mech.unassigned with claims that a blocked species, seasoned or not, on every layer, ends with zero stock.
  • A lint that fails any non-ASCII character in a displayed string.
  • Timing budgets as claims: each tab's refresh and each job's tick measured and bounded.

Done when

Run against today's v6.2.1, the new checks fail on the two macro keys, the em dashes, the blocked-but-unseasoned species and the Live tab's time. A validator that passes the current build has not learned anything.

W1

Block means block, on every layer

critical

Steps 4.2, 7.2, 11.1, 11.3, 3.3, 3.7, 4.4 · root R1

Cause

Two functions build the set of species to manage from season assignments alone: the season-boundary apply and the daily out-of-season hold. Anything without seasons is skipped. Blocking works only by accident, when the species still has a season. Separately, the Default view filters on "in this embark", exactly like Current, so the two lists are identical by construction. Reset restores the stock of species in the first-run snapshot, which leaves added species and roster choices alone. That matches its own promise but not the name "Reset to default".

Fix

  • Manage every roster species. Inactive (blocked) is zero stock, always. Active with no seasons means "all year", shown as All, so the "—" state disappears.
  • In the caverns, stock is not the lever (T7: closing 109 entries stopped no cavern wave); inactive cavern species are held at frequency 1, the lever the cavern ceiling already uses.
  • Default view shows the first-run snapshot: which species the embark had, at what stock, before any edit.
  • Reset becomes two actions behind one confirmation: restore stock (today's behaviour) and restore the embark (remove added species, re-apply the preset).
  • F leaves a category alone unless its target is set, so apex species are no longer blocked by a default target of 0.

Done when

On this fort, after the fix and one day of play: all 63 blocked species show zero stock, no inactive species arrives over a season, and the 46 active-but-unseasoned water and cavern species are managed. Validated by the new W0 claims and a two-replicate experiment on a copy of Boatsbowed.

W2

A species model built from the raws

high

Steps 2.1, 1.4, 5.2, 11.2 · root R2

Evidence

  • DF 53 stores the raws' [AQUATIC] as cannot breathe air, [AMPHIBIOUS] as can breathe water, [UNDERSWIM] as AQUATIC_UNDERSWIM. Tiles carry a water_salt bit. The raws name each creature's water: OCEAN, and RIVER, LAKE and POOL each as fresh, brackish or salt.
  • Big BENIGN swimmers read as prey: GIANT_ORCA, ORCA, ORCA_MAN, SHARK_WHALE, FISH_RAY_MANTA, FISH_SUNFISH_OCEAN.
  • 45 species fall in "other", sixteen of them aquatic: crabs, horseshoe crabs, nautilus, cuttlefish, ammonite, gigantic squid.
  • 28 species are "aquatic" without swimming underwater: three albatrosses (fliers), eleven night creatures, otters, hippos, pond grabbers.
  • "Large" for a predator is DF's LARGE_PREDATOR behaviour flag: a dingo (20,000 cm³) and a frill shark (60,000) are large; a monitor lizard (100,000) is large by the 5,000 threshold.
  • Ocean fish not on the embark are filed on the land layer, which is why the Roster's Water view shows almost nothing.

Fix

  • Two independent axes. Habitat: aquatic (cannot breathe air, or cannot move on land), amphibious (can breathe water and air), flier, land; plus the cavern and deep layers. Role: predator, prey, grazer, scavenger. A bird can be a predator: an eagle is a flier and a predator.
  • Size by adult body volume in bands, independent of DF's behaviour flags; LARGE_PREDATOR is kept, shown as "aggressive".
  • A curated vanilla table for the raws' gaps (orcas, whale shark, octopus, squid, crabs), each line citing its raw, and a per-species role and habitat override on the species detail page.
  • Water body and salinity per species from its biome tags, used by the diet model (W3) and placement (W8).
  • Every creature is filed on the layer its habitat belongs to, whether or not the embark has it.

Done when

A generated audit table of all 767 vanilla creatures, raw tags beside the tool's reading, has no row the tester flagged left wrong. It becomes a validator fixture, so a later change that misfiles an orca fails the run.

W3

Season shaping and the food web

high

Steps 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 5.2 · root R3

Cause

Co-align walks the pool and extends every partner of every creature to all of its seasons, writing as it goes. Fill natural prey and predators allow partners inside the same walk, so newly allowed species pull in their own partners. The diet test checks mass and layer but not habitat, and lets any bird eat any smaller aquatic creature.

Fix

  • Diet requires compatible habitats: land hunts land and amphibious; aquatic hunts aquatic; amphibious and piscivorous birds bridge the shore. Every cross-habitat edge carries its reason.
  • Co-align reads a snapshot and solves a small problem per season and ecosystem: every predator has a prey and every prey a predator. It extends a species by at most one adjacent season, and reports what it could not pair instead of widening further. This is the tester's proposal.
  • Y and D add only direct partners of species active before the key, and only where a predator has no prey (or a prey no predator); they list what they added.
  • Matrix assign splits each habitat group four ways by climate lean, one quarter per season, with at least one predator–prey pair per layer per season. This is the tester's proposal.
  • Targets per layer; F fills only layers below target.
  • Food web: N hidden in By-season (it shows all four); chains marked in every mode.

Done when

On this fort after the preset: no season holds more than half the roster, co-align changes fewer than 20 sets and leaves no species in all four seasons unless it began there, Y adds a number you could count by hand, and no land predator lists an aquatic prey.

W4

Speed and silent failures

high

Steps 1.1, 5.2, 6.1, 12.2 · root R4

Fix

  • Build the layer set once, outside the loop, at all six sites: the Live tab, the cavern ceiling, coupling, the water and cavern stocking passes, and the dawn table.
  • Hold the configuration in memory and write it back when changed, instead of decoding it on every call.
  • Classify the 1,120 raws once per loaded world, not on every pool rebuild.
  • Ecology writes only pairs that are new since the last pass, in bounded batches; 1,611 pairs at once is what tripped the fuse.
  • A tripped fuse shows as a banner on every tab, naming the job and offering re-arm.

Done when

Every tab refreshes in under 100 ms on this fort; the groups job and the ecology job stay under 50 ms per tick across a season; no fuse trips. All of it is measured by the validator's new timing claims. The tester's frame-rate comparison is repeated on the Jobs tab (W9).

W6

Seeing what is on the map

medium

Steps 4.2, 6.1, 6.2, 6.3, 7.2, 9.2, 11.3, 2.5

Evidence

  • 90 wild units on the map at inspection; the Live tab tracks 27, in 16 groups. The rest are deep-layer demons and magma crabs, cavern residents, three elephants the tool never tracked, and the five tool-placed water animals.
  • Ctrl+F runs DFHack's fix/wildlife, which removes only animals stuck while trying to leave, then asks DF for a new wave. Nothing sends the current group away, which is the pile-up.
  • The overlay draws a group marker at its screen position on every z-level, which is the g1 the tester saw on every level below ground.
  • The undo ring is ten deep by design and was full (10 of 10).

Fix

  • Live lists every wild unit on the map by origin: DF wave (tracked), tool-placed, resident since embark, born here, deep layer. Tracked groups become one section of it, not all of it.
  • The Ledger records every arrival on every layer, including DF's own and the force action.
  • Force sends the current group off (countdowns zeroed) before calling the next; a call this species action on the species detail page, as the tester asked.
  • Overlay markers only on the viewed level.
  • Undo 50 deep, the depth shown, and a list of what each step would undo.
  • Live redesigned around the question "what is here and why".

Done when

Every wild unit on the map appears on the Live tab with an origin; Force leaves at most one arriving group on the map; the overlay draws nothing on levels its groups are not on.

W8

Water and caverns

medium

Steps 7.1, 7.2, 7.4, 11.2

Evidence

  • The first-run defaults leave the water and cavern layers off, so the preset seasons neither; turning them on later adds unseasoned, unmanaged species.
  • reveal all unhid the caverns, but DF's discovered flag stayed false on all three and its first-cavern announcement never fired. The tool counts a cavern as found only by that flag.
  • The water job is working: 7 animals placed, 5 on the map, target 12. It places at any wet edge tile, so a river otter entry went into the ocean. DF never draws river or lake entries during play (measured 17 September); ocean entries arrive only when the map edge is wet.

Answers to the tester's questions

These are the answers the in-window help pages (W7) will carry, written against the code as it stands today.

What is the "Cyrillic RCo" text?

An em dash. The tool writes it in UTF-8, and DF draws text in its own 256-character set, so the three bytes appear as ΓÇö. It sits in the Dry-run title, the Food web headings and the water and cavern status lines. W5 replaces all seventeen with ASCII, and W0 fails any that come back.

What is the difference between Matrix and Co-align?

Matrix gives each active species seasons from its climate range: the coldest third of your roster autumn and winter, the warmest third spring and summer, the middle all year. Co-align then widens seasons so predators and their prey overlap. Today co-align widens without limit, which is why everything ends up in every season (W3).

Can a bird also be a predator or prey?

Not today: category is one choice. W2 splits it into habitat and role, so an eagle is a flier and a predator, and a parakeet a flier and prey.

Does abundance come from a shared pool across layers, or per layer?

Neither. Abundance sets each population entry's stock to 100 × abundance ÷ 50, so 50 means a stock of 100. DF's choice of which species arrives ignores stock (measured 17 September); stock only matters when it reaches zero, which stops the species, and it caps how many can come before the entry runs out. So abundance does not change proportions at all. W7 renames it stock, and adds an odds lever on the raw's frequency showing each species' share of its layer.

A creature shows "—" for seasons and Y for ok. When does it appear?

Whenever DF draws it. With no seasons the tool does not manage it at all, so it keeps its worldgen stock all year. That is also true when it is blocked, which is the root of the blocked arrivals (R1). After W1, active with no seasons means all year, shown as All, and inactive means never.

Are creatures being spawned by the game rather than the tool?

Yes, and all land and cavern arrivals always are. The tool never creates land or cavern animals: it sets which entries DF may draw from and holds or releases DF's gate. The only animals it creates are water placements (7 on this fort) and cavern stocking (off). The pile-up is DF drawing 63 species the tool believed were blocked, plus Force calling new groups without sending the current one off, plus eleven rhinos held for thirty days in step 6.3.

What is a quota, and what is stocking?

A quota is a per-layer ceiling: land, how many tracked groups at once; water, how many animals the water job keeps on the map; caverns, how many wild units the caverns may hold. 0 means unset. Stocking is the tool placing animals itself, because DF never draws river or lake entries during play and rarely fills caverns: the water job places up to its target, and cavern stocking (off by default) fills up to the cavern quota.

Why don't all layers have the same options?

Because DF gives each layer a different lever. The surface is steered by stock and the arrival gate. Water only by placement, since DF never draws river or lake animals in play. Caverns only by the raw's frequency, since closing a cavern entry stopped no cavern wave (109 entries held at zero, thirty arrivals anyway). W7 greys the cells that do not apply and says why.

What do ecology and coupling do? Is nudge per layer?

Ecology writes DF's own predator–prey relation between wild animals on the map, so a predator near prey attacks it; with it off they meet by chance. It also nudges any surface predator that has stayed more than 40 tiles from all prey for 3,000 ticks to within 6 tiles of the nearest. Nudge is one policy for surface predators: the "radius 6" under the Water column is a layout bug (W8). Coupling: when a prey group arrives, the tool briefly opens only that prey's natural predators so DF's next wave is one of them, then restores the roster. The jackals following the rhinos in step 6.4 were coupling. On this fort ecology has been off since day 79, when its fuse tripped (W4).

If pack size is set to a number, is the group always exactly that size?

No. "Raw" leaves the creature's own range (a dingo's is 3–12). A number writes that range as N to N, and DF then draws N and sometimes N + 1: ravens and emus both drew three under a written maximum of two, about one wave in six, never four (E42b). It applies to the predator waves coupling opens.

What does "armed" mean for irruptions?

The group's members get DF's agitated-wilderness flag, the one that makes wildlife attack what it meets, for ten days, then it is cleared. It cannot aim: an armed group is dangerous to dwarves working in that cavern, and does not march on the fort. Pressure only rises with citizens in a cavern's depth band, which is why the tester's three pressures read 0 with no dwarves underground.

What does "the cavern gate takes steady until v6.1" mean?

Nothing any more. It is a message from before v6.1 shipped, and it is wrong in v6.2.1; W7 removes it.

Which water can a river otter live in?

Its raw lists any pool, lake or river, of any salinity, but no ocean. The tool placed it at an ocean edge because placement ignores the water body. W8 matches ocean, river or lake and salt, brackish or fresh.

Why is undo limited?

The undo ring holds ten edits by design, of any kind, and the trial filled it. W6 makes it fifty deep and shows the depth.

What were the g1 and r11 marks and the red dashes?

The map overlay: g/r plus a count marks a gated or resident group, and the red dashes are predator–prey links. It is off by default; our validator had left it on. It also drew markers on every z-level, which W6 fixes.
