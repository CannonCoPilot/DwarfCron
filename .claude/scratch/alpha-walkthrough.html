    Before you open the window

    The trial runs on a fort you can afford to lose and a game that shows its frame rate, because two of the questions are "did anything persist wrongly" and "what does the tool cost".

    Use a backup. The tool writes to the map's wildlife populations and to persistent site data. Copy the save folder first, or embark a throwaway fort. A fort that never opens the window is never touched, so the untouched copy is your control.
    MarkUse it when
    PassWhat the step promises is what you saw, receipt and all.
    AnomalyAnything else: a wrong value, a missing receipt, a clipped column, a key that did nothing, a design choice that made you stop and think, a frame-rate dip you noticed. Write what you pressed, what you saw, what you expected. Screenshot names help.
    SkippedThe fort cannot show it (no water at the map edge, no cavern found yet, no season boundary reached). Say why in the note.
    0.1

    Choose the fort

    DoPick or embark a fort with wildlife on the surface and, ideally, a river, lake or ocean edge and a breached cavern. A seven-dwarf fort a season or two old is ideal: little happens on it but wildlife.
    ExpectNothing yet. Note the embark's biomes and whether the map edge holds water and whether a cavern has been discovered, because the Layers tab will tell you what the tool thinks of both.
    WatchRecord the fort's population, year and season here so later steps have a baseline.
    0.2

    Turn on the frame counter

    DoIn the game's Settings, turn on the FPS display, then read the frame rate while the game is unpaused for a minute at your usual speed.
    ExpectA baseline number to compare against in phase 12. A paused game reports a meaningless high figure, so read it running.
    WatchTwo identical loads of the same fort can differ by ten to fifteen percent on their own, so a single reading is a rough guide, not a measurement.
    0.3

    Confirm the install

    DoOpen the DFHack launcher with \ and start typing gui/seasonal.
    ExpectThe launcher autocompletes gui/seasonal-wildlife. Do not run it yet.
    WatchIf it is missing, both scripts are not in dfhack-config/scripts (one at the top, one under gui/). This is the one launcher command the trial needs.
    1

    First open and the window itself

    The first run of the window on a fort applies a biome preset and writes it to the Ledger. Everything you see afterwards rests on that, so look at it before touching anything.

    Needs: a loaded fort, paused.

    1.1

    Open the window

    DoLauncher \, run gui/seasonal-wildlife.
    ExpectA resizable window titled Seasonal Wildlife opens on the Overview tab: the date and the four switches (tool, groups, coupling, ecology), the pattern per layer, what each layer holds now, in-season counts, the next boundary with who arrives and leaves, and the Ledger's last five lines.
    WatchAny text clipped at the right edge or a row cut off at the bottom; a tab bar with fewer than eight tabs (Overview, Roster, Seasons, Food web, Live, Layers, Vermin, Ledger).
    1.2

    Read the first-run preset

    DoGo to the Ledger tab.
    ExpectA build line naming the preset after your embark's biomes (temperate shrubland, say): every in-embark natural species allowed, seasons from the climate matrix, predators co-aligned with their prey, vermin families on their seasonal defaults, the land gate steady.
    WatchA preset name that does not match your biomes; no build line at all on a fresh fort; more than one build line on a fort you only just opened.
    1.3

    Resize and close

    DoDrag the window's corner to make it wider and taller, then narrower than it opened; press Esc.
    ExpectColumns re-flow with the width; the window closes on Esc and reopens in the same state with the launcher.
    WatchColumns that overlap when narrow; a status line hidden below the fold; the window remembering a size it should not.
    1.4

    Cycle the layer selector

    DoReopen. Press Alt+L four times, on the Overview and then on the Roster.
    ExpectThe title cycles All layers → Land → Water → Cavern and names the reason when a layer is off or dormant, for example Water (dormant: no open water at the map edge near the surface) or Cavern (2 found). The Roster's rows follow the layer; the Overview keeps every layer.
    WatchA reason that contradicts the map (water at the edge but "dormant"; a discovered cavern reported as not found). Which tabs follow the selector and which do not.
    2

    Roster: filters, one creature, and its detail

    The Roster is where you decide who may visit and when. Nothing changes until you press an action, and every action leaves a receipt on the status line under the keys and a line in the Ledger.

    Needs: the Roster tab. Since v5.11.0 the old Set roster tab's keys live here.

    2.1

    Filters

    DoPress V three times, C through every category, B through the biomes, N through the seasons.
    ExpectV cycles Current → Default → Add-new; C includes an aquatic category; the header's per-category counts and the list change with each filter.
    WatchA creature in a category you would not put it in (a bird that is not a "bird", a fish under vermin). Note the species; the category only affects this tool's matrix, never the game, but it is a design question.
    2.2

    Search takes focus only when asked

    DoType a few letters with no box focused, then Alt+S, type part of a name, Enter, then type letters again.
    ExpectPlain letters before Alt+S are actions, not search text; after Alt+S the box filters by name; Enter or Esc gives focus back so letters are actions again.
    WatchLetters landing in the search box when it should not have focus, or an action firing while you meant to type. This is the single most likely place for a key to go astray.
    2.3

    Allow, block, and the why column

    DoSelect a creature; press Enter twice. Try a click on another row.
    ExpectThe ok column flips Y → - → Y; the why column says you with the date; the status line confirms each flip; the Ledger gains an edit line each time.
    WatchA locked species (megabeasts, night creatures) should refuse and say why. A species the preset allowed should read preset: … in why until you touch it.
    2.4

    Seasons on one creature

    DoOn an allowed row: Shift+Enter six times, then S U A W one at a time.
    ExpectShift+Enter cycles the SEASON column none → Sp → Su → Au → Wi → all → none; S/U/A/W toggle single seasons. Each is one edit line.
    WatchA season toggle that also changes another row; the column not redrawing until you move the cursor.
    2.5

    Species detail

    DoPress i on a row you know, read it, press Esc.
    ExpectA detail page over the window: what it is, body, embark presence, allowed and why (the full reason), abundance, seasons, eats, eaten by, on the map now, history. Esc returns to the Roster with your row still selected.
    WatchAn "eats" or "eaten by" list that names something implausible; a history that omits an edit you just made; a detail that does not fit the window at its opening size.
    2.6

    Abundance

    DoCtrl+W on a row, enter a number; then filter to one category and Ctrl+G, enter another.
    ExpectThe ab column shows the value for the row, then for every filtered row; the status line counts what it set (Set N abundances to 60.).
    WatchWhat happens with 0, a blank, or a very large number. Whether the prompt can be cancelled cleanly.
    3

    Roster: the whole-roster tools

    These act on many creatures at once from the climate matrix, the food web and per-category targets. Each promises to only add, or to say exactly what it changed.

    3.1

    Matrix assign

    DoPress M. Then open the Seasons tab and come back.
    ExpectStatus: Assigned matrix seasons to N creatures. The coldest third of your allowed species take autumn and winter, the warmest third spring and summer, the middle stay all year. It overwrites hand-set seasons.
    WatchA species whose seasons make no sense for its climate (a tundra animal in summer only). Note it; the cut is relative to your own pool, so edge cases are expected but worth listing.
    3.2

    Co-align predators with prey

    DoPress O.
    ExpectEach creature's seasons extend onto its allowed food-web partners so predator and prey share the map; the status line counts the changes.
    WatchA predator gaining a season with no prey in it, or seasons only ever widening (they should never narrow).
    3.3

    Targets and fill

    DoCycle Shift+P, Shift+R, Shift+B, Shift+V a few times each, then F.
    ExpectThe targets row at the foot changes for prey / predator / bird / vermin; the header shows a Thin: hint for any category under its target; F allows or blocks members at random to hit the targets, preferring native creatures, and reports what it did.
    WatchFill blocking something you allowed by hand a moment ago (it may, by design: note whether that felt right). A Thin hint that stays after a fill.
    3.4

    Fill natural prey and predators

    DoBlock a few prey animals, then press Y; block a predator, press D.
    ExpectY allows the natural prey of your allowed creatures, D their natural predators; both only ever add; the allowed counts in the header never decrease.
    WatchAny count going down after Y or D is a defect.
    3.5

    Fill one category to N

    DoFilter C to one category, press Ctrl+L, enter a count. Then filter to all and press it again.
    ExpectThe category is random-filled to your number; on all or aquatic it refuses and says so.
    WatchA refusal with no message; a count above the category's size.
    3.6

    Dry-run

    DoPress Ctrl+D, read it, close it.
    ExpectA table of every allowed creature against the four seasons, nothing written.
    WatchWhether the table matches the Seasons tab exactly. A Ledger line appearing from a dry run would be a defect.
    3.7

    Add a non-native species

    DoV to the Add-new view, select a creature not in your embark, Ctrl+X. Then try it from the Current view.
    ExpectAdded to the roster from Add-new with a receipt; from any other view the status line says it only works in Add-new. Megabeasts, species at the innumerable sentinel and species already present are refused with a reason.
    WatchAn addition with no receipt; a species that then never appears in the Current view.
    4

    Roster: apply, force, rotate

    The three actions that touch the live map. DF allows one wild group on the surface at a time, so a wave is something you watch for at the map edge after unpausing.

    Needs: the map visible beside the window; a few minutes unpaused.

    4.1

    Apply now

    DoPress Ctrl+A.
    ExpectStatus: Applied <Season> live: N active. An announcement in the game's log. A season line in the Ledger.
    WatchThe map looks the same at first: the current wild group must leave before DF draws from the new roster. That is the game, not a defect.
    4.2

    Force a wave

    DoPress Ctrl+F, close the window, unpause, watch the map edge for a few minutes of game time. Reopen and check the Live tab.
    ExpectThe current wild group is cleared and a new one arrives; the Live tab lists it as a tracked group with its arrival day; the Ledger has a wave line.
    WatchWhich species arrived and whether it is in season on the Seasons tab. How long the wave took (write it down: on the test fort waves land from a few hundred ticks to, rarely, ten days later). A wave of a size that surprised you.
    4.3

    Automatic rotation

    DoPress Ctrl+E; read the Overview's switches; press it again; leave it on for phase 10.
    ExpectStatus: Auto rotation ON. then OFF; the Overview's tool switch follows.
    WatchThe switch and the status disagreeing.
    4.4

    Reset to default (last, on purpose)

    DoPress Ctrl+R. Read the confirmation. Cancel it. Press again and confirm.
    ExpectA confirmation before anything happens; cancel leaves everything as it was; confirm restores worldgen quantities and clears abundances, with a receipt. Your allow/block choices and seasons stay.
    WatchAnything lost that the promise says stays. After this step, re-run M and O so later phases have a full roster.
    5

    Seasons and Food web

    Two planning views. The Seasons tab shows what the roster will do at each boundary; the Food web shows which species can be on the map together and, in Graph mode, which pairs the game itself holds as predator and prey right now.

    5.1

    Seasons tab

    DoOpen Seasons. Find a creature you gave two seasons in 2.4.
    ExpectFour seasons side by side, one row per allowed creature under its trophic level; + the arrival season, - the last season present, X present, . absent. Your creature's marks match its SEASON column.
    WatchA creature with all four seasons showing a + or - anywhere; a blocked creature listed.
    5.2

    Food web modes

    DoOpen Food web. Press G four times; at each mode press N through the seasons; press L.
    ExpectPyramid: chains under All, a trophic pyramid with an aquatic mini-web for one season. Graph: every predator → prey edge, with the pairs DF holds right now drawn as ==> and counted. By season: four columns with a prey-to-predator mass ratio each. By layer: the enabled layers side by side with bridge edges. L jumps straight to By layer.
    WatchAn edge between two species you would never pair; a "related" count that changes without anything moving; text running past the window in By layer.
    6

    Live: the map this instant

    Tracked groups, wild units by race and layer, the biomass ratio, and the herds and packs lever. The group keys act on the selected row; the rest act without one.

    Needs: at least one tracked group on the map (phase 4.2 gave you one).

    6.1

    Read the tab

    DoOpen Live; press R.
    ExpectThe status lines first, then group rows (TOKEN xN gated/resident (day D)), the Herds & packs block with each species' label (flock / pack / herd) and reason, wild-on-map by race, the quota line, the cavern line, and a prey:predator biomass ratio coloured green at 8:1 or better, yellow to 3:1, red below.
    WatchA group row whose count disagrees with what you can see on the map; a ratio colour that does not match its number.
    6.2

    Centre and follow

    DoSelect a group row, press Enter; then F, close the window, watch, press Esc in the map view.
    ExpectEnter moves the viewport to the group; F makes the camera follow a member the way gui/sitemap does, and Esc on the map stops it.
    WatchFollowing a member that then leaves the map; the camera jumping between members.
    6.3

    Hold and dismiss

    DoWith a row selected, Q; note the row. Select another (or the same later), X. Unpause and watch for a while.
    ExpectQ: the row says held N more days (every member's leave countdown raised to thirty days); the group stays. X: the row says dismissed; the animals walk off on their own path and the population entry is refunded when they leave. Each is a hold or dismiss Ledger line.
    WatchA held group leaving early; a dismissed group that never leaves (note how long you watched).
    6.4

    Groups, coupling, pack size, next wave

    DoWith no row selected: G twice, P twice, K six times, then W.
    ExpectG toggles resident groups (the status and the Overview switch follow); P flips migratory coupling; K cycles the pack size raw → 3 → 5 → 8 → 12 → raw and the Herds block shows the override; W forces the next wave, the same as the Roster's Ctrl+F.
    WatchA key acting on a row when you meant the tab, or the reverse. The pack override not appearing in the Herds block.
    7

    Layers: the settings table

    One column per layer, one row per setting, and the lines that say what the tool found: whether the map edge holds water and whether a cavern has been discovered. This is also where the biome preset can be re-applied and where irruptions are switched on.

    7.1

    Water and caverns as the tool sees them

    DoOpen Layers. Read the water line and the cavern lines.
    ExpectWater: live, or dormant with the reason the wet-edge survey gave. Caverns: found with its groups on the map, or not yet found, and nothing more shown until your dwarves reach it.
    WatchThe two things that only you can check against the map: is the water verdict right for your edge, and does "found" match what your dwarves have actually breached?
    7.2

    Pick a column and switch a layer

    DoL to the water column (or cavern if you have one), O twice.
    ExpectThe layer switches off and on; the Overview's per-layer block and the layer selector's title reason follow.
    WatchSwitching a dormant layer on: does it say it stays dormant, and why?
    7.3

    Pattern, quota, groups, gap

    DoL to land. T five times; Q and enter a quota, then 0; C and enter a count; D and enter min and max days.
    ExpectT cycles steady → burst → trickle → dawn → follow and back; Q 0 means unset; C is the gate's concurrent groups (land: max at once; cavern: cavern max); D the land gap in days. The Overview shows the pattern per layer. Leave the pattern on dawn for phase 10.
    WatchA prompt that accepts min greater than max; a pattern the cavern column refuses (it should take steady only until irruptions are on).
    7.4

    Ecology, nudge, coupling, pack

    DoE twice, N and read the three numbers it asks for (tiles, ticks, radius), P twice, K a few times.
    ExpectThe ecology switch flips (the Food web's Graph mode and the Overview follow); the nudge policy is the "more than N tiles from all prey for N ticks, to within N" rule; P and K are the same coupling and pack levers as the Live tab and stay in step with it.
    WatchThe Live tab and the Layers tab disagreeing about coupling or pack after you change one of them.
    7.5

    Irruptions (needs a cavern)

    DoWith the cavern column selected, press I. Read the cavern pressure row. Press I again to turn it off unless you want phase 10 to include it.
    ExpectIrruptions on: the pressure row shows three pressures (one per cavern depth) that rise with citizens working in that cavern's z-band and decay with time; at the threshold the next cavern group the roster admits is armed for ten days, then stood down; one at a time, then a cooldown. Off clears any armed flag at once. On a fort with no cavern found, it should say it needs the cavern layer.
    WatchPressure rising with no dwarf underground; an armed group attacking anything other than dwarves working that cavern. The module cannot aim: it makes what arrives lethal to what it meets. Every step is an irruption Ledger line.
    7.6

    Re-apply the preset

    DoPress R. Check the Ledger, then the Roster's why column.
    ExpectThe biome preset is applied over your changes: a new build line, and species it allowed read preset: … again in why. Your roster from phases 2 to 4 is overwritten, which is why this comes last.
    WatchSettings that survived the preset when the promise says they are overwritten, or the reverse.
    8

    Vermin

    Vermin are in the roster as families read from the raws, one row each, with seasonal defaults that depend on how cold your embark is.

    8.1

    Families and their defaults

    DoOpen Vermin. Select a family; Enter twice; S U A W; Shift+W with a number; then D.
    ExpectRows for fish, flies, fliers, mammals, soil, colony, crawlers with species count, allowed count, season set (or mixed), abundance (or mixed) and layers. Enter blocks or allows the whole family; S/U/A/W toggle a season for it; Shift+W sets its abundance; D applies the defaults (land insects spring to autumn, or summer to autumn on a cold embark; mammals, fish and anything underground or in water all year) and the status line says so. Each family action is one Ledger line.
    WatchA small bird listed as vermin (they should be birds); a default that does not fit your embark's cold or warmth.
    9

    Ledger and undo

    Every write the tool made and why, coloured by kind, newest last, three hundred entries deep. Undo restores the state before your last edit from a ten-deep ring; the automatic jobs never push onto it.

    9.1

    Read and filter

    DoOpen Ledger. K through the kinds; L through the layers; R.
    ExpectThe header says kind=… as you cycle edit, season, wave, arrive, coupling, ecology, hold, dismiss, sweep, place, cavern, fuse, build, undo, irruption; the layer filter narrows the same way; you can find every action you took in phases 2 to 8 as a line, each saying why.
    WatchAn action of yours with no line; a line you cannot understand without the source. Write down the exact text of any line that reads as jargon: "every automatic decision logs one readable line" is a promise about wording.
    9.2

    Undo

    DoOn the Roster, block a species you can find again. Back on the Ledger, press Z. Check the Roster. Press Z several more times.
    ExpectThe species is allowed again and the Ledger gains an undo line; further presses step back through your last ten edits (fills, matrix, quotas, patterns, presets included) and then say there is nothing left. The worldgen snapshot is never undone.
    WatchAn undo that reverts something an automatic job did; an undo that reverts two edits at once.
    10

    The map overlay

    The overlay marks each tracked group beside its first member and draws the predator and prey links the game holds on the level you are looking at, the same pairs the ecology job writes.

    Needs: DFHack's control panel, a UI.

    10.1

    Enable it and look

    DoLauncher \, run gui/control-panel, open its Overlays tab, enable seasonal-wildlife.groups. Close the panel and look at a tracked group on the map, then at a level with a predator and prey both present.
    ExpectA marker beside each tracked group's first member (g4 gated, r4 resident) and a dotted red line between every related predator and prey pair on the viewed level, up to sixty pairs a frame.
    WatchMarkers that drift from their group; lines to animals on another level; any frame-rate change with the overlay on versus off. Note: the marker also paints beside the tool's own window when it is open over the map.
    11

    Across a season boundary

    The tool's reason to exist. With rotation on, the boundary applies the next season's roster, and the patterns you set decide the shape of the arrivals that follow. This phase takes real play time.

    Needs: rotation on (4.3), a pattern chosen (7.3), a fort played up to and past a season boundary. Play at your normal speed; check the window every few days of game time.

    What the measurements say to expect, so you can tell the game's own variance from a defect. Waves on the test fort came nine to twelve days apart at the median. Dawn: at the boundary the gate opens and only birds and vermin are open for the first week; a bird wave landed inside that week in six of eight releases, and twice the wave waited ten days or more with nothing wrong. Trickle: draws sized one or two, and about one wave in six runs one over to three, for any bird. Burst: waves close together, then a long quiet gap. Steady: the plain gate. Follow: predators after prey.
    11.1

    Before the boundary

    DoA few days before the season changes, open the Overview. Read the next boundary block.
    ExpectIt names the next season, who arrives and who leaves, matching the Seasons tab's + and - marks.
    WatchA creature listed as arriving that is blocked; the date being off by a season.
    11.2

    The boundary itself

    DoPlay through the change of season with the window closed. Then open the Ledger and the Overview.
    ExpectAn announcement <Season> wildlife roster applied. in the game's log; a season line in the Ledger; with dawn set, a wave line saying the week's table is set and the gate opens; the Overview's switches unchanged.
    WatchThe announcement missing; the roster applied twice; a pause or stutter you noticed at the boundary (write the frame rate down).
    11.3

    The first two weeks

    DoPlay two weeks of game time. Each time a wave lands, open the Live tab and the Ledger.
    ExpectEvery arriving group is tracked with its day; every wave has a Ledger line saying which pattern shaped it and when the gate reopens; with dawn, the first week's wave is birds. Species that left are out of season on the Seasons tab.
    WatchAn out-of-season species arriving; a wave the Ledger did not record; a group tracked long after it left; the surface going quiet for more than two weeks (note what arrived underground meanwhile).
    11.4

    Change the pattern and repeat

    DoSet T on Layers to trickle, play another two weeks; then burst.
    ExpectTrickle: small draws of one or two, occasionally three; the Ledger says draws sized 1-2 standing on N species. Burst: a cluster of waves, then quiet.
    WatchA trickle draw of four or more; a burst that never bursts. Count wave sizes as you go, they are the data.
    12

    Persistence and cost

    Two questions no single session answers: does everything survive a save and reload, and what does the tool cost while its jobs run.

    12.1

    Save, quit, reload

    DoNote three things: a species you blocked, the pattern per layer, the Ledger's last line. Save the game, return to the title, load it again, open the window.
    ExpectAll three are as you left them; the undo ring still works; no second preset build line appears.
    WatchA preset re-applied on reload; a ledger that restarted; settings that fell back to defaults.
    12.2

    Frame rate with the tool on and off

    DoUnpaused at your usual speed, read the frame rate for a minute with rotation and groups on. Then turn rotation off (Ctrl+E on the Roster) and groups off (G on Live), read it again for a minute. Turn both back on.
    ExpectThe measured cost of the jobs on the test fort was about ten to twelve percent of frame rate while they run. Something in that range is the tool working as measured; more is worth a note.
    WatchBecause two loads of the same fort differ by up to fifteen percent on their own, compare within one session, and disregard the first few seconds after each switch.
