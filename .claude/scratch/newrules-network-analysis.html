What the analysis shows

Structure

New Rules is a cleaner web than either ladder. It has no mutual or cyclic predation, predators outweigh their prey (median ×3.3, against ×1.0 for both ladders), and more animals sit on an apex-to-vermin chain (51%, against 38% and 43%).

Casts

Built and scored on New Rules, casts have fewer holes than today's. On your Terrifying fort, land-ground casts with a member that has no partner drop from 16% to 6%. The holes that remain are specific creatures, listed below, and "Build one year" cannot show them.

Your walk

Every member ends up on a vermin-to-apex chain. Selection then follows connectedness (rank correlation 0.66–0.92 against 0.14–0.25 today), casts grow to 10–12 animals, and layers with no apex seat get no casts at all.

Part 2: a season's casts together

Your hypothesis: score each season's land-ground, land-flying and water casts as one web, joined by the cross-layer links New Rules allows, and almost no member is left without a partner. Confirmed for the surface. Members with no partner fall to 0–1% on every fort. Three findings qualify it:

  • It is not exactly 0. What remains is mostly giant herbivores whose few eaters were not cast that season.
  • Joining the caverns changes nothing, because cavern casts have no holes to fill: no member is unlinked and no predator is without prey. New Rules also keeps each cavern its own realm.
  • With G2 and G3 the layers are already connected inside their own casts, so the cross-layer step adds links but rescues few members.

Counting rule (corrected): a predator is an apex or meso seat, or a sentient seat whose creature is a predator by role; vermin, including vermin seated as murky-pool fish, are never predators. The tundra's 1.0% before G2 is the giant mosquito and giant dragonfly on land-flying.

Cross-layer links are the ones the explorer draws between layers: DF's diet tokens, vermin the tool feeds and mass and numbers. Hunting pairs need a shared layer. A species holding seats in two casts of the season counts once. 200 years per fort, 800 seasons.

Who is still alone, and why

  • Giant herbivores: brachiosaurus, brontosaurus, diplodocus (Terrifying), deinotherium and giant elephant (coast), woolly mammoth (tundra, arctic). Each has New Rules eaters, usually apexes: brachiosaurus 8, deinotherium 15, giant elephant 3, the tundra's woolly mammoth only the polar bear. With one apex drawn per cast, a season that does not draw one of them leaves the giant unlinked. This is a matter of which species are cast together, not of the rules.
  • Arctic ground vermin: fluffy wambler and two-legged rhino lizard are eaten only by porcupine, stoat and weasel. In about 5% of seasons each, none of those is cast and the vermin hangs on a single link to a flier.

Leaves, cross-layer species and bridges

  • Leaves whose only neighbour is in another layer are uncommon: about 2% of seasons on the Terrifying fort and 1% on the coast, mainly small ground vermin eaten only by a flier (procompsognathus, slug, tick, honey bee); none on the lake or brook; about 10% on the arctic coast (the two vermin above, giant elephant seal, giant walrus).
  • Species whose links lie mostly in another layer: the land-flying raptors (peregrine falcon in a third of calm-lake seasons, buzzard, eagle, barn owl, kestrel, harpy), the coast's freshwater fish linked into the ocean (stingray, milkfish, lungfish), the river otter, the polar bear (seated on land, feeding in the sea) and dimetrodon. They are seated in one layer but feed in another.
  • Bridges (removing one splits that season's web): the river otter in about a third of evil-tundra seasons, the alligator in 8% of Terrifying seasons, and harpy, eagle, great horned owl, cougar, polar bear, porcupine and the owl men elsewhere. The joined surface is one connected web in at least 79% of seasons on every fort (every season on the lake and the brook), and these few species hold it together.
  • Caverns: no cavern member is unlinked and no cavern predator is without prey, alone or joined, on every fort.

Do creature raws define seasons?

Almost never. Of 869 natural surface creatures, 61 carry any NO_SPRING…NO_WINTER tag, and 57 of those are NO_WINTER alone (hibernators). In the caverns it is 2 of 57. So a raws-based season filter keeps nearly the same list in spring, summer and autumn and drops a few hibernators in winter. The seasonal variety in a built year comes from the tool assigning species to each season's cast, not from DF.

Who the holes are, under New Rules

  • Correction: an earlier version of this page counted the sentient seat as a predator seat. Gorlak, plump helmet man and manera (caverns), and the platypus, hippo and beaver men (freshwater), are prey by role and eat nothing in their raws, so they are not hungry predators. Recounted by role, with vermin seated as murky-pool fish also excluded, no cavern cast has a predator without prey, on any fort, under any builder.
  • Flying sentient seats with no link inside the flying cast: animal people of insect and bat roots (fly man, roach man, mosquito man, mantis man, firefly man, moth man, bat man). They are small predators by role, and their prey (vermin) is in the other layers' casts. Joined with those casts they are fed (Part 2).
  • Giant mosquito and giant dragonfly on land-flying: your 17 Oct rule made them freshwater apexes, but they are still on the land-flying list, where New Rules gives them no link. When one takes the flying apex seat, that cast has an apex with no web.
  • Land-flying casts are not grown on links: they are filled straight from the slot table (apex, two fliers, vermin), so no builder connects them. Over half the coast's flying casts seat a predator with no prey in the cast.
  • The largest grazers (brachiosaurus, brontosaurus, diplodocus): nothing in the cast is big enough to eat them, and grazers eat nothing, so they stand alone.

Your walk algorithm, prototyped

Built as you described: for each layer and season, a non-apex seed and an apex (neither repeated within the year), a quarter of the layer's vermin dealt to each season, and a random walk from each vermin and from the seed up through its eaters to the apex. A step goes only to an eater that can still reach the apex, so no walk dead-ends. Scored exactly like the builders.

  • What it does well: every member lies on a vermin-to-apex chain, and casts with an unlinked member fall to zero on land-ground, ocean and caverns.
  • Why selection concentrates: a uniform step to "any eater that can reach the apex" passes most often through species that sit on many chains: mid-sized predators and animal people with many Mass-and-numbers links. The calm lake's rattlesnake is in 77% of its land-ground casts. This is the hub inflation you wanted to avoid. The builder today does not show it: its selection is close to independent of how connected a species is.
  • Where it cannot run: a layer with no apex seat (a river's freshwater cast) gets no cast at all, and land-flying vermin often have no route to that season's apex.
  • Ways to keep its strength without the inflation (untested): weight each step against how often the eater has already been cast, or keep the slot-table builder and add the walk's directed guarantee (every predator seated gets a prey in its cast, every prey an eater).

What this means for the port

  • New Rules goes in as it is. On structure it is the better web on every measure above, and none of these findings asks for a rule to be re-decided.
  • The rig tests check, for every cast in play: each New Rules link among the units present is written, no other link is, and the cast-hole rates match what the explorer predicts for that fort. A hole in play that the explorer did not predict is a bug in the port. A predicted hole is one of the cases listed below.
  • The four gaps the analysis raised, with your decisions:

G1Sentient seats: resolved

Not holes. The cavern and freshwater ones are prey-role creatures with no diet. The flying insect- and bat-root animal people eat vermin from the other layers' casts once the season is joined. Left as they are (your decision).

G2Giant mosquito and giant dragonfly off land-flying: approved

They have no links on land-flying under New Rules; they stay freshwater apexes.

G3Grow land-flying on New Rules, then link across layers: approved

Land-flying casts grow on New Rules like the other layers, and a final step writes the New Rules links between the season's land-ground, land-flying and water casts.

G4No directed guarantee (your tentative decision)

Supported by Part 2: with G2 and G3, every seated predator on every fort has prey once the season is joined. Today's builder keeps selection even, without the hub inflation the walk showed.

Method

  • Forts: your Terrifying broadleaf forest + temperate river; the explorer's five presets (calm temperate lake, savage tropical coast, good temperate brook, evil tundra with murky pools, savage good arctic coast); all with caverns 1–3 and your cavern picks.
  • Graphs: the explorer's own link functions (New Rules; the ladder family); the old ladder from explorer v3 (commit f6d36122). Trophic levels prey-averaged (solved as a linear system); cycles by strongly connected components; paths by breadth-first search (scipy).
  • Casts: the explorer's cast builder (growth on the ladder, or on New Rules), the old v3 builder, and the walk prototype; 200 years each, seeds as the explorer's.
  • Scripts: DwarfCron scripts/castsim/netanalysis/ (export.js, analyze.py, casts.js, README). Nothing here has run in a game yet.
