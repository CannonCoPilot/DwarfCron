    Before you open the window

    Alpha two tests v6.6, the release that answers alpha one. Most steps re-test something alpha one found; the tag on a step names it.

    Use a backup. The plugin under test is seasonal-wildlife v6.6.0. It passed 177 of its claims on the test rig with none failing (run 002027), but the rig forts are small and seven dwarves strong; yours is the real test.
    MarkUse it when
    PassWhat the step promises is what you saw.
    AnomalyAnything else: a wrong value, a key that did nothing, a clipped column, a word you did not understand, a frame-rate dip. Write what you pressed, what you saw and what you expected.
    SkippedThe fort cannot show it (no water, no cavern found, no boundary reached). Say why.

    Choose the fort

    DoUse a copy of the alpha one fort if you still have it, or embark a fort with wildlife on the surface and, ideally, a river or lake and a breached cavern.

    Turn on the frame counter

    DoIn Settings, show the frame rate. Read it unpaused for a minute at your usual speed.

    1

    First open: Overview and Panel

    The window opens on the Overview. The Panel tab is new in v6.3: every switch the tool has, and how long each job takes.

    1.1

    Open the windowre-check R5

    DoIn DFHack’s console type gui/seasonal-wildlife, or use your launcher.
    ExpectA window titled Seasonal Wildlife with nine tabs: Overview, Panel, Roster, Seasons, Food web, Live, Layers, Vermin, Ledger. The Overview shows the date, what is on the map per layer, what is in season, and the next boundary.
    WatchAll text should be plain ASCII. Alpha one saw dashes drawn as ΓÇö.
    1.2

    Read the Panel

    DoClick Panel.
    ExpectA SWITCHES list with [ON] or [off] on every row (rotation, each layer, groups, cohesion, ecology, nudge, water draws, deep layer, coupling, livestock, vermin hunting, irruptions, overlay), then a JOBS table: every, last, worst, runs, over, fuse. A frame-rate line reads the game’s rate; it is information only.
    WatchEnter on a switch row flips it. Note any switch whose description you could not follow.
    1.3

    Read the job timingsre-check R4

    DoLeave the window open a game day, then press R on the Panel.
    ExpectEach job shows its last and worst pass in milliseconds against a 50 ms budget; over counts passes above it; fuse reads intact.
    WatchRecord the worst figures. The rig saw the groups job reach 149 to 196 ms on some passes, so a nonzero over on groups is known; a tripped fuse is not.
    1.4

    All off, then all on

    DoPress X (All off) and answer the question; then A (All on).
    ExpectEach asks first. All off stops every job and restores every creature raw the tool changed; all on puts the switches back as they were.
    WatchAnything left on after All off, or a switch that came back different.
    2

    The roster rule

    Alpha one’s biggest finding: a species you blocked could still arrive. v6.3 has one rule. An active species has at least one season; the only way to keep one away is to make it inactive, and an inactive species is held on every layer.

    2.1

    Read the new columns

    DoClick Roster.
    ExpectColumns CREATURE, ROLE (predator, prey, vermin), SIZE (small, medium, large), HAB (land, fly, wbird, semi, aqua), BIOME, SEASON, STOCK, ODDS, act, WHY. The header counts active species per role and says how many special creatures are left to DF.
    WatchSpecial means DF’s own classes: demons, megabeasts, titans, forgotten beasts, night creatures and the like. They are not on the roster by design.
    2.2

    Filters

    DoCycle V (View: Current, Default, Add invasive), C (category, including habitats), B (biome), N (season).
    ExpectEach changes the rows. Default shows the roster as the embark was first found.
    WatchA filter that empties the list without saying why.
    2.3

    Make a species inactivere-check R1

    DoSelect an active species and press Enter.
    Expectact turns to -, its SEASON clears, WHY reads you and today’s date.
    WatchRemember the species; step 16.1 checks it never arrives.
    2.4

    Make it active againre-check R1

    DoPress Enter on the same row.
    Expectact turns to Y with exactly one season, chosen from its climate.
    WatchNo season, or all four.
    2.5

    Cycle seasons

    DoPress E (or Shift+Enter) on an active row several times.
    ExpectThe season steps through single seasons and pairs and never lands on none.
    WatchA row left with no season.
    2.6

    Try to remove the last season

    DoOn a species with one season, press that season’s key (S U A W).
    ExpectRefused: the last season stays: make the species inactive instead.
    WatchThe row going to no season.
    2.7

    Cycle the layerre-check R5

    DoPress Alt+L five times.
    ExpectThe title reads All layers, Land, Water, Cavern (with how many are found), Deep (magma sea), then All layers again; the rows follow the layer.
    WatchThe layer key used to be Ctrl+L, DF’s macro key. Nothing should start recording a macro.
    3

    One creature: the species detail

    The detail page is where one species’ controls live: its habitat and role, its stock and odds, its group size, and a call.

    3.1

    Open the detailre-check R2

    DoSelect a large animal and press I.
    ExpectRows for species, habitat, role, body (adult volume in cm³ and its band), embark, allowed, stock, odds, group size, seasons, eats, eaten by, on the map, history.
    WatchBands are small under 150,000 cm³, medium to 1,000,000, large above. A lion reads about 200,000 (medium), an elephant 5,000,000 (large). Until v6.4.1 every size read ten times too small.
    3.2

    Habitat and role come from the rawsre-check R2

    DoOpen the detail for an orca or giant orca, an albatross, and a dingo if your world has them.
    ExpectOrca: aquatic predator. Albatross: waterbird (flies, lives by water), not aquatic. Dingo: land predator, small.
    WatchIf one reads wrong, H cycles its habitat and R its role; the row then says you and what the raws said.
    3.3

    Set its stock

    DoPress K and enter a number.
    ExpectThe stock line reads the number you set. For a species out of season it says how many come back in season.
    WatchStock is how many remain in the region to arrive. It does not make a species more likely; that is odds.
    3.4

    Set its odds

    DoPress O and enter a number.
    ExpectThe odds line shows the weight and its share of the layer.
    WatchOn the rig, raising two species’ odds took them from about a quarter of surface waves to about three quarters.
    3.5

    Set its group size

    DoPress G and enter a number.
    ExpectThe group size line says you set it.
    WatchDF sometimes sends one more than the size you set, never two more. That is DF, not the tool.
    3.6

    Call a wave

    DoFor an active land species in season, press C.
    ExpectThe next land wave is that species. The ledger records the call.
    WatchA call that brings another species.
    4

    Stock and odds on the Roster

    The same two controls, for one row or for every row the filter shows.

    4.1

    Stock for one row

    DoSelect a row and press Ctrl+W.
    ExpectA prompt Stock for the species; the STOCK column changes.
    4.2

    Stock for the filter

    DoFilter to one category and press Ctrl+G.
    ExpectEvery row shown takes the number.
    WatchRows outside the filter changing.
    4.3

    Odds for one row

    DoPress Alt+O on a row that is in season.
    ExpectThe ODDS column shows its share of its layer.
    WatchOut of season the column shows - whatever you set; the species detail gives the share it would have in season.
    5

    Whole-roster tools

    Each of these rewrites many species, so since v6.3 each asks first.

    5.1

    Every tool asksre-check 7.6

    DoPress M (Matrix assign), O (Co-align), F (Fill to targets), Y and D (fill natural prey or predators), and answer No each time.
    ExpectA yes/no question every time, and No changes nothing.
    WatchA tool that acts without asking.
    5.2

    Matrix assign

    DoPress M and answer Yes.
    ExpectEvery active species gets one season by climate, spread across the year, and every season of every habitat group keeps at least one predator with prey it eats. WHY reads matrix, or pair where a season was added for a pair.
    WatchOne season holding more than half the roster.
    5.3

    Co-alignre-check R3

    DoPress O and answer Yes.
    ExpectPartners are aligned to within one adjacent season of each other. Seasons are only ever added, and never spread through the whole roster.
    WatchAlpha one saw co-align give most species all four seasons in one press.
    5.4

    Fill natural prey and predatorsre-check R3

    DoPress Y, then D.
    ExpectEach adds only direct partners where a pairing is missing, and the ledger says how many.
    WatchA fill that activates species with no partner.
    5.5

    Category targets

    DoPress Shift+P (prey), Shift+R (predator) or Shift+V (vermin) to set a target, then Alt+F to fill a category to a number.
    ExpectThe P / R / V line shows the targets; the header says Thin when a category is under its target.
    6

    Apply, send off, rotation

    The roster only reaches DF when it is applied, at each season boundary or by hand.

    6.1

    Dry run

    DoPress Ctrl+D.
    ExpectA list of what an apply would change, with nothing changed.
    6.2

    Apply now

    DoPress Ctrl+A.
    ExpectDF’s announcement log reads <Season> wildlife applied (N active).
    WatchIf the filter box has focus, Ctrl+A may select its text instead; the label works by mouse.
    6.3

    Send off and next

    DoWith wild animals on the surface, press Ctrl+F.
    ExpectThe groups on the map are told to leave, and DF may send the next wave.
    WatchThis replaces force-wildlife, which piled a new wave on the old one.
    6.4

    Rotation off and on

    DoPress Ctrl+E twice.
    ExpectThe header reads rotation off, then on.
    7

    Reset

    Two levels since v6.3: the roster, or everything.

    7.1

    Reset the rosterre-check R5

    DoPress Alt+R, choose The roster, answer Yes.
    ExpectThe roster returns to the embark as first found: every active species on one season (a predator may keep a second one for a pair). Stock and odds you set are kept.
    WatchReset used to be Ctrl+R, DF’s record-macro key.
    7.2

    Reset everything

    DoPress Alt+R, choose Everything, answer Yes.
    ExpectStock returns to the worldgen snapshot and every setting to its default; the roster as first found.
    WatchDo this last in a session, or on the copy.
    8

    Add an invasive species

    A species that is not native to the embark can be brought in live.

    8.1

    Add invasive

    DoPress V until View reads Add invasive, select a species, press Ctrl+X.
    ExpectThe species joins the roster for this map, and the ledger records the add.
    WatchNote whether it arrived active and with which seasons; an added species that never shows on the Roster is an anomaly.
    9

    Seasons tab

    Every active species under its level across the four seasons. New in v6.6: you can edit here.

    9.1

    Read it

    DoClick Seasons.
    ExpectColumns Spring to Winter; + arrives that season, - leaves after it, . not present.
    9.2

    Edit from the grid

    DoSelect a row and press S, U, A or W.
    ExpectThat season flips for the species, as on the Roster, and the header names the species with its new seasons.
    WatchRemoving its last season should be refused here too.
    10

    Food web

    Who eats whom, from the species model: role, habitat and body size.

    10.1

    The pyramid

    DoClick Food web.
    ExpectA season’s trophic pyramid: four unlabelled tiers of species with ^ between them (prey feeds upward), apex at the top (every medium or large predator), then small predators, prey, and vermin at the base; an aquatic chain below it, each line a predator ----> what it eats.
    WatchA tier missing on a fort that has its animals, or a pairing that makes you stop (the rig’s round saw a giant otter eating a jaguar man).
    10.2

    Diet follows habitatre-check R3

    DoLook for any land predator shown eating a sea creature, or a bird eating a large fish.
    ExpectNone. A lion takes no shark, an eagle no tuna, a great white no deer. An osprey takes small sea fish (vermin).
    WatchAlpha one saw lions eating sharks.
    10.3

    The four modes

    DoPress G to step Pyramid, Graph, By season; L for By layer; N for the season.
    ExpectGraph shows arrows and marks live pairs; By season shows four columns with the mass ratio; By layer puts layers side by side.
    11

    Live

    What is on the map this instant, and where each animal came from.

    11.1

    Origins

    DoClick Live.
    ExpectA Wild on map line, then each animal by origin: DF wave, tool-drawn, resident, born here, untracked, deep. Then the ecology, limits, cavern and fuse lines.
    WatchAn animal counted twice or in no origin.
    11.2

    Groups

    DoScroll to Groups:.
    ExpectOne row per tracked group: species, size, how it came, its day.
    11.3

    Hold and send off

    DoSelect a group; press Q (hold 30 days), then X (send off).
    ExpectThe row reads held N more days, then dismissed, and the group leaves.
    11.4

    Follow and centre

    DoPress F on a group, then Enter.
    ExpectThe camera follows a member; Enter centres the map on the group.
    11.5

    Herds and packs

    DoRead the Herds & packs block.
    ExpectEach tracked species is herd, pack, flock or school, with the reason from its raws.
    12

    Layers

    One column per layer, with the same words on every layer.

    12.1

    Read the table

    DoClick Layers.
    ExpectColumns LAND, WATER, CAVERN, DEEP; rows layer, drawn by, groups at once, ceiling, gap between waves, pattern, coupling, cohesion, ecology switch, nudge after, pack size, irruptions, cavern pressure.
    WatchWater is drawn by the tool (DF sends no river or lake waves in play); land and caverns by DF; deep by frequency.
    12.2

    Change a limit

    DoPress L to pick a column, then C (groups at once) or Q (ceiling).
    ExpectThe cell changes and the ledger records it.
    12.3

    The water line

    DoRead the line under the table that starts water:.
    ExpectGroups at once, animals against the ceiling, the water on the map (ocean, lake, river, pool; salt and fresh), and the next draw.
    WatchA fort with water that reads dormant.
    12.4

    Caverns

    DoRead the Caverns block.
    ExpectEach cavern is not yet found until your dwarves reach it, then its species.
    13

    Vermin, and vermin hunting

    Vermin by family, now editable per species. Vermin hunting is an opt-in switch new in v6.6.

    13.1

    Families

    DoClick Vermin.
    ExpectOne row per family (fish, flies, fliers, mammals, crawlers) with active count, seasons, stock and layers.
    13.2

    Open a family

    DoSelect a family and press E.
    ExpectIts species appear under it, each with active, seasons, stock and layer.
    13.3

    Edit one vermin species

    DoOn a species row press Enter, a season key, or Shift+W (stock).
    ExpectThat species alone changes.
    13.4

    Vermin hunting on

    DoOn the Panel, turn on Vermin hunting.
    ExpectPredatory and water birds get the dive-hunting flag and small land predators the hunting flag, for as long as the switch is on.
    WatchOn the rig, wild kestrels and badgers with the flags behaved no differently from those without. Tell us if yours do anything visible.
    14

    Ledger, undo, help

    Every write the tool makes is in the ledger, and the edits can be undone.

    14.1

    Filter the ledger

    DoClick Ledger; press K (kind) and L (layer).
    ExpectThe list narrows; the count line shows how many of how many.
    14.2

    Undore-check 9.2

    DoMake three roster edits, then press Z three times.
    ExpectEach edit is undone in reverse order. Undo now goes fifty edits deep.
    14.3

    Help on every tab

    DoOn each tab press Alt+H.
    ExpectA help page for that tab, with the vocabulary: active, stock, odds, group size, groups at once, ceiling, send off, call.
    WatchA word used in the window that the help does not explain.
    15

    The map overlay

    Group markers and predator-prey links drawn on the map.

    15.1

    Turn it onre-check R5

    DoOpen gui/control-panel, find the overlay seasonal-wildlife.groups, enable it.
    ExpectMarkers on tracked groups, only on the level you are viewing, and lines between related predators and prey.
    WatchAlpha one’s run left the overlay on; turn it off again at the end if you do not want it.
    16

    A season boundary

    Let the game run into the next season. This is where the roster meets DF.

    16.1

    Inactive stays awayre-check R1

    DoPlay through at least one boundary; watch for the species you made inactive in 2.3.
    ExpectIt never arrives, on any layer. On the rig’s copy of the alpha one fort, v6.4 let no inactive animal in across two test runs, where v6.2.1 let in 32 and 12.
    WatchAny inactive or out-of-season arrival: note species, layer and date.
    16.2

    The boundary itself

    DoAt the change of season, read the announcements and the Ledger.
    ExpectA <Season> wildlife roster applied announcement and a season line in the ledger.
    16.3

    Water groups

    DoIf your map has river or lake water, watch the water line and the Live tab over a few days.
    ExpectGroups drawn into water that suits them, swimming together, leaving on their countdown.
    WatchAn animal placed on dry land.
    17

    Persistence and cost

    Does everything survive a reload, and what does the tool cost while its jobs run.

    17.1

    Save, quit, reload

    DoNote an inactive species, a stock you set, and the ledger’s last line. Save, return to the title, load again, open the window.
    ExpectAll three as you left them; undo still works.
    WatchSettings back at defaults, or a ledger that restarted.
    17.2

    Frame rate on and offre-check R4

    DoUnpaused, read the frame rate for a minute with the tool on; then All off on the Panel and read it again.
    ExpectOn the test fort the jobs cost about ten to twelve percent of frame rate while they run.
    WatchCompare within one session and ignore the first seconds after a switch. Note the Panel’s worst times too.
