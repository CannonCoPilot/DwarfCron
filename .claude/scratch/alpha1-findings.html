Seasonal Wildlife · alpha trial one · digest and repair plan · rev 2

Alpha One Findings

The first alpha trial of seasonal-wildlife v6.2.1 marked 22 of 46 steps as anomalies. They trace to five root causes. Rev 2 records your decisions and resolves the open questions with evidence. It then rebuilds the plan around one principle you set: every creature, on every layer, is either active with at least one season or inactive and never appears, and every layer is steered with the same controls and the same words.

Five root causes

Unchanged from rev 1, with one sharpening to R1 that your cavern question uncovered.

R1 · critical

A species with no seasons is not managed at all, on any layer

Every pass that enforces the roster builds its list from season assignments only: the season-boundary apply, the daily out-of-season hold, the cavern frequency pass and the water placement pass. The water pass goes further and treats an unseasoned species as unrestricted. Matrix assign clears the seasons of everything blocked, and no water or cavern species on this fort has seasons. The result is 63 blocked species that DF can still draw (cavies 5,191, giant rhinos 5,203, every shark), every cavern species left open, and water placement that ignores the roster.

R2 · high

One raw flag decides a species' whole identity

Category is a single choice from one flag. A BENIGN orca is prey; a bird cannot be a predator; DF's LARGE_PREDATOR behaviour flag decides "large". "Aquatic" comes from any water-breathing flag or an all-water biome list, and the diet test never checks habitat.

R3 · high

The roster tools cascade through the whole web

Co-align and the Y/D fills write inside the loop they read, so one pass spreads a change across every partner of every partner.

R4 · high

Hot loops allocate on a 54 MB Lua heap

A throwaway table per population entry costs 1.5 s a pass, against 0–33 ms without it. Reading the configuration costs 65 ms a call. The ecology job overran on day 79 and its fuse cut it off silently.

R5 · ours

The validator could not see what the player sees

Its keys bypass DF's macro bindings, it left the overlay on, it recorded R1 as a PASS, and it passed seventeen em dashes that DF draws as ΓÇö.

Decisions taken

Your answers, and how each one changes the plan.

D1
Active / inactive; Add invasive. Adopted across the window, the guide and the ledger.
D2
An active species always has at least one season; activating one gives it exactly one. There is no "no season" state for an active species and no "all seasons" default. The only way a species never appears is inactive. The one default season comes from the species' own climate lean, balanced by the quartered matrix (D6). The player adds seasons by hand; removing the last one is refused with "make it inactive instead".

Q2

Predatory birds and vermin hunting

Both tokens exist as writable caste flags in DF 53, and vanilla barely uses them: DIVE_HUNTS_VERMIN is on the peregrine falcon alone, HUNTS_VERMIN on the cat alone. The raws hold twelve predatory fliers, eleven of them without the dive: buzzard, eagle, kea, kestrel, osprey, three owls, roc, giant cave swallow and vulture. The water-biome fliers are albatross, duck, loon, osprey and puffin.

Plan (W6): the tool writes DIVE_HUNTS_VERMIN on every predatory bird and every marine or riparian bird, and HUNTS_VERMIN on every small-band predator, with a switch on the control panel. It snapshots the flags before writing and restores them on disable and unload.

What is not known, and gets tested first (X5): whether either flag changes a wild animal's behaviour, as opposed to a pet's; vanilla only gives them to a falcon and a housecat. This is the experiment the Wilderpop programme has carried since E32b. Two caveats go in the help text either way. A raw flag belongs to the species, so a tamed eagle gets it too. And vermin in DF are not units, so the effect is on the vermin on the map, not on the food web's prey.

Q3

If not stock, what sets the odds of a species arriving?

Measured so far. When a wave comes, DF picks the species by a weight that belongs to the species, and stock plays no part: a kangaroo beat a porcupine at even stock, at five to one and at one to five (E3). Stock only decides eligibility (zero or extinct stops a species) and how many can come before the reserve runs out. It does not change wave size either (E24). In the caverns the species weight is the raw's FREQUENCY, a comparative weight on a shared budget: two species held at the same 4 : 1 ratio took 22–28% of arrivals at frequencies 100 and 25, and 0% at 4 and 1 (E36). On the surface FREQUENCY is the only candidate for E3's species weight, but it has not been measured there.

The two controls, independent, per creature (W5):

  • Stock: the reserve population in the region, shown as a number. Debited one per arrival, refunded when an animal leaves; 0 means none left to come. Editable.
  • Odds: the species' weight when its layer receives a wave, shown as a percentage share of that layer's active, in-season species so the effect of a change is visible before it is made. Written to FREQUENCY in memory, restored when the tool is off or the world unloads, never below 1 (a frequency of 0 on the last creature in a role freezes DF, addendum 54).

Settled by X3 before odds ship on the surface: the E36 design on land, with two species held at the same ratio at different absolute weights, two replicates each. If the surface ignores frequency, odds there are implemented by the arrival engine (Q5) choosing the species itself.

Q4

Did closed cavern entries really fail to stop cavern waves, or was that leakage from unmanaged species?

Both are true, about different things.

The T7 finding stands. Its decisive probe ran with the tool disabled, so no seasonal logic was running, and all 109 local cavern entries were zeroed by hand: 12,071 ticks later every entry was still zero and cavern wildlife had gone from 13 to 43. A second probe zeroed all 914 master entries across all 45 caves, and 33 units arrived. Setting one species' raw frequency to 0 stopped that species while an untouched control kept arriving (addendum 52), and E34 confirmed it both ways, 2 of 2. Cavern spawning reads the raws, not the population lists.

Your leakage hypothesis is also right, for Boatsbowed. The tool already rotates the cavern layer by frequency: a daily pass holds out-of-season or inactive cavern species at frequency 1. But that pass, like every other, lists only species that have seasons, and no cavern species on this fort has any. So every cavern species was open to DF all trial. The ledger shows one species held at frequency 1, once.

Consequence (W1): R1's fix covers the caverns through the lever that works there. Every cavern species is active with seasons or inactive, and the frequency pass enforces it. X1 measures it on a copy of Boatsbowed.

Q5

Is "stocking" a stop-gap? How do water and caverns act and feel like land?

Honestly: for caverns it is unnecessary, and for water it is the only mechanism but a crude one. DF draws cavern waves by itself, steered by frequency, so cavern stocking duplicates what DF already does and can be retired. DF never draws river or lake animals during play; it seeds them once at embark (measured 17 September). Ocean animals arrive only where the map edge is wet. So water placement is the one way to have river life at all. As built, though, it places single animals toward a count, ignores seasons for unseasoned species, has no groups, no pattern and no group size, and its animals are never tracked. That is why it felt like an override.

The redesign: one arrival engine with two drivers under the same controls (W4).

Driver A · DF draws

Land, the ocean at a wet edge, the caverns. The tool shapes DF's draw: stock (surface) or frequency (caverns) for who may come, odds for who is likely, the raw's cluster for group size, and the gate and pattern for when.

Driver B · the tool draws

Rivers, lakes, pools, and any layer DF will not serve. The tool runs the same draw itself. It waits for the same gate and pattern, picks a species by the same odds from active, in-season species, sizes the group by the same group-size rule, debits stock, and places the group together at a matching water edge. It stamps the animals as wildlife with a leave countdown, so they depart like a DF wave, and tracks them as a group.

What the player sees

One set of controls on every layer: active, seasons, stock, odds, group size, groups at once, gap, pattern, ceiling, coupling, cohesion (herd, pack, flock, school), ecology and nudge within the habitat, hold, send off, call. Which driver serves a layer shows only as a note on the control panel.

Driver B is built from pieces already proven on 53.16: headless placement that DF treats as wildlife (E30), and the leave countdown hold and dismiss already use. X2 measures a placed group against the promise: it stays together, leaves on its countdown, debits and refunds stock, lands in its own water body and salinity, and responds to coupling and cohesion.

Q6

"Quota" meant three different things

Agreed: it capped groups on land, a placement count on water, and wild units in the caverns. It is retired. Every layer gets the same two limits, meaning the same thing everywhere: groups at once (how many wild groups the layer holds) and ceiling (the most wild animals of that layer on the map at once). Each driver enforces them its own way (the gate and odds under Driver A, the tool's own draw under Driver B), and the player never has to know which.

Q7

Packs, herds, schools and coupling on every layer

Group size, cohesion, coupling, hold, send off and call all act on groups, and under the engine every layer has groups. So each applies everywhere with the same meaning. Group size uses the raw's range, or a value you set (DF draws N and sometimes N + 1, E42b; X4 settles whether that holds at one). Cohesion keeps a group behind a leader: a school for aquatic species, a flock for fliers, a pack for predators, a herd for the rest. Coupling lets a prey arrival call its predators next, on its own layer and habitat. Ecology writes DF's predator–prey relation for every wild pair within a habitat. Nudge moves a predator toward prey within its own habitat, so a shark is nudged through water, a troll through its cavern, and a lion across land.

Q8

"All creatures ever", including vermin

Adopted as the roster rule, and enforced by the validator: every species that can reach this map on any layer is on the roster, either active with one or more seasons or inactive. That covers land, water, cavern and vermin, each through the lever that works for it: stock on the surface and for vermin (E27), frequency in the caverns, the engine's own draw for rivers and lakes. Species that cannot reach the map are listed under Add invasive. Two assumptions, open to your correction. First, the magma sea's natural species (magma crab, fire imp) join the roster on a deep layer steered by frequency. Second, demons and night creatures stay locked, shown with the reason, because they are DF's world content rather than wildlife.
