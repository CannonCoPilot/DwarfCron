The Seasonal Wildlife Plugin

What the tool can be built to do now that the game's wildlife machine has been measured rather than guessed at; how each capability the brief asked for maps onto a lever that has a run number behind it; what it does and does not touch among caravans, sieges, visitors and megabeasts; and the experiments that still stand between the measurements and the code.

Every capability on the list is buildable, and all but two rest on levers already measured. The two that do not — the predation switch and the aquatic layer — were the ones the tool had written off, and both turned out to be real this week.

The engine the tool needs is smaller than the one it has. DF keeps a per-pair relation table that decides who hunts whom, honours a written residency timer, reads pack size at the draw, opens the surface within 450 ticks of the last held animal, and draws sea life through a wet map edge exactly as it draws deer through a dry one. The tool's job is to write five numbers at the right moments and to say, in its own window, what it wrote and why. What follows is the design for that, in the order it should be built, with the tests each step must pass and the experiments that must run first.

3 · Architecture

One engine, three layers, one scheduler. The layer is a key on every entry, every config value and every group, never a filter.

Land · feat −1, cave −1

Surface animals, birds, vermin. Rotated today. Gate: surface flag category.

Water · lake and ocean tiles

The water tile's own surface entries — fish, sharks, hippos, otters. Same gate as land. Dormant if the map edge is dry. River and pool feature entries stay data-only.

Caverns · cave 0, 1, 2

Three layers, three entry sets, their own gate categories. Waves spawn unrevealed. Managed after the player has seen one, or on opt-in.

Deep · innumerable

Magma sea and below. Shown, locked, never written.

Two arrows, both two-way: the engine reads and writes DF; the window reads and writes the engine's config and reads its ledger. Nothing in the window touches DF directly, which is what makes the engine testable headless on the rig.

The arming sequence, which is the heart of it

-- when a PREY group is released and ecology is on (v5.6) 1 snapshot the layer's entries; zero every entry but the prey's matched predators 2 if groups.pack > 0: write cluster_number = {pack, pack} on those predators 3 release the prey's hold → predators land within ~450 ticks 4 on the predator wave's arrival: restore entries and raws 5 nudge: if the two groups are > N tiles apart for M ticks, move the predators to walkable tiles within R of the prey (policy b, §4) 6 relate: for every predator × prey pair with a slot, rel_map[p][q].ur = rel_map[q][p].ur = PREDATOR_OR_PREY never for a unit with civ_id ≠ −1, never for two BENIGN castes 7 recompute the pairs from the map every 1,500 ticks and after every load (a load clears the cache and every slot number; E16) 8 ledger: "coupling WOMBAT → 7 DINGO (pack 7), nudged at +3,000, 14 pairs related"

4 · Decisions that are the product's, not the measurement's

Does the tool move animals?

Yes — the nudge is on by default. Without it, predator and prey on a 4×4 embark usually expire without meeting (E11). Policy (b): when a coupled pair has stayed more than N tiles apart for M ticks, the predators are moved to walkable tiles within R of the prey. Defaults N = 40, M = 3,000, R = 6, all exposed as "Nudge predators toward prey"; E18 tunes them.

Does the ecology switch default on?

Yes. The point of the tool is an ecology; a player who wants a tableau turns it off. Presets may soften it per biome (fewer couplings on a calm temperate embark, more on a savage one) but the switch itself starts on.

Whom may a predator attack?

Any wildlife prey, and any animal that is not the fort's own: a caravan's reindeer and pack animals, a visitor's mount, an invader's war beast. The relation write pairs wild predators with those units too. Fort livestock is excluded by default behind a toggle ("Predators may take livestock"), because a wolf in the pasture is a different game than a wolf at the caravan. E22 measures the caravan case first, and if it breaks something the pairing narrows.

What does "predation" mean in the window?

Exactly what was measured: a predator beside prey, related in DF's table, will attack it. The food-web tab says so and names the two writes. It never implies that DF hunts unaided across the map.

When is a cavern layer managed?

When the player has found it: any of its entries carries discovered, or a tile in it is revealed. Until then the layer is hidden entirely — not locked, hidden, so nothing about the caverns is spoiled. On the rig every test reveals all three layers and manages all three, so the code that hides them in play is exercised at full load.

What if the map has no water at its edge?

The water layer is marked dormant at load, with the reason in the window and the roster still visible. OCEAN taught this: a coastal fort can hold 28 shark entries and never draw one.

Is aquatic life something the tool merely allows, or something it is built to raise?

Built to raise, and to hold. A design principle, not a side effect (decision of 17 September, evening). DF on 53.16 keeps river life rare by structure: its wildlife entry list stops at the river banks, its river feature carries a world-level count of zero, and a year on two river forts with only river species eligible drew nothing. The tool exists to lift aquatic activity to a level the player sets and keep it there, across rivers, lakes and oceans. The measurements decide only the mechanism: register the river's edge tiles and keep the feature counts live where that opens DF's own door (E26c, E26d), and place river life from the feature entries where it does not (E30). Placed river animals breed on the map (carp on RIVER4) and fish linger long past their countdown, so a seeded population sustains itself and the tool rotates it out itself.

Vermin?

Their own panel. Vermin are pool entries too; players want to forbid mosquitoes and keep fireflies, and vermin have seasons that make sense (insects from spring to autumn, none under snow, rats all year). Rotated and curated in the Vermin tab; excluded from the ecology switch. E27 checks that a zeroed vermin entry actually stops the vermin.

Per-layer quotas?

A player setting. "About two land groups and two water groups" is max_concurrent per layer with a gap range, on the Patterns tab, with the preset supplying the first values.

5 · Work packages, in the order they should ship

Each ends with a deployable version and a rig manifest that passes with the save byte-identical afterwards. Numbering is the build order; it matters.

  1. v5.6

    Ecology switch

    Shipped 17 September 2026 (tag v5.6, commit 67a7487; STATE addendum 38). Predators are the food web's land predators that carry LARGE_PREDATOR in a caste, and while the switch is on the coupling opens only those: the first gate run drew badger packs for the groundhogs and elk birds, wrote up to 120 badger–prey pairs a pass, nudged them together, and logged not one badger attack in sixty thousand ticks, because a badger is BENIGN in the raws and the game's hunting drive lives on LARGE_PREDATOR.

    groups ecology on|off, default on (decision of 17 September). Adds steps 5–8 of the arming sequence to the coupling. Slot guard for the 500-slot table; wildlife×wildlife pairs only. Rewrite policy, measured (E16, E17): write every 1,500 ticks, not once; write again after every load (the cache and every slot number are cleared by a load); skip units whose slot is still −1 and catch them next pass; zero a departing or dead unit's row and column so the next holder of that slot starts clean. Nothing is persisted: the pairs are recomputed from who is on the map.

    Gate T4, passed on the rerun (Wilderpop §5): the tool alone on CTRL, roster applied like a first run, ecology on against off, three replicates each, sixty thousand ticks. On: a wolf pack took the wombat, a dingo the great horned owl, a dingo a badger — three of three, each kill attributed from the victim's own combat reports. Off: none by an armed predator in three, though a wolf pack arrived in one and took nothing. The off bar is a rate, not zero: an unwritten cougar killed two badgers in the first run's off arm. E18 (Wilderpop §5) then tried the far-apart threshold at 20, 40 and 80 tiles: kill latency followed the armed pack's arrival tick, not the threshold, so 40 stays the default and groups nudge remains the player's dial. E16, E17 and E22 done: the package is unblocked. E22 measured what the levers do to a caravan: with a trade depot the caravan comes inland and gathered dingoes took a pack animal in one trial of two; merchants and citizens were never touched. The Food-web tab says so. The experiments that gate each package are kept in the Wilderpop Model.

    Open, queued as E32. Mid-predator behaviour — a badger or fox that hunts only small prey and vermin. The only caste flag with a hunting drive against units is LARGE_PREDATOR; HUNTS_VERMIN, DIVE_HUNTS_VERMIN, ROOT_AROUND and GOBBLE_VERMIN act on vermin objects; none of them is in the per-unit add-caste-flag mask (the syndrome set). The route is an in-memory caste-flag flip on the creature raw, species-wide and unsaved, with the relation write choosing small prey; the hunter profession (TRAINED_HUNTER) is the one per-unit lever. Costs to measure: dwarf job cancellations, dog engagements, whether a wild unowned animal hunts vermin at all.

  2. v5.7

    Herds, packs, turnover, stuck sweep

    Shipped 17 September 2026 (tag v5.7; STATE addenda 39–43; Wilderpop E28, E19, T5). The nudge now places on floor or ramp tiles only: T5 found it had been stranding non-climbers in tree canopies since v5.6, because a branch is "walkable". A held herd is fed by the map for a season (E19), so the hold has no ceiling and turnover stays a player verb.

    groups hold <token> <days> raises the countdown on a resident group; groups dismiss <token> zeroes it and DF refunds. Cohesion: herd and pack species are labelled from the raws (§5a) and their groups are kept together by the lever E28 proved (Wilderpop §5, 17 September): a leader — the lowest-id member, the rest following it as PACK_LEADER at follow_distance 4 — re-picked when the leader dies or leaves and cleared on dismiss. Mean spread 5.0 tiles against 9.9 untouched, the pack still roaming the map. A shared station is ignored by a wild roamer and a centroid teleport stalls the pack, so neither is built. The stuck sweep re-grounds a tracked non-flier that has not moved in 5,000 ticks (the measured case is the canopy stranding; fliers perch and are left alone); fix/wildlife cannot see unflagged units, so this is ours.

    Gate: T5 — a herd held a season, dismissed, refund exact, no stuck unit at year end; a pack of seven stays within 12 tiles of its centroid for 90% of samples. Blocked by E19, E28.

  3. v5.8

    Water and cavern rotation

    Built 18 September 2026; T6 passed 2 of 2 the same day. Three pieces, each measured before it was written: headless placement (addendum 47), the water job (E33, addendum 48) and wet-edge detection (E25, addendum 44). Verbs place TOKEN [n] [layer] and water [on|off|now|target N|cadence N]. An unassigned water species counts as unrestricted rather than excluded, because the game does nothing for this layer at all and exclusion would leave a lake empty for a player who never built a roster. The last two pieces are built as well (addendum 49). DF's own invasions leave every count: a siege, an ambush or a cavern invasion puts units on the map that read as wildlife but that the tool never gated, cannot retire and holds no entry for — and since the water job stocks up to a target, a dozen invading beasts in the water could have told it the lake was full and silenced the very mechanism keeping it alive. Every count now goes through one predicate, which probes invasion_id once rather than assuming the build carries it. Per-layer quotas are a player setting — quota land|water|cavern N — and every one defaults to 0, meaning unset, changing nothing. Land and water stand. The cavern ceiling was withdrawn and must be rebuilt: it closed the tool's managed cavern entries exactly as designed and achieved nothing, because closing a cavern entry does not stop a cavern wave (T7). See The cavern layer below, which is now its own package.

    Measured, 18 September 2026. Wet-edge detection (E25, addendum 44) is a four-part test and each part was needed: deep water (flow ≥ 4) on the map's true perimeter — DF's plotinfo.map_edge is a land-entry tile list and never holds water — within about six levels of the land-entry surface, in an ocean or lake biome, with a stocked feature entry. OCEAN2 and LAKE pass; OCEAN fails on depth (its water is 39 levels down), CTRL on depth (caverns at 65–80), RIVER3 on biome (a river edge, and E26 says rivers get no waves). The whole scan is one RPC call, about a second. Cavern rotation (E21, addendum 45) already works as v5.7 ships it: zero out-of-season cavern arrivals in two replicates, and cavern and surface waves arrive in the same buckets, so the single scheduler starves neither layer — the per-layer quota is a player setting, not a fix.

    The roster tick and groups job run per layer with per-layer quota and gaps. Wet-edge detection at load; dormant layers say why. Cavern layers managed on visibility or opt-in. DF's own cavern invasions (units with invasion_id ≥ 0) excluded from every count. No water body gets waves (E26 on rivers; E20, addendum 46, a full year on a lake twice with zero water arrivals): DF seeds a feature entry once at embark and never again, and the cohort it placed leaves on its own countdown. The whole water roster is therefore placed and rotated by placement and release, never by a quantity gate — and as of 18 September there is a placement call a shipped tool can make. Headless placement (addendum 47): dfhack.units.create(race, caste) returns a live unit with a full body, in world.units.all and off the map; copying a stocked entry's six-field population reference onto it makes isWildlife true, and a leave countdown, the two cleared roaming-source flags, pos, idle_area, an append to world.units.active and the tile's occupancy bit put it on the map. No menu, no pointer, no gametype change, nothing the player sees. Measured on the lake fort: four carp and two ostriches placed, all six alive and moved after 5,155 ticks with every countdown exact, carp in flow-7 water and ostriches on dry land, and six of six still there after a save and reload. modtools/create-unit is not repairable on 53.16 — the viewscreen and interface key it drives are both gone — and the arena panel that replaced them still needs a real pointer click (E30). Measured as a mechanism (E33, addendum 48): a job that tops the map up to twelve feature-layer animals every 5,000 ticks — one at a time, round-robin, only from entries that are stocked and in season — restored the layer to exactly twelve on every one of 72 passes across two replicates of a season, while the control arm drained to a single alligator. Between passes it drifts down to seven as animals leave on their countdowns: the tool tops up, the game retires. Seven citizens throughout, no tile ever unavailable, saves byte-identical. Shipped as the water job with a water verb (on|off|now|target N|cadence N). Open: placement is round-robin but survival is not, so the species mix drifts — a stocking rule that wants a balance must weight by what is swimming, not by what it last placed. A wet edge is what makes ocean-biome surface species eligible — that is where a wet map's albatrosses and crocodiles come from — and the tool must say so rather than implying the feature layer will fill.

    Gate: T6 on LAKE and OCEAN2 (a year, per-layer checker), T7 on CTRL caverns — now designed as the cavern ceiling's own test, a season with the quota set to fifteen against no ceiling at all, two replicates each. E20, E21, E25 and E33 all done; T6 is running on the lake fort — a full year with every feature from v5.6 to v5.8 live at once. Its first run found a defect and failed on it. Four of the five criteria passed in both replicates: the water layer never fell below nine of twelve, the surface and caverns kept arriving, both forts lived, both saves were byte-identical. The fifth did not. Thirteen arrivals in one replicate and twenty in the other were out of season, every one of them the same modded carnotaurus — assigned to autumn and winter, arriving in spring and summer — and every one inside a migratory coupling window. (The failure was first read by hand as fifteen and twenty-one. Three of those were water-layer placements of our own, first seen a few hundred ticks into spring but provably placed in winter by the leave countdown they still carried; the gate is now scored by a script that dates a placement by its countdown and reports a boundary case apart from a breach.) Opening a prey's natural predators gave their entries ten stock without consulting the roster, which is a hole in the tool's oldest guarantee; the year-long season regression never caught it because that regression runs with coupling switched off. The coupling now opens only predators the season allows, and the rerun passes 2 of 2: a full year on the lake with all three layers live, the water layer never below seven of twelve and averaging 11.3 in both replicates, both forts grown from seven citizens to twelve and eleven, both saves byte-identical, and zero out-of-season arrivals in both replicates. One arrival is reported as unresolved rather than counted either way — a surface eagle first seen 2,174 ticks into spring, which the game waves itself and which therefore carries no placement countdown to date it. v5.8 is not tagged yet, on purpose: the gate ran on the build before the invasion exclusion and the quotas were written, so those two have never executed in the game. Deploy, smoke-test, T7, then tag. A long run must sample the fort's living citizens: E20's two attempts on OCEAN2 ended with the settlement crumbled to ruin — the map's own saltwater crocodiles killed all seven dwarves three seasons in — and a dead fort is indistinguishable from a stalled one in wildlife data. No key dismisses that screen.

  4. v5.8.1

    The cavern layer, on the lever that actually works

    The cavern layer has never been managed. Zeroing a population entry gates a surface wave and does nothing underground. Measured twice, with the tool switched off so nothing could re-apply: all 109 managed local entries held at zero for 12,071 ticks — the entries stayed at zero and thirty new cavern units arrived; then all 914 cave master entries, locals untouched — same, thirty-three arrived. The right lists were hit (the map's feature objects are the same objects as the underground masters, and all 115 of the map's feature population entries read zero afterwards). So the seasonal rotation, the ceiling and the whole cavern design rested on a mechanism that does not reach the caverns. E21, the experiment believed to have shown cavern rotation working, is corrected: 68 of its 70 cavern token-rows carried all four seasons, so its "zero out-of-season arrivals" was close to vacuous and never tested the gate.

    creature_raw.frequency does reach them, and reverses. E34: seven common cavern species at frequency 0 produced zero arrivals across two suppressed half-seasons while 27 and 39 other cavern animals arrived beside them; restored, they came straight back (28 and 15); an untouched control had them arriving freely in the same window (29 and 43), ruling out a timing effect. A raw edit cannot outlive the world — set frequency, cluster and layer range, unload, reload, and the originals read back — so a crash cannot leave a player's world altered.

    ⚠️ Never write frequency 0. Write 1. Zeroing CREEPY_CRAWLER on its own freezes the game silently and unrecoverably: no crash, no log entry, the game log stops growing, the process has to be killed. It is the only creature in the raws carrying VERMIN_ROTTER with any underground range. It is not a quantity effect — thirteen assorted cavern species can be zeroed with no effect at all, and the same creature set to 1 runs at full speed. A weight of 1 against a typical 50 or 100 is suppression for every practical purpose, and the rule removes the whole class of hazard without needing to know which creature is load-bearing on a given world.

    What this makes buildable, reusing v5.8's throttle machinery unchanged except for its write target — the snapshot, the restore, the season guard and the held-closed count all stand: seasonal rotation on the cavern layer (lower out-of-season cavern species to 1, restore in season); the per-layer ceiling (lower while over, restore while under); per-cavern placement via underground_layer_min/max, which is how the three layers finally separate; and wave size via cluster_number, already understood from packs. Rules, all measured: snapshot before the first write and restore on disable, unload and shutdown — not for the player's world, which reloads clean, but for other forts in the same session; re-apply at every load, since nothing persists; never write a bare zero; never write a raw for a creature the roster does not manage.

    A counting fix came with it. Half of what the tool reported as cavern wildlife was the magma sea and the underworld — magma crabs and demons at z5–21, kilometres below a fort in no danger. layer_depth 0–2 is a cavern; 3 is the magma sea and 4 the underworld. Those are now counted and shown separately, and labelled as never managed.

    Open before a balance rule: whether frequency is an independent per-species probability or a comparative weight sharing one spawn budget (E36). Gates: E21b re-runs the cavern seasonal test with a roster that can actually fail; T7b gates the ceiling rebuilt on frequency.

  7. v6.1

    Cavern pressure and irruptions

    ✅ Mechanism rebuilt and gated. Irruptions were specified to raise pressure by opening cavern population entries, which T7 showed does nothing underground — the game does not read those entries when it spawns cavern life. The lever that does reach the caverns is creature_raw.frequency (E34, E36, E21b), and T7b gated a ceiling built on it: it bit and released in both replicates, ran the layer 27% below an unceilinged control, and harmed no fort. Never write a bare zero — frequency 0 on the last creature filling a vermin role underground freezes the game (addendum 54); 1 is a 1-in-100 weight and is safe. Two constraints came with it: the ceiling brakes ARRIVALS and culls nothing, so a layer already over its ceiling takes as long to come down as DF's own leave countdowns (44 days from 28 to 9 in one replicate); and a seasonal roster must hold total in-season frequency mass roughly constant, because suppressing a large share of a layer thins it by more than proportionally (E21b). E29 and E23 have since settled the arming half — see §10. Named 18 September 2026: a managed cavern surge is an irruption, never a raid. "Raid" is DF's own invasion machinery, which this module never touches; an irruption is the ecological term for a population surging out of its usual range, which is exactly what a pressure score produces. DF's own agitated cavern arrivals keep the name invasion and stay excluded from every count. DF's own cavern invasions (agitated cavern dwellers) are governed by world settings many players turn off. This module reimplements the shape on the tool's own machinery, opt-in: a pressure score per cavern layer rises with the player's activity there (dwarves present, trees cut, fishing, mining) and decays; pressure raises that layer's coupling and pack size, and above a threshold arms an arriving pack against the fort by the lever E29 proves — DF's flags4.agitated_wilderness_creature (the flag gui/sandbox sets for a hostile disposition) or the reaction relation to citizens. 8 · Herds and packs

In vanilla play a wave arrives together and then scatters; a "pack" is a coincidence of arrival, not a behaviour. The tool can label which species should hold together and then hold them.

Holding them together — three candidate levers, one experiment

  • Shared station. Every unit carries idle_area (a coordinate, "station"), idle_area_threshold (default 3) and idle_area_type. Wild units wander under WildernessCuriousWander. Writing the group's centroid into every member's station with a threshold of 8–12 may keep the wander within a radius. Cheapest if it works.
  • A leader. unit.following (a unit pointer) and follow_distance are what pets use to trail their owners. Point every member at one leader with a distance of 4; the leader wanders, the pack trails. Reads well on screen if DF honours it for wild units.
  • Centroid nudge. Every 1,500 ticks, teleport any member more than R from the centroid to a walkable tile within R. Certain to work (E11b's gather is this), least natural.

E28 runs all three against an untouched pack and measures mean distance to centroid per sample. Herd cohesion also makes coupling sharper: a nudged pack meets a herd, not a lone animal.

9 · Vermin

Vermin are pool entries of type Vermin alongside the animals, and the same quantity lever should apply (E27 confirms). The Vermin tab groups them by family — biting insects, crawling insects, fireflies and butterflies, rodents, snails and worms, fish vermin — with allow/block per family and per species, seasonal defaults that follow the biome's freezing months, and abundance. Vermin never enter the ecology switch, the coupling, or the biomass pyramid; they stay a curated background.

10 · Cavern pressure and irruptions

Two things are true: cavern layers are the same wildlife machine as the surface (E13), and DF's own cavern raids are a separate invasion system that many players disable. The module proposed in v6.1 builds irruptions out of wildlife instead: a pressure score per layer that the player's activity raises and time decays; more and larger cavern couplings as it climbs; and, above a threshold, an arriving pack armed against the fort by the lever E29 has now measured — flags4.agitated_wilderness_creature. Because it is wildlife, everything else the tool knows applies — the roster, the seasons, the ledger — and turning the module off returns the caverns to a quiet ecology. It never touches

What E29 and E23 settle, and what they cost the specification. The flag alone is enough: holding agitated_wilderness_creature on wild land units produced 2,121 and 1,108 combat rows against zero in both controls, and cost one fort three of its seven dwarves — no relation write was needed to make wildlife attack. But it does not aim. Proximity to the dwarves' centroid was unchanged from the control, so agitated animals do not come for the fort; they become lethal to whatever they meet, and dwarves working outside meet them. An irruption built on the flag by itself is therefore an unpredictable tax on outdoor labour rather than a directed event a player can read and answer, and the module must either say so plainly or pair the flag with relation writes to citizens, which remains untested. It also decays: re-applied every 3,000 ticks the agitated share held at 89–93%, never 100%, so the module has to hold the flag as a job exactly as the ecology switch holds relations — setting it once and walking away is not an option.

The roster survives agitation, and is the intensity control. E23 pinned outdoor irritation at maximum on a savagery-72 map, where every wave DF sends is agitated by construction, and closed the land roster to its three largest entries. Not one of the 220 closed entries produced an arrival, in either replicate — the pool gates agitation as completely as it gates an ordinary wave. The control says what that is worth: with nothing closed, the same map sent eleven species including allosaurs and arthropleura, logged 3,096 combat rows against roughly 460, and took the fort from seven dwarves to one, where the closed arm lost three and none. So the player's species choices hold when the world turns hostile, and the roster is not merely flavour under an irruption: it is the difference between a scare and a dead fort. v6.1 may ship irruptions on the roster the player already sets, without a species filter of its own.
