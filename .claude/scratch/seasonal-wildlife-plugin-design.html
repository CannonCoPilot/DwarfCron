The Seasonal Wildlife Plugin

What the tool can be built to do now that the game's wildlife machine has been measured rather than guessed at; how each capability the brief asked for maps onto a lever that has a run number behind it; what it does and does not touch among caravans, sieges, visitors and megabeasts; and the experiments that still stand between the measurements and the code.

The ECO study then measured the hunting the design rests on and corrected it in three places: any predator that is not BENIGN acts on a written relation, DF itself aims non-wild units at every new arrival, and kills follow the hunting group's size. Section 0 says what shipped, where this design no longer holds, what the tool still lacks, and the thirteen decisions waiting on the user. The rest of the report is the record of the design as it was decided.

Every capability on the list is buildable, and all but two rest on levers already measured. The two that do not — the predation switch and the aquatic layer — were the ones the tool had written off, and both turned out to be real this week.

The engine the tool needs is smaller than the one it has. DF keeps a per-pair relation table that decides who hunts whom, honours a written residency timer, reads pack size at the draw, opens the surface within 450 ticks of the last held animal, and draws sea life through a wet map edge exactly as it draws deer through a dry one. The tool's job is to write five numbers at the right moments and to say, in its own window, what it wrote and why. What follows is the design for that, in the order it should be built, with the tests each step must pass and the experiments that must run first.

Where the sections below no longer hold

  • §3 Architecture. The caverns are gated by the tool (count, release, hold at 1, trim), not just steered by FREQUENCY. The deep layer's natural species stay the tool's to weight (D7), but the gate, census and irruptions never key or write it (R60). A fifth script holds the controls.
  • §4 Decisions. The nudge is now off by default and the ecology cadence is 3,000 ticks (R37, R38).
  • §6 Other arrivals. DF's own invasions, cavern ones included, are disabled in DF's code (R4, R23), so the invasion exclusion has nothing to exclude while they stay off; it stays in the tool. Irruptions v2 set the agitated flag on a cavern's non-civ animals during an event, by design.
  • §7 First run. The builder is v2.2's, by units and by pyramid, with an apex scheduler; animal people and giants are picked less often.
  • §8 Herds and packs. The labels are now the whole-list cohesion table with overrides, pods included; no adult male means no leader, and a lost leader causes a panic.
  • §9 Vermin. Predation on vermin is real foraging toward vermin on the map, not tokens alone.
  • §10 Irruptions. Irruptions v2, above, replace the v6.1 module: placed civ-race waves with tokens instead of arming whatever DF sends, per-cavern pressure instead of the maximum across caverns, DF announcements instead of ledger lines only.
  • §11 The window. The Panel renders the registry in 13 sections; the tab bar scrolls below 92 columns instead of wrapping.

3 · Architecture

One engine, three layers, one scheduler. The layer is a key on every entry, every config value and every group, never a filter.

Land · feat −1, cave −1

Surface animals, birds, vermin. Rotated today. Gate: surface flag category.

Water · lake and ocean tiles

The water tile's own surface entries — fish, sharks, hippos, otters. Same gate as land. Dormant if the map edge is dry. River and pool feature entries stay data-only.

Caverns · cave 0, 1, 2

Three layers, three entry sets, their own gate categories. Waves spawn unrevealed. Managed after the player has seen one, or on opt-in.

Deep · innumerable

Magma sea and below. Shown, locked, never written.

Two arrows, both two-way: the engine reads and writes DF; the window reads and writes the engine's config and reads its ledger. Nothing in the window touches DF directly, which is what makes the engine testable headless on the rig.

The arming sequence, which is the heart of it

-- when a PREY group is released and ecology is on (v5.6) 1 snapshot the layer's entries; zero every entry but the prey's matched predators 2 if groups.pack > 0: write cluster_number = {pack, pack} on those predators 3 release the prey's hold → predators land within ~450 ticks 4 on the predator wave's arrival: restore entries and raws 5 nudge: if the two groups are > N tiles apart for M ticks, move the predators to walkable tiles within R of the prey (policy b, §4) 6 relate: for every predator × prey pair with a slot, rel_map[p][q].ur = rel_map[q][p].ur = PREDATOR_OR_PREY never for a unit with civ_id ≠ −1, never for two BENIGN castes 7 recompute the pairs from the map every 1,500 ticks and after every load (a load clears the cache and every slot number; E16) 8 ledger: "coupling WOMBAT → 7 DINGO (pack 7), nudged at +3,000, 14 pairs related"

Rev 22: the sequence as v7.0 runs it. Step 6 relates every food-web predator that is not wholly BENIGN (v6.9), filtered by habitat reach (shark against deer is never written), and with v7.0 only where the hunting group present weighs at least 5% of the prey (pack_floor). Cavern civ races hunt and are hunted; no relation is ever written for a forgotten beast, megabeast, demon or undead unit (fb_safe). A cleared slot reads NONE (−1), not STRANGER. After each write the relation sweep clears DF's own PREDATOR_OR_PREY cells between two managed wild animals the roster does not pair. Step 5, the nudge, showed no measurable effect in the sweep and turning it off is decision 2. The layer boxes above are also out of date: water is drawn by the tool per water body, the caverns are steered by FREQUENCY, and the deep layer now holds the natural magma-sea species (D7) while demons and the rest stay DF's (D8).

4 · Decisions that are the product's, not the measurement's

Rev 23. Ruled on 1 October: the nudge is off by default and the ecology cadence is 3,000 ticks (R37, R38); the caverns are the tool's to gate (R44). Section 0.

Rev 22. These are the decisions of 17 September. Two are back with the user (the nudge default and the ecology cadence, decisions 1 and 2 in section 0). The livestock toggle shipped as v7.0's domestic, off by default; "hidden until found" became D5 (a revealed cavern counts as found); per-layer quotas became the limits, groups at once and a ceiling, with land and cavern auto by embark size. The vermin answer moved to GOBBLE_VERMIN tokens (v7.0 gobble).

Does the tool move animals?

Yes — the nudge is on by default. Without it, predator and prey on a 4×4 embark usually expire without meeting (E11). Policy (b): when a coupled pair has stayed more than N tiles apart for M ticks, the predators are moved to walkable tiles within R of the prey. Defaults N = 40, M = 3,000, R = 6, all exposed as "Nudge predators toward prey"; E18 tunes them.

Does the ecology switch default on?

Yes. The point of the tool is an ecology; a player who wants a tableau turns it off. Presets may soften it per biome (fewer couplings on a calm temperate embark, more on a savage one) but the switch itself starts on.

Whom may a predator attack?

Any wildlife prey, and any animal that is not the fort's own: a caravan's reindeer and pack animals, a visitor's mount, an invader's war beast. The relation write pairs wild predators with those units too. Fort livestock is excluded by default behind a toggle ("Predators may take livestock"), because a wolf in the pasture is a different game than a wolf at the caravan. E22 measures the caravan case first, and if it breaks something the pairing narrows.

What does "predation" mean in the window?

Exactly what was measured: a predator beside prey, related in DF's table, will attack it. The food-web tab says so and names the two writes. It never implies that DF hunts unaided across the map.

When is a cavern layer managed?

When the player has found it: any of its entries carries discovered, or a tile in it is revealed. Until then the layer is hidden entirely — not locked, hidden, so nothing about the caverns is spoiled. On the rig every test reveals all three layers and manages all three, so the code that hides them in play is exercised at full load.

What if the map has no water at its edge?

The water layer is marked dormant at load, with the reason in the window and the roster still visible. OCEAN taught this: a coastal fort can hold 28 shark entries and never draw one.

Is aquatic life something the tool merely allows, or something it is built to raise?

Built to raise, and to hold. A design principle, not a side effect (decision of 17 September, evening). DF on 53.16 keeps river life rare by structure: its wildlife entry list stops at the river banks, its river feature carries a world-level count of zero, and a year on two river forts with only river species eligible drew nothing. The tool exists to lift aquatic activity to a level the player sets and keep it there, across rivers, lakes and oceans. The measurements decide only the mechanism: register the river's edge tiles and keep the feature counts live where that opens DF's own door (E26c, E26d), and place river life from the feature entries where it does not (E30). Placed river animals breed on the map (carp on RIVER4) and fish linger long past their countdown, so a seeded population sustains itself and the tool rotates it out itself.

Vermin?

Their own panel. Vermin are pool entries too; players want to forbid mosquitoes and keep fireflies, and vermin have seasons that make sense (insects from spring to autumn, none under snow, rats all year). Rotated and curated in the Vermin tab; excluded from the ecology switch. E27 checks that a zeroed vermin entry actually stops the vermin.

Per-layer quotas?

A player setting. "About two land groups and two water groups" is max_concurrent per layer with a gap range, on the Patterns tab, with the preset supplying the first values.

    The cavern layer, on the lever that actually works

    The cavern layer has never been managed. Zeroing a population entry gates a surface wave and does nothing underground. E21, the experiment believed to have shown cavern rotation working, is corrected: 68 of its 70 cavern token-rows carried all four seasons, so its "zero out-of-season arrivals" was close to vacuous and never tested the gate.

    creature_raw.frequency does reach them, and reverses. E34: seven common cavern species at frequency 0 produced zero arrivals across two suppressed half-seasons while 27 and 39 other cavern animals arrived beside them; restored, they came straight back (28 and 15); an untouched control had them arriving freely in the same window (29 and 43), ruling out a timing effect. A raw edit cannot outlive the world — set frequency, cluster and layer range, unload, reload, and the originals read back — so a crash cannot leave a player's world altered.

    ⚠️ Never write frequency 0. Write 1. Zeroing CREEPY_CRAWLER on its own freezes the game silently and unrecoverably: no crash, no log entry, the game log stops growing, the process has to be killed. It is the only creature in the raws carrying VERMIN_ROTTER with any underground range. It is not a quantity effect — thirteen assorted cavern species can be zeroed with no effect at all, and the same creature set to 1 runs at full speed. A weight of 1 against a typical 50 or 100 is suppression for every practical purpose, and the rule removes the whole class of hazard without needing to know which creature is load-bearing on a given world.

10 · Cavern pressure and irruptions

Two things are true: cavern layers are the same wildlife machine as the surface (E13), and DF's own cavern raids are a separate invasion system that many players disable. The module proposed in v6.1 builds irruptions out of wildlife instead: a pressure score per layer that the player's activity raises and time decays; more and larger cavern couplings as it climbs; and, above a threshold, an arriving pack armed against the fort by the lever E29 has now measured — flags4.agitated_wilderness_creature. Because it is wildlife, everything else the tool knows applies — the roster, the seasons, the ledger — and turning the module off returns the caverns to a quiet ecology. It never touches plotinfo.invasions; an irruption is an angry pack.

What E29 and E23 settle, and what they cost the specification. The flag alone is enough: holding agitated_wilderness_creature on wild land units produced 2,121 and 1,108 combat rows against zero in both controls, and cost one fort three of its seven dwarves — no relation write was needed to make wildlife attack. But it does not aim. Proximity to the dwarves' centroid was unchanged from the control, so agitated animals do not come for the fort; they become lethal to whatever they meet, and dwarves working outside meet them. An irruption built on the flag by itself is therefore an unpredictable tax on outdoor labour rather than a directed event a player can read and answer, and the module must either say so plainly or pair the flag with relation writes to citizens, which remains untested. It also decays: re-applied every 3,000 ticks the agitated share held at 89–93%, never 100%, so the module has to hold the flag as a job exactly as the ecology switch holds relations — setting it once and walking away is not an option.

The roster survives agitation, and is the intensity control. E23 pinned outdoor irritation at maximum on a savagery-72 map, where every wave DF sends is agitated by construction, and closed the land roster to its three largest entries. Not one of the 220 closed entries produced an arrival, in either replicate — the pool gates agitation as completely as it gates an ordinary wave. The control says what that is worth: with nothing closed, the same map sent eleven species including allosaurs and arthropleura, logged 3,096 combat rows against roughly 460, and took the fort from seven dwarves to one, where the closed arm lost three and none. So the player's species choices hold when the world turns hostile, and the roster is not merely flavour under an irruption: it is the difference between a scare and a dead fort. v6.1 may ship irruptions on the roster the player already sets, without a species filter of its own.
