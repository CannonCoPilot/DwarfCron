The Wilderpop Model

How Dwarf Fortress stores, selects, spawns and accounts for wild animals, written against one design goal: managed ecological reality on screen, in real time, with no save and reload. Every claim is marked by how we know it. Every place the evidence still fits two mechanisms is a fork with an experiment built to kill one side of it.

Companion reports: The Seasonal Wildlife Plugin (the design, rev 23: v7.1 as built) · ECO Wildlife Study (30 Sep to 1 Oct, rev 7 with the user's review; the source of most of revs 33 and 34) · Bestiary (every creature's raws) · Alpha Four (the v7.1 walkthrough) · Alpha Three (the v7.0 walkthrough) · Seasonal Wildlife Playtest (v6.2.1) · The Wildlife Backlog (beyond v6.2) · the plugin's STATE.md (addenda to 100).

The game wants one wildlife group on the surface at a time. We want a food web. Everything in this document is about the gap between those two sentences.

Revision 34 (1 October, evening) sharpens the vocabulary and closes one question by fact rather than experiment. The user's review asked what our own words mean, and the answers are now part of the model. A wild animal reaches the map in one of four ways, and what separates them is one flag: whether DF still counts the unit as its own roaming wave. "The fort's side" is not an allegiance but every slotted unit with that flag clear. The number of groups on the map at once obeys Little's law, so a release clock, not the cap, set it near two or three on every fort we measured. Predators come to harm at thresholds, not along a slope of prey mass. "Depth" meant five different things and now means one each. Raw cavern depth is DF's layer number plus one, and cavern 1 is open to 37 species, not 2. And DF's own invasions are switched off in its code, which is why four routes never raised one. Section 0 holds it; rev 33's account follows as 0a.

Revision 33 (1 October) is the ECO study and the X series, and it changes who does the hunting. DF writes the predator relation itself, but only from units it does not count as wild (dwarves, livestock, and anything the tool has released or placed) toward each new wild arrival; two wild surface animals almost never get one (5 of 370 entries). Any attacker that is not BENIGN acts on a written relation; LARGE_PREDATOR was never the switch. Kills follow the size of the hunting group, not the mass of the prey, and a lone hunter is nearly inert unless prey is dense. Raw FREQUENCY sets each species' share of the surface and cavern draws, so the tool can weight a pool after all, by the raw rather than the stock. DF draws from all nine region tiles a map block can name. The tool's own hunting dials (cadence, nudge, pack floor, sneak bonus) showed no measurable effect. Section 0 holds all of it, with the open questions and the experiment that would close each.

Revisions 14 and 15 (17 September) are phase two, and they correct this document twice and then sharpen the correction. Predation is real, and it has a switch. E11's "they do not hunt" was distance: on a 192×192 map the closest a cougar came to a wombat in a replicate was 29 tiles. Put dingoes beside a wombat and they kill it (E11b). But put fifty predators of three species beside a handful of prey and they fight each other and kill no prey at all — until DF's own pairwise reaction cache is written to PREDATOR_OR_PREY, after which every prey animal on the map died, 19 of 19 (E11c). DF keeps the relation; it just populates it sparsely. Aquatic waves are real too. Sharks and fish carry [AQUATIC][IMMOBILE_LAND] and arrive anyway when the map has water at its edge; the first OCEAN fort drew none because that map has no surface water. OCEAN2, built wet, drew a blue shark in both replicates. Both corrections point the same way: the pool was never the constraint — the map and the relation table were. Rev 16 closes the last fork: the debit is exactly one per animal at every entry size (82 of 82 waves at 339, 354, 300 and 30), and an entry at its floor still spawns the raw's full cluster. Fifteen forks, fifteen closed.

Revision 12 (17 September) closes the layer questions. Map-feature entries never produce an arrival: on RIVER3, LAKE, OCEAN and MAGMA every river, lake and magma entry was raised to 200 with the whole surface closed and every hold released, and in eight replicates of 20,000 ticks not one unit arrived carrying a feature index, while each fort's control produced waves as usual. Water and magma life is data for fishing and worldgen, not a layer the tool can put on screen. Cavern waves arrive unrevealed: 25 and 8 cavern-sourced arrivals before the reveal on CTRL, and the discovered flag flips per entry on its first wave, not on reveal.

Revision 11 (17 September, small hours) takes the census to SPLIT and settles what the tool must manage. Seventy waves: the site's primary tile, the one holding the site centre, supplied fifty; the second site tile ten, every one entering on its own side; the two block-biome tiles ten, every one a sea bird the site tiles do not list. So the tile is not a lottery the tool can lose, provided it manages the site's tiles and the block-biome tiles together, which v5.3 does. Species choice within a tile ignores stock: kangaroo beat porcupine at even stock, at five to one, and at one to five. Stock is eligibility; the tool cannot bias a mixed pool, only exclude. And the predator coupling is now in the tool (v5.4) and ran live on CTRL: a porcupine released, the pool closed, four badgers by the next daily tick, the pool restored.

Revision 10 (16 September, late night) settles F10, and the answer is the one that changes what the tool may claim. Wild predators do not hunt wild prey. Twenty-three dingoes and wolves shared the map with kangaroos, ravens and an owl for twenty thousand ticks apiece, three replicates, and no prey animal died and no combat was logged. The coupling loop delivers co-presence, which is real and visible; it does not deliver predation. The tool's ecology is a roster on screen, and its documentation must say so.

Revision 9 (16 September, night) answers the second question. The coupling loop works. Prey arrive; their hold is released; the pool is closed to wolf, cougar and dingo; a predator wave lands 300 to 430 ticks later with the prey still on the map; the pool is restored. Five of five. Release without exclusion brought a random species (three of three); exclusion without release brought nothing (three of three). Every lever this document was written to find is now measured on one fort. What remains is whether the predators hunt (E11), what a second tile does to the tile lottery (E2, E3 on SPLIT), and the feature forts.

Revision 8 (16 September, later) closes F1 and F8. The surface gate opens when at most one held unit remains: hold exactly one and the next wave lands within 450 ticks; hold two and nothing comes. A zeroed entry is not drawn from, and neither is a flagged one; both gates are real, and with every other entry closed the one open species arrived alone, debited exactly, three of three. The exclusion lever the coupling loop is built on works. The wave latency the tool was designed around, 1,500 ticks, was the tool's own daily cadence; DF's is under 450.

Revision 7 (16 September, late) closes three forks with the second harness run, E4 with E9b and E9c on CTRL, twelve replicates, all valid. A death is never refunded; a departure always is, flag or no flag; a written countdown is obeyed to the tick. The second of those means the tool's resident sweep had been refunding animals DF had already refunded, and v5.3 removes it. The same run showed a new wave arriving while one held unit was still on the map, five times, so the hold is not "any held unit blocks"; F1 gains a third model. And the tile count is now fifteen waves, twelve from the site tile and three from its block-biome neighbour.

Revision 6 (16 September, night) carries the first result off the new harness. E9a ran three valid replicates on CTRL and read the number this document said it lived or died on: a wild animal's stay is set per unit at spawn, 19,913 to 29,918 ticks, falls one per tick, and is followed by a walk to the edge that can take days more. The coupling window is not tight; it is wide. The same run reopened the tile question the previous revision thought closed: on a fort placed wholly inside one region tile, nine surface waves carried the site's tile and two carried its north-west neighbour, the very tile revision 4 called a phantom. Both are draw sources. F2 gains a third model and the tool's "one tile" fix is on hold until E2 says which rule governs.

Revision 4 (16 September, evening) corrects a fact under half the region claims below: region1 is a one-tile fort. The tool's region detection had been reading a DFHack lookup that returns every tile shifted by minus one, so it managed a phantom neighbour for months, and the "other region" in the wombat experiment was that phantom. The harness that found it is described in the df-worldgen skill; two purpose-built forts now exist.

Revision 2 misread the founding problem. It called the one-group rule a leave step that "fails to complete", as if stuck animals were the defect. They are not. The rule is the design: while any animal from the current wave still carries its source flag, no new wave is drawn, and that flag clears only when the animal's own countdown expires and it walks off the map. Stuck animals are a nuisance case of the rule, and DFHack's fix/wildlife exists for that case. Our problem is the rule itself. Revision 3 is organised around defeating it deliberately rather than around its edge cases.

The two questions this document exists to answer

Answered · measured

Can the pool of animals a draw can select from be changed live, without a save and reload?

Yes. The draw pool is world.populations.all, the local tier. It is saved with the fort and never rebuilt from the world master on load, which is why a species added to the master alone never appeared after a reload (check 10). Writes to the local tier are honoured immediately: an entry built by hand with a valid back-reference spawned twelve badgers on the next forced wave, debiting exactly (check 10); species the seasonal roster zeroed stopped arriving and species it restored resumed, across a full year and three boundaries (S1 run 2); and DFHack's own region-pops boost does nothing but write quantity on those same entries. Two qualifications, both measured: the entry must belong to the region tile DF actually draws from (G4, section 4), and DF's own refunds will overwrite a zero unless it is re-pinned (S1 run 1).

Answered · measured

Can a predator wave be triggered while its prey are still on the map?

Yes, and it has been run end to end. The pieces were measured first: clearing the source flag on the prey brings a new wave within roughly 1,500 ticks while the prey stay (G1, G2); animals whose flag was cleared still leave on their own timer (G3, dingoes from eight to one over 29 days); and a species at zero does not arrive (S1 run 2). So the candidate procedure is: prey arrive, clear their flag, restrict the pool to their predators by zeroing everything else, wait for the wave, restore the pool. Whether it produces predators and what it does to the accounting are forks F3, F9, F10 and F11 and experiments E9b through E11.

The largest unknown was how long a prey group stays. E9a answered it: every surface arrival across three replicates carried a countdown of 19,913 to 29,918 ticks at first sighting (n = 46), it fell exactly one per tick, and the animal then took 367 to 31,138 more ticks to reach the edge. The shortest stay seen was 22,499 ticks, fifteen times the wave latency. A prey group will still be on the map when its predators arrive. E8 then showed the exclusion lever produces exactly the species left open (emus, three of three, nothing else), and E1 showed the gate can be opened by leaving a single prey animal held.

E10 ran the loop. Two kangaroos and a wombat arrived; released, pool closed to predators; eleven dingoes at +398 with all three prey present. A kakapo and a skunk; five wolves at +428. Seven ravens; a cougar at +298. An owl and a cougar; seven dingoes at +391. Three groundhogs and a porcupine; eight dingoes at +430. Five of five, restored after 3,000 ticks with the predator entries back at their originals. The mechanism is: release every prey animal (or all but one), write zero and the extinct flag on every entry but the predators', raise the predators' quantity, wait one sample, restore. It costs one wave latency of a deliberately wrong pool, under 450 ticks.

Origins: four ways a wild animal reaches the map

O1
DocumentedAll four origins are real code paths, and one flag separates them: flags2.roaming_wilderness_population_source.
Arrival (“DF brought”): DF draws a group from a regional, cavern or water population entry and walks it in at a map, wet or cavern edge; the entry is debited per unit at the draw (E15); the flag is set, and while any unit of a source keeps it, DF sends no new wave from that source (G1, G2; open at one flagged unit, E1). Resident (“released”): a DF arrival whose flag and its twin ..._not_a_map_feature the tool has cleared; nothing else changes, and DF still refunds the entry when it leaves (E4, E9b). Tool-placed: dfhack.units.create plus the population reference copied from a pool entry, a leave countdown (25,000 by default), both flags cleared at birth, an enemy-status slot allocated at once with its row and column at NONE, and the entry debited by the tool. Rig-placed (cx-eco spawn, experiments only): the same writes, but until the v7.1 harness it borrowed the first world entry of the race (or another species' entry), debited nothing, set a 200,000-tick countdown and initialised the row to STRANGER (0), not NONE. The v7.1 harness defaults to the tool's placement (NONE row, site reference, debit) and can keep the roaming flag set. All four are wildlife to DFHack's isWildlife, which reads only the population reference; only an unreleased arrival is wild to DF itself. Vocabulary from here on: arrival, resident, tool-placed, rig-placed; DF-wild (flag set) against DFHack-wild.
O2
Measured“The fort's side” is every slotted unit whose roaming flag is clear; DF writes PREDATOR_OR_PREY from those units toward each new arrival.mechanism inferred
That set is citizens, livestock and pets, caravan and visitor animals, and every resident, tool-placed or rig-placed animal: in short, every slotted unit that is not an unreleased DF arrival. It is not an allegiance. The arrival writes BENIGN_ANIMAL or DANGEROUS_ANIMAL back (R1 in 0a). The decisive contrast: RELS2 cleared the flag on every arrival and got 1,551 entries between such units; RELS2b, the same steering without the clear, got 29 between wild units (4 blocks, 2 reps each). Species tokens made no difference. On the target's side a population reference is enough: residents are still aimed at. The flag reading is a correlation; no run has set the flag back on a placed animal. WILD1 would (n = 5, region8). Underground a second, species-dependent rule lets flagged cavern natives aim at each other (R7 in 0a). The writer function in DF is not reverse-engineered: all of this is observed rel_map content.

How many groups at once: Little's law

L1
InferredThe number of groups on a layer at once is the release rate times the mean stay (L = λW), so the tool's release clock, not its cap, held every measured fort near two or three groups.
Little's law needs no assumption about the shape of arrivals or stays, only a steady state. The inputs are measured: a group stays 22,500 to 59,000 ticks from arrival to its last member's exit (E9a's countdowns of 19,913 to 29,918 ticks plus the walk off; T8g's mean about 34,500). The tool released one group per gap of 5 to 20 days, a mean of 12.5 days or 15,000 ticks. So L = W / 15,000 ≈ 1.5 to 3.9, and about 2.3 at T8g's W, whatever cap was set. That is the hidden limit behind SW3 and SW3B (cap 1 < 3 ≤ auto) and ECO2-G's counts. A desk simulation of the old clock, with DF's gate always holding a wave and stays drawn from the measured range, reproduced it: mean L 2.3 to 2.7 at caps of 5 and 7. Reaching a cap of K needs a gap near W / K, and DF's own supply can still bind: its gate sends a new wave only once at most one flagged unit of that source remains (E1), and stock and season decide what it can send. seasonal-wildlife v7.1 builds this into an adaptive clock that learns W per layer; G2r, at n = 5 on region8, tests whether counts then approach the cap.

Harm to the hunter

K1
MeasuredPrey mass alone does not harm predators. Two thresholds do: prey vastly larger than the hunter, and prey that is not BENIGN, whatever its size.pooled, 1–4 reps per block
Wolves against BENIGN deer, elk, moose and buffalo (3.5× to 25× wolf mass): deaths flat, odds ratio 1.07 per doubling of prey mass [0.68, 1.68], 11 of 831 wolves. Elephants (125×) killed 28% of wolves and 17% of hyenas (83×), but none of 20 giant hyenas (8×). Capybaras, about wolf-sized but not BENIGN, killed 5 of 5 wolves; deer made raging or not BENIGN killed 19 of 72. Wound events per strike, pooled over all wolf data, do not rise with prey mass (×1.00 per doubling [0.76, 1.32]), so “heavier prey make fights longer” does not hold in general; Part 1's ×1.63 came from the PK block alone. Read with H1 and R5 in 0a: kills follow the hunting group's size, and harm follows the prey's willingness and size advantage per blow (inferred). Never measured: injury severity, harm building up in survivors, prey mass at fixed prey speed, any hunter but wolves on a mass ladder. Wounds were never logged directly; the attack rows are wound events. PMH1 (wolves on a BENIGN mass ladder at fixed speed, the elephant as reference, non-BENIGN deer, a wound logger; n = 5) would close it.

Depth: one word, five meanings, now kept apart

Z1
DocumentedDF stores only a tile's water level, 0–7. “Column depth” exists only where our code counts stacked tiles, and the rig and the tool counted them differently.
tile_designation.flow_size is three bits, 0–7; df-structures has no column-depth field. The wiki's thresholds are per tile: 3/7 or lower suffocates aquatic creatures, 4/7 and up is swimming water, which is the tool's flow_size ≥ 4 test. The rig's cx-eco depth counts the contiguous run of tiles at 4/7 or more from the top water tile. v7.0's survey counted distinct z-levels with ocean water at 4/7 or more, not contiguous, on a quarter sample (every second x and y), so Y1's “0 of 5,374 OCEAN2 columns” and the rig's BOATS counts are not comparable denominators. v7.1 surveys full columns. Terms this page uses from rev 34: water level n/7 for a tile; column depth (levels) for stacked tiles at 4/7 or more; z below the surface; cavern layer 1–3, counted from 1 as players see them; the magma sea and the underworld, never “deep”. Y1's “one level deep” is column depth.
Z2
DocumentedRaw cavern depth is DF's layer number plus one, and cavern 1 is open to 37 species, not 2.
UNDERGROUND_DEPTH:min:max runs 0–5: 0 also above ground, 1–3 the caverns, 4 the magma sea, 5 the underworld. world_underground_region.layer_depth runs 0–4 for the same places, so raw = layer_depth + 1. A region's population draws species whose range contains its depth and whose biome is the matching SUBTERRANEAN type (chasm for land, water for pools, lava for magma); that rule is inferred from the field names and agrees with the wiki's per-level tables species for species. Alignment does not gate caverns. A parse of the 53.16 raws (67 species carry the token, none of the 200 extinct creatures) gives the eligible counts: cavern 1, 37 (29 neither vermin nor lava-bound); cavern 2, 51; cavern 3, 39; magma sea, 5; underworld, 1 plus the generated demons. Only RAT_LARGE and MOLE_DOG_NAKED are confined to cavern 1. MAGMA_CRAB is 3:5 and lava-bound; on CTRL it lived in the magma sea, not the underworld. DF's waves cannot land on the wrong layer, because placement follows the entry's cave, not the raw. Raw FREQUENCY is shared across every layer a species can live on, so a weight written for one cavern moves the others (code; size unmeasured).

What is not controllable, as far as we know

  • Where a wave enters. DF picks the edge. Under positional selection (F2) the edge picks the tile, so on a multi-tile embark the tile is a random variable we can observe but not set.
  • The exact tick. Latency from a clear to an arrival is 266 to 450 ticks (E1, E8, E10), tight enough to plan around but not a value we set.
  • Which species, directly. Exclusion plus a release is the route, and the wrong-pool window is under 450 ticks. Rev 33: within the open set, FREQUENCY now weights the pick (W1), and headless placement (P1b) is the route the shipped tool uses; the arena route below is a rig instrument. DFHack's create-unit is dead on 53.16-r1.1 (it reads a structure field that no longer exists, E14c), but direct placement is not: E30 places a unit through DF's own arena route, writes the entry's six-field reference onto it, and DF counts it as wildlife with a countdown. That is the route the river roster uses.
  • Pathing to prey. gather measured on r1.1 DF will not walk a predator across a 192-tile map to find prey, and a predator with rivals in reach fights them instead. Whether two groups meet is the map's business unless the tool moves one of them; whom they attack once met is the relation table's, and that can be written (E11c). Rev 33: DF fills that table from non-wild units toward each arrival (R1), and at natural spacing a lone hunter rarely meets prey (H2, H3); the nudge that moves predators shows no measurable effect (H7).

What breaks when a group is detached into residents

The user's instinct is right that this is the clever workaround and that it probably disturbs the accounting. Stated precisely, these are the things it could break:

  • Departure refund. fix/wildlife refunds at the moment of detach, which suggests its author believed DF would not refund an unflagged unit on departure. Our sweep refunds on departure instead. If DF also refunds unflagged departures, our sweep double-counts; if it does not and we are wrong about which flag matters, both are wrong. F9.
  • Countdown. L2 shows detached animals still leave, so the timer survives detachment. Not broken.
  • Offload. If the master is synced from the local tier, a detached-and-refunded animal may be counted twice into the world. Depends on F6 and F9 together.
  • Stuck detection. fix/wildlife ignores units without the flag, so a detached resident that gets stuck is invisible to it. Our sweep must own that case.

The write policy, rev 18 (E16, E17). The relation holds without decay while both units live; a load clears the cache and every slot number; about one unit in nine sheds its row and column soon after a write; departed units' cells persist and the next holder of the slot inherits them; slots are assigned lazily, so a unit can have none for a while after arriving; a pair made after the write is never filled by DF. Rev 33: who writes it, and who acts. RELS shows DF writes PREDATOR_OR_PREY from non-wild units (dwarves, livestock, released or placed animals) toward each new wild arrival, and almost never between two wild surface animals (R1, R2). The feature enum has ten members. Pits, deep tubes, surface portals and the from-layer features do not embark as choices; they are found, and they are either INNUMERABLE (the underworld) or carry no animal populations that any run has seen. How the forts get made

  1. Worldgen with fixed seeds through the advanced parameters screen, a pocket world with minimal history so generation takes seconds. Seeds make every fort reproducible from nothing, which is better provenance than a save file.
  2. Site selection by reading, not looking. On the site screen, walk world_data.region_map and the feature lists in Lua to find a tile matching the spec, then move the embark cursor there by key. embark-anywhere lifts the placement rules if a spec needs it.
  3. Embark, save, back up. Then record the tile map, the edge census, the biome list, and every population reference into the fort's manifest, so its properties are data rather than memory.

7 · The apparatus

Six failures from this week's runs, each mine, are the specification for the harness. They are listed in the order they would have been caught.

Arms ran in sequence on a contaminated fort

The second quantity arm ran on the first's leftovers. Every arm restores its save first; the save's checksum is recorded at start and end.

Two variables moved at once

Raising one entry and flooring the rest in one step gave two explanations. One manipulation per arm.

A fact was inferred that could have been read

Which entry a wave came from was deduced from debits. Every unit carries its source. Revision 2 had to retract a debit claim for this reason.

The instrument was prose

Sentences recovered with grep. Probes emit one record per observation: run, arm, replicate, tick, subject, metric, value.

The timer measured itself

"5,000 ticks in 32s" against DF's own 99 a second, which is 50s. Wall clock from the wall clock; tick rate from DF's counter.

Pass criteria written after the output

The prediction table goes in the manifest, committed before the run, and the runner reads it.
